## Commander identity and natural ceiling
Edea is `{2}{U}{B}{R}` 2/5 Human Warlock with **ward {2}**. Beginning of EACH combat on your turn (including extra combats) **TARGET** creature an opponent controls, gain control until EOT, UNTAP, grant haste. Whenever an opponent-owned creature you CONTROL dies, Edea triggers: return it to the battlefield under its OWNER's control, and YOU draw one. Ownership != control; not merely any sacrificed token or own creature. If original card is no longer in grave on resolution (due to Black Crystal/Nightmare Shepherd or replacement), return instruction may fail while Edea still draws. Opposing owner gets ETB on return and may benefit. The key resource is *opponent creatures as temporary sacrifice ammunition*, not own generic aristocrats. Need enemy creature target and ability to sacrifice before cleanup. Against creature-light or hexproof boards engine is weaker.
**Natural ceiling Bracket 4 — Optimized**, tags **Theft / Sacrifice / Control** (subject to preserving canonical labels if they differ; exact existing Archidekt tags are a presentation classification, not a changed artifact). Do not switch into generic Grixis recursion, aristocrats or off-commander cEDH combo for rating. Four pillars: repeatable theft, free sac and death payoffs, extra combats/commander copies, permanent retention (Thassa blink at end step or Black Crystal death-exile plus next-upkeep recovery), backed by efficient interaction.
**Timing:** Edea borrow happens BEFORE attackers on our turn, can attack borrowed creature with haste. Steal duration ends during cleanup, after own end-step triggered sac effects such as Braids/Kethek/Industrial. A sac before returning to owner produces at least Edea card draw, plus any outlet/payoffs. Dying stolen permanent RETURNS to opponent under Edea unless an alternate effect/replacement legally captures it; this can GIVE OPPONENT an ETB trigger. Edea ward costs opponents extra2 to target Edea, not blanket invulnerability.

Sustained cards: Edea each borrowed death card, Skullclamp for small bodies, Grim Haruspex other nontoken death, Braids, Disciple, Village Rites, Corrupted Conviction, Deadly Dispute, tutor plus large payoff off each borrowed sacrifice. Opponent's best creature is unreliable source in creatureless archetypes, and retaining a creature through Thassa *doesn't trigger Edea death draw*, so not all theft branches additive. Interaction: An Offer, Arcane Denial, Mana Drain, Swan Song, Fierce, Deflecting, Deadly Rollick, Chaos Warp, Toxic Deluge, Blasphemous Act, Vandalblast, plus Grave Pact edicts. Protection: ward2, Greaves, counters; opponent can pay ward2/kill Edea in response to beginning-combat trigger without stopping already-on-stack theft but prevents later death draw if Edea removed before borrowed death.
Distinct victory lanes: repeated Edea steal+Goblin Bombardment or Devil damage/Grave Pact, Mass Mutiny/Mob Rule/Insurrection mass sac/combat finish, Kethek/Industrial monster tutoring, Thassa permanent steal, Impostor Syndrome nonlegendary copies of borrowed attackers (need successful combat damage). No natural compact deterministic two-card win invented.
**Role bottlenecks:** high setup 6MV `Impostor Syndrome` needs borrow-connect; `Industrial Advancement` needs right MV/sac timing; `Captivating Crew` activation 4 mana every use; `Dominus of Fealty` high MV/one upkeep trigger; `Grim Haruspex` competing generic draw vs more combat. But cuts must preserve enough sac outlets and earlier game card flow. `Arcane Lighthouse` is NOT expendable as just another colorless utility land: it makes Edea's target legal vs hexproof/shroud.

## Oracle, legality and rules
1. **Current Edea Oracle** 2UBR, ward2, 2/5, begins COMBAT target enemy creature control until end turn, untap and haste; dying stolen while controlled returns to owner and draws. If opponent has no creatures, mandatory target doesn't exist and combat steal fails; if target is illegal on resolution, no theft. Edea's ward doesn't cover stolen creature.
2. **Death and return:** A creature you control but don't own dying causes Edea trigger; if grave replacement EXILES instead of dies, no Edea trigger; if death occurs but intervening card moved from grave before Edea resolves, return impossible but independent draw still occurs. Returning to owner retriggers owner ETBs. Counterspell killed Edea after its own combat trigger on stack doesn't eliminate that existing steal but absence of Edea before stolen creature's death prevents new draw trigger.
3. **Extra death triggers:** Multiple Edea copies / Roaming Throne named Warlock cause additional triggers when same stolen creature dies. Each can draw, but only first resolving return can move original card from grave; others still draw and cannot return it again. Roaming Throne is non-Warlock before choosing; choose WARLOCK to match Edea. Its copying does not itself change Edea's existing control duration.
4. **Thassa** on end step blink borrowed creature under YOUR control as new object: unlike Edea death return it does NOT cause dying card or draw; if opponent removes borrowed target before blink resolves Thassa effect fails. Captured body is now under your control independent of old end-of-turn theft and still owned by opponent, so FUTURE Edea sacrifice can draw. By contrast blink effects saying return under its OWNER's control would lose the stolen body.
5. **Black Crystal and Nightmare Shepherd**: Both can move opponent-owned creature CARD from grave as triggered death effects before Edea's own return, denying the return of the physical card to its owner; **Edea still draws** when the trigger resolves; possible trigger order affects card destination. Nightmare Shepherd creates controller-OWNED token copy which is no longer a "controlled but don't own" source for Edea, so can't endlessly farm Edea with it.
6. **Saw in Half** `{2}{B}` targets and destroys borrowed creature, two tokens are created under SPELL CONTROLLER (you), with half rounded-up power and toughness, copy base printed characteristics, are NOT owner-original and don't qualify as stolen for Edea's death trigger; original return+draw if Edea controlled and card reaches grave. Opponent can remove target in response causing Saw to fail without tokens; legally surviving indestructible creature won't die so tokens may not be created (specific spell wording).
7. **Impostor Syndrome** `{4}{U}{U}` triggers only on NONTOKEN controlled creature dealing COMBAT DAMAGE to a player. Borrowed attacker must actually connect. Token is NONLEGENDARY copy YOU create (you own) and cannot be treated as same opponent-owned Edea death material; useful stable board value. Six-mana and blockers make it situational.
8. **Full Throttle** `{4}{R}{R}` adds TWO COMBATS after main phase, untaps creatures that attacked at beginning each; NO added main. Edea triggers at BEGINNING of each newly created combat even if the previous attack was blocked/didn't damage player. `Karlach` produces one extra combat on first combat ATTACK, not without attacking; Edea steals on first combat before attackers and again on extra. `Combat Celebrant` must be declared an attacker and exerted for its extra; cannot retroactively exert from a token entering attacking.
9. **Mass Mutiny/Mob Rule/Insurrection** gain control until end of turn, not permanently; stolen creature may be sacrificed during second main/end step BEFORE cleanup. Mass Mutiny chooses one target each opponent; opponents might lack legal target. Insurrection steals *all* available creatures untaps/haste and can be sac for burst cards/edicts. Edea's return instructions generate genuine opponent ETBs rather than destroying their whole army permanently.
10. **Mana/stack:** `Grave Pact` needs BBB plus generic, `Full Throttle` RR, `Insurrection` RRR, and Edea costs **{2}{U}{B}{R}**, five mana; be careful not to claim four. `Phyrexian Altar` one any-colored per sac, `Ashnod's Altar` 2 COLORLESS; `Phyrexian Tower` BB per land tap-sac, may also be Swamp via Urborg. Edea trigger does not require its borrowed creature to deal damage before sacrificing, so delayed credit is direct.
11. **Conjurer's Closet** `{5}` artifact own end step exiles target creature YOU control then returns that card under YOUR control, can permanently retain borrowed creature, but redundant to Thassa and vulnerable to artifact destruction; no extra Edea death/card. **Helm of Possession** `{4}` artifact `{2},T,sac creature` takes another creature for as long as Helm remains tapped; later untap/replacement conditions dictate duration and colorless payments, not a zero-cost infinite theft outlet.
12. **Official legality:** Oct14 exact approved 100-card gate UBR Commander legal, no exceptions. Changers Conjurer's Closet, Combat Celebrant, Helm and related legal UBR/colorless cards verified from Oracle/ruling sources (sources below), but no proposed 100 modified list legally re-gated or approved, and no exception invoked.

## Finite sequencing and adversarial scenarios
**A — no target.** Turn5 Edea plus Goblin Bombardment ready, all three opponents only artifacts/planeswalkers, ZERO creatures. At beginning combat Edea theft needs creature opponent controls, no legal target so no theft, no borrowed sacrifice, no Edea card. Do not count generic creature card in hand as stolen target.
**B — positive baseline.** Edea and Bombardment on board, opponent A 6/6 no protection. Edea at beginning combat steals, untaps, gives haste. Attack with borrowed 6/6 or sacrifice it immediately. Sac it to Bombardment during combat: 1 damage to any target, Edea dying-borrowed trigger -> returns 6/6 to A and YOU draw1. Opponent A obtains any ETB of 6/6 again. If A kills Edea first with legal paid ward2, our theft trigger on stack remains but later death gives no Edea card if commander gone.
**C — choice baseline versus new Conjurer.** Edea steals A's 6/6, no damage connection (blocked). At own end step Thassa present: exile stolen creature, return under OUR control as new permanent, persists next turn. Existing `Impostor Syndrome` would yield NO copy in same blocked combat. Alternate `Conjurer's Closet` replaces Syndrome: if Thassa not drawn, Closet achieves equivalent retention for five mana when it survives. In hands where Thassa already present, second blink effect often provides no incremental theft and reduces damage-copy threat density. Needs controlled testing, not certain win.
**D — death trigger doubling.** Edea + Spark Double Edea + Roaming Throne named Warlock. Sac one opponent-owned creature using Altar. EACH Edea's death ability triggers TWICE from Throne -> FOUR instances. First one returns creature to owner and draws1, remaining three cannot return already moved card but still draw1 each if they resolve. If opponent exiles borrowed creature IN RESPONSE to sac before it dies impossible once sac is cost already paid, but graveyard exile while triggers on stack prevents return, not independent draws.
**E — Black Crystal response ordering.** Borrowed NONTOKEN creature dies with Edea and Black Crystal controlled, both own triggered abilities go on stack; choose Crystal above Edea (if legal), Crystal exiles original from opponent GY as specified and **at your next upkeep** can put it under your control **with a finality counter**; when Edea resolves it cannot find former card in GY but YOU draw1. Reverse order returns enemy creature to its owner first so Crystal can't exile the original card from opponent GY; no phantom card both returning to enemy and staying exiled.
**F — own token nonbo.** Saw in Half targets borrowed opponent body under your control, dies and Edea triggers, two nonlegendary tokens you create (YOU OWN) are half-size copies; later sacrifice tokens to Viscera Seer/Altar creates mana/scry/devil triggers but NOT Edea draw for tokens you own. Nightmare Shepherd similarly leaves you an owned token even if original card is exiled under Shepherd.
**G — extra combats.** With Edea, a valid opponent creature A, and sorcery main `4RR` Full Throttle, we can steal at beginning first combat, second and third (subject to available legal targets and Edea survival); if own sac outlet remains we can sacrifice each before end-of-turn to generate three separate Edea death draws, even if none attacks successfully. Combat Celebrant challenger would require it ready to attack/exert, only one extra and it may die to blocker. An absent target at any combat yields no steal.
**H — source-paid five mana.** Lands High Market, Phyrexian Tower, Ancient Tomb, Island, Island and Sol Ring can produce plenty raw mana but have NO RED source. Edea `2UBR` not castable until R and B sources exist (Phyrexian Tower's BB only if another creature controlled to sacrifice, otherwise colorless). Comparing another generic six-drop instead of Talisman confuses abstract mana with actual colored pips.
**I — opponent removal and attrition.** Opponent controls hexproof creature, Edea trigger cannot target it; Arcane Lighthouse ability `1,T` before beginning-combat selection removes hexproof/shroud for turn and makes target legal. Opponent removes Arcane Lighthouse or Edea in response can interrupt sequence; paying ward2 may allow commander destruction. Grave Pact when sacrifice stolen creature forces opposing creature sacrifice but opp might sacrifice fodder; their returned borrowed threat may re-enter after edict depending ordering. No fabricated automatic permanent 6/6 steal.

**Empirical limits:** All A–I are manual finite rules traces, not measured tabletop games, exact Scryfall full-list refresh, randomized goldfish or quantified win rates. M5 projections (114/12) do not model current opponent creature availability or return-to-owner opponent ETB properly. A controlled future paired experiment should compare `Conjurer's Closet → Impostor Syndrome`, `Conjurer's Closet → Industrial Advancement`, `Combat Celebrant → Grim Haruspex` at three opposing creature densities (zero/medium/high), 35 actual lands with UBR payment, removal/ward payment, damage blockers, number of creatures permanently retained, returned enemy ETB benefits, own Edea draws and sacrificial damage per turn. Do not automatically apply.

## Conclusions and unresolved evidence
**Author verdict: KEEP approved locked list, no substitutions.** `Conjurer's Closet` is the most credible alternate retention engine, **not necessarily a real gain** because Thassa already supplies same function and late-game engine saturation may be more costly than lost Impostor token-copy win path. `Combat Celebrant` adds cheaper access to another Edea beginning-combat trigger but sacrificing Grim Haruspex's steady nontoken draws is a major tradeoff. Both are genuine evidence-required challenger tests; neither justifies silent list mutation. Opponent-board dependency and potential helpful ETB on Edea's return are central ceiling limits.
**Strongest counterargument:** Thassa redundancy is precisely what a five-mana commander needs: Conjurer's Closet may provide a *second independent way* to convert temporary theft to permanent board advantage even when a borrowed creature is blocked, making a 6MV damage-trigger enchantment less reliable. This is credible but currently unmeasured. More copies cannot replace target density when table has no creatures.
**Unresolved verification:** no current new 94-name full Oracle sweep, no measured source-paid table games or hard comparative win-rate, no independent GrokBot Pass C. Previously approved official Commander legality PASS and exceptions ZERO sourced to October14, no new unexpected legality problem seen.
