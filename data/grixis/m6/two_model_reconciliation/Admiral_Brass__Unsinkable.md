## Actual list, identity and hard rules

The retrospectively approved file was fetched from live canonical main and independently parsed again here: **100 total cards, 97 unique names, commander 1 + library 99; only 2x Island, 2x Mountain, 2x Swamp repeat.** It includes Merchant Raiders, Power Conduit, Roaming Throne, Skeleton Crew, Brass's Tunnel-Grinder, Mary Read and Anne Bonny, and The Indomitable; does **not** include Dire Fleet Ravager. Prior October 16 whole-list Oracle/identity/legality gate reports PASS and no authorized exception needed. This reconciliation spot-checked challenger Oracle, legalities and the specific baseline engine, not all 97 unique official card records anew.

Admiral Brass ({2}{U}{B}{R}) mills four on ETB, then on the **beginning of each combat on your turn**, returns **one targeted Pirate creature card from your graveyard** as base 4/4, finality counter and haste. The card must already be in the graveyard when the ability is put on the stack and targets chosen. Returned Pirates retain their abilities and subtypes. Finality means any would-be battlefield-to-graveyard movement sends that permanent to exile instead; it does NOT prevent bounce to hand, direct exile or other non-grave zone changes. **Power Conduit** removes a finality counter as part of its activation COST, using tap once without untapping, and thereby enables a later death/reanimation. No automatic repeated recursion, no pretend activated-mana ability. **Roaming Throne naming Pirate** doubles both Brass beginning-combat and other Pirate ETB triggers; simultaneous copies must obey independent targeting.

Preserve commander identity: graveyard-setup by Entomb/Buried Alive/Brass self-mill/Mary Read, hasty Pirate ETB/attack advantages and repeated combat/steal via Port Razer, Coercive Recruiter, Admiral Beckett Brass, Malcolm, and direct Pirate board control. Not another arbitrary Grixis creature value shell. Provisional Bracket 4 stays unchanged.

### Card facts (live Oracle / legality)

- **Dire Fleet Ravager**: {3}{B}{B}, **Orc Pirate Wizard**, 4/4 **menace, deathtouch**; when it enters the battlefield, **each player loses one-third of their current life, rounded up**. Black identity; Commander legal. Brass bypasses the printed mana cost if reanimating and gives haste/finality; Ravager stays 4/4; ETB triggers even if no damage dealt and does not target. With player at 40, 14 life lost -> 26; 13 life -> 5 lost -> 8. A repeated ETB from the same permanent requires a new entry or a separately resolving trigger copy; just additional attack/combat steps do **not** repeat the old ETB. Multiple ETB triggers resolve successively and recompute current life each time. Life loss is **not damage**, so Angrath's Marauders does **not** multiply this life loss.
- **Merchant Raiders**: {3}{U}, Human Pirate 2/4, Commander legal blue identity. "**Whenever this creature or another Pirate you control enters, tap up to one target creature. That creature doesn't untap during its controller's untap step for as long as you control this creature.**" Brass returns it as base 4/4 haste/finality, and its **own entry triggers** to lock/tap an opposing blocker, which the GrokBot 'low-impact ETB' description undervalues. Every subsequent Pirates' entry triggers it again, including new Brass combat reanimations. It can choose a creature that's already tapped to keep it locked; the target creature need not be attacking, and the tapped status persists while Raiders stays controlled. Its effects end when you cease controlling Raiders, and future tap/untap effects can override some assumptions; it does not itself freeze every creature permanently.
- **Roaming Throne:** With Pirate chosen and Raiders on battlefield, each Raiders trigger happens twice (two targeted blockers may be tapped per Pirate ETB); when Ravager enters, its symmetrical life-loss trigger doubles (first 40->26, next 26->17 for each 40-life player). Both are genuine on-deck, high-leverage but different roles. Do not describe two Ravager triggers as 2/3 of the original life by simple addition.
- **Power Conduit:** removes finality counter on either Pirate for repeatability if later dies. It costs the tap and doesn't automatically return any Pirate from grave; Ravager's mass ETB can be replayed after successfully resetting and re-reanimating. Raiders also benefits from repeated entering, but can hold multiple creature tap-lock effects while it remains in play.

### Commander-first strategic comparison

**Why Ravager could improve:** A single Brass combat can produce a large immediate *noncombat* opponent life loss even through blockers or pillowfort; menace/deathtouch 4/4 supplies profitable combat pressure. Asymmetric *life totals* can mean favorable absolute damage races if opponents have more life remaining, and a copied ETB can greatly accelerate elimination if your life total is sufficiently healthy. Entomb and Buried Alive can select it for a threat that has an immediate effect. Five printed MV is largely bypassed when Brass returns it, but costly {B}{B} if naturally drawn without grave preparation.

**Why removing Raiders is costly:** Raiders taps a blocker at its own ETB and protects a valuable attack lane **while it survives**; Pirate ETBs from Brass each subsequent combat create another tap-lock. This directly advances Port Razer's damage connection/extra combat, Admiral Beckett Brass's three-Pirate theft condition, Zara/other attacking Pirate connections and general damage. Roaming Throne gives double blocker-lock triggers. This repeatable *opponent board denial* is not replaceable by one symmetrical life-loss ETB. At four printed mana it is easier to hard-cast than Ravager's five with BB, and allows early setup independent of brass reanimation (though Raiders printed stats are lower).

**Actual downside of Ravager:** Self life loss at low life can be dangerous in a list also spending life with Reanimate, Black Market Connections, Ancient Tomb, Mana Confluence and Toxic Deluge. A trigger can cause a player with one life to lose one and leave the game; low-life players are most vulnerable, **including its controller**. It does not clear blockers, graveyard hate, Rule of Law, Rest in Peace, Grafdigger's Cage or a removal spell aimed at Brass. A full-40 table loses 14 each; while the 3 opponents jointly lose 42, you still lose 14, and this aggregate loss is not Abaddon's threshold because the commander here is Brass. It does not directly create mana/draw/treasure. Some of its impact may be achieved by tapping blockers and connecting with evasive Pirates instead.

**Alternative cuts checked:** `Fathom Fleet Captain` is a plausible *competing cut* if Ravager is desirable (menace Pirate but attack requires another nontoken Pirate and {2} for token production; lower-impact Brass reanimation immediate ETB), but cutting the cheap two-drop diminishes early Pirate board density and repeatable Pirate token setup. `Dire Fleet Daredevil` can be worse when opponents' graveyards lack castable targets but provides an important on-identity targeted spell-grab ETB and lower natural casting cost. `Admiral Beckett Brass` lacks immediate ETB effect but rewards exactly the three-Pirate combat plan; cutting it would erase a distinct theft payoff. `Merchant Raiders` itself may be the proper cut **only** for tables where blocker denial is irrelevant and life pressure demonstrably converts more games. Preserve Power Conduit, Entomb, Mary Read, Tunnel-Grinder, Port Razer, Angrath's Marauders and cheap interaction.

### Finite illustrative counterfactuals (rules arithmetic, not game-win simulations)

1. **Brass first combat, one target in grave, three blockers available:** reanimate Raiders, trigger taps one critical opponent blocker and keeps it tapped while Raiders controlled, enabling a hasty Pirate or already-present team to connect. Reanimate Ravager instead, every opponent at 40 falls to 26 (controller also), still leaves blockers unaddressed; its 4/4 menacing body attacks if legal but two blockers can block it and deal damage. Both require legal graveyard target before start-combat.
2. **Brass/Throne(Pirate) with two eligible graveyard targets:** Reanimate Raiders plus a second Pirate; Raiders enters and taps one (twice if doubled by Throne), then second Pirate entry taps one (twice), up to four legal target taps/locks, subject to targets and resolution. Relative sequencing of triggers can differ, so do not assume all four work if Raiders is removed at a priority window. Instead return Ravager, a copy of its life-loss trigger makes player at 40 lose 14 then 9 => 17 remaining; but your controller also reaches 17 if started 40. A second Brass target entering doesn't retrigger Ravager's ETB. Opponents can respond to triggered abilities/removal; removal of Ravager after ETB does not by itself counter already-queued ETB triggers.
3. **Low-life squeeze:** controller at 13 with opponents 40: Ravager makes controller lose 5 to 8 while each opponent 40->26. A subsequent copied effect via Throne makes controller 8->5 while each 26->17. This may be worthwhile if lethal is available, but otherwise severely endangers a commander engine relying on mana/life payments.

### Test gate and resolution

A targeted effect-aware multiplayer replay is the most decision-relevant next evidence, or manual matched-turn traces if the simulator does not model tap locks: hold constant **opening hands, precombat grave contents, blockers, available mana, life totals, Roaming Throne and priority**, and compare:
1. turn when Brass first casts and first eligible Pirate reanimates;
2. damage actually connected, extra combats generated via Port Razer, and Admiral Beckett Brass theft events;
3. turns and life lost to Ravager **including controller** under high-life vs low-life cases;
4. how long Raiders survives, how many persistent blockers it locks and removal responses;
5. mana/cast curve and graveyard draw risk from natural Ravager in hand;
6. whether **Fathom Fleet Captain** or **Dire Fleet Daredevil** is a less harmful cut than Raiders.

Existing M5 proxy passes/EDHREC frequencies do not model these effects; do not invent win rates. **Current evidence supports retaining Merchant Raiders pending targeted tests; it does not rule out Ravager as a strong context-dependent inclusion.** No shift in power bracket justified.
