## Correct commander mechanics
Don Andres {1}{U}{B}{R}, 4/3: each **creature you control but do not own** gains +2/+2, menace, deathtouch and Pirate type. Whenever you **cast a noncreature spell you don't own**, Don creates **two tapped Treasure tokens**. This is a cast trigger (can resolve even if the spell gets countered), but Treasures are created tapped and not spendable that turn without a separate untap effect. Casting one's own spell from exile/grave or casting someone else's creature does NOT trigger Treasure. Casting an opponent's card can count even if card is currently in exile; ownership never changes due to exile. Spell copies you create are your own and not cast.

## Challenger and cut: real Oracle and effect comparison

**Mnemonic Betrayal {1}{U}{B}**, Commander-legal U/B sorcery. On resolution: exiles all cards from ALL OPPONENT graveyards, permits **CASTING spells (not playing lands)** from the exiled group this turn using mana of any type to pay their colors; **no free cost**, no unlimited casts. At next end step, exiled uncast cards return to owner's graveyard; Mnemonic Betrayal itself exiles (not another immediate recast from own grave). Normal timing restrictions and additional costs still apply; no guaranteed viable spells if graveyards empty or mostly lands/uncastable/insufficient mana; some desired targets leave graveyard earlier to hate. Returned spells don't become owned by us. If you cast stolen noncreature spell, Don creates TWO tapped Treasures, but those cannot fund **another Betrayal-cast spell this turn** in the ordinary sequence; GrokBot's claim that they fund the next cast in the current chain is incorrect. They untap on next untap step, AFTER one-turn Betrayal permission has expired. With a special separate ability untapping Treasures, assess again; none is assumed. Without Don, Betrayal can still cast opponent spells, but remains graveyard-dependent and sorcery-speed.

**Siphon Insight {U}{B}**, Commander-legal U/B instant: choose an opponent, inspect top 2, exile one face down, other to bottom; you may **PLAY** the exiled card (including a land subject to land-drop/timing rules) **for as long as it remains exiled**, paying its normal cost with flexible color; **flashback {1}{U}{B}** repeats this once later. The opponent owns cards exiled. If you later cast a stolen NONCREATURE card from the exile, Don creates TWO tapped Treasures. A stolen CREATURE still gains +2/+2/menace/deathtouch/Pirate after resolving as a permanent (even though no Don Treasure trigger). A stolen LAND can be played but won't trigger either cast condition. Any card put into a new zone loses the permission of Siphon Insight's original exile object. Siphon itself is our own instant, so casting it grants no Don Treasure; later stolen noncreature does.

### Practical finite cases

1. **Early setup (no opposing graveyard):** Don or two-mana engines on field, opponents' graveyards mostly empty. Mnemonic Betrayal at 3 mana exiles few/no spells, often zero playable effects. Siphon Insight at 2 instant gives one opponent-owned library card with indefinitely available access, and possible second with 3-mana flashback later. This is a resilient low-curve theft enabler for Don.
2. **Three loaded graveyards late-game:** Mnemonic at 3 mana exiles several usable noncreature spells; player must still PAY every spell from remaining untapped land/rocks/previously available mana. Don triggers produce tapped Treasures, creating strong **NEXT-turn** benefit but not an immediate feedback loop; on following turn stolen cards are no longer available under Betrayal unless another effect independently grants access. This is a genuine explosive ceiling if initial mana is abundant. Graveyards of multiple opponents make Betrayal higher upside than two Siphon cards, but the burden is metagame/timing dependent.
3. **Discarding/fixing:** A stolen spell copied by an effect you control creates a copy **you own** and not a cast; copies alone cannot double Don triggers. With Roaming Throne choosing Pirate on Don battlefield, Don's triggered Treasure ability can double to FOUR tapped treasures per eligible noncreature spell cast, still not usable immediately. If stolen creature is cast instead, no tapped Treasures, but it receives the creature boost while controlled.
4. **Stax / hate:** Rest in Peace can prevent graveyard accumulation, weakening Betrayal; Siphon draws from opponent libraries, bypassing graveyard hate. Rule of Law restricts spells after Betrayal, often making broad batch permission useless after initial one-per-turn sorcery; Siphon may be cast ahead of a later stolen card as Rule of Law permits. Opponents can interact after Betrayal resolves before cast spells; they cannot cast grave cards the Betrayal controller owns simply because those cards changed zones.

### Commander-first merits, competing cuts
The deck's natural identity is persistent borrowed permanents and spells, upgraded opponent-owned Pirate combat plus tapped Treasure *banked for following turns*. Siphon is one of few cheap, instant-speed opponent-library sources with flashback and indefinite permission and therefore belongs to essential **on-ramp**. Mnemonic is a **higher-variance sorcery-speed graveyard-burst payoff** that rewards a developed table; it can be exceptionally good against spell-heavy graveyard decks but is bad against grave hate, creatures, mana-empty states and Rule of Law. More theft effects are not automatically better if they delay Don's early triggers.

**Alternative potential cut:** high-cost `Brainstealer Dragon` or `Stolen Strategy` competes in high-end theft access but also provides recurring theft from opponents' libraries independent of graveyards; removing it for one-off Mnemonic lowers persistence. `Thieving Amalgam` creates repeat opponent-owned bodies each opponent upkeep; removing it would weaken the OTHER half of Don's commander identity. `Kindred Discovery`, `Roaming Throne`, `Wand of Wonder`, early mana and protection are defended. No alternate proven cut is established.

**Disposition: REJECT EXACT `+Mnemonic Betrayal/-Siphon Insight` — KEEP baseline Siphon Insight.** Mnemonic itself remains **NEEDS TESTING / METAGAME-DEPENDENT** against a suitably high-cost library theft slot, with tangible mana/curve and late graveyard density evidence. This is not a general rejection of the legal card. 
**Bracket:** retain Bracket 4 — Optimized. No changes to locks, approved SHA, Pass C tracker, official independence count (0/62), Phase B or M6 certificates. If an owner-approved pair later emerges, it requires freshly verified 100-card source and approval receipt.
