## Distinct theft/sacrifice engine and trigger conditions

`Edea, Possessed Sorceress` {2}{U}{B}{R}, 2/5 with **ward {2}**. Beginning of EACH COMBAT on YOUR turn, target creature controlled by opponent: gain control until EOT, UNTAP it and give HASTE. Every added combat from `Full Throttle`, `Aggravated Assault` etc creates another chance to borrow. When an opponent-owned creature you currently control DIES, Edea's trigger instructs it **RETURN to the battlefield under its OWNER's control** and YOU DRAW 1 (subject to ability resolving/zone). Thus ordinarily sacrificing enemy permanent to `Viscera Seer`, `Ashnod's Altar`, `Phyrexian Altar`, `Goblin Bombardment`, `High Market`, `Industrial Advancement` extracts value/one draw but may return enemy ETB and future blocker; important tradeoff. Edea doesn't trigger from own creatures or opponent creature you never controlled. If another controlled replacement/trigger such as `Chaos Shrine's Black Crystal` EXILES the borrowed card before Edea's resolving return, the original object may be absent to return while Edea DRAW still resolves, and Crystal can reanimate card under your control later subject to its independent conditions and finality counter; precise stack ordering matters. Do not assume sacrifice inherently permanent theft.

**Existing ways to convert temporary theft to LASTING control:** `Thassa, Deep-Dwelling` at own end step exiles creature YOU CONTROL then returns under YOUR CONTROL as NEW object. Its new continuous-control timestamp isn't subject to original “until end turn” theft effect, making borrowed opponent card persist. `Impostor Syndrome` 6MV triggers on **borrowed controlled creature dealing combat damage to a player** (source specific copy tokens/conditions) and may copy value without returning original; requires a successful hit, unlike Thassa. `Nightmare Shepherd` death-related replacement can create a TOKEN copy when non-token creature dies under controller, with card exiled rather than returned, but actual eligible triggers/replacement ordering and source ownership matter. `Industrial Advancement` sacrifices controlled creature at own end step to tutor next higher MV creature onto battlefield, while Edea gains draw, native conversion even if opponent creature returns; no reason to delete it for another purely generic blink. `Mass Mutiny`, `Mob Rule`, `Insurrection`, `Captivating Crew`, `Dominus of Fealty` supply more targets; `Grave Pact`, `Mayhem Devil`, `Grim Haruspex`, `Blood Artist` convert attrition.

## Main challenger: +Conjurer's Closet / -Impostor Syndrome

`Conjurer's Closet` {5} artifact triggers at YOUR END STEP, exiles a target creature you control and returns it under YOUR CONTROL. Stealing opponent A's 6/6 via Edea first combat, then reaching end step while you still control it allows Closet to blink and retain it across turns **even if it was BLOCKED** and failed to deal combat damage. This is a genuinely stronger rescue condition than Impostor Syndrome's hit-gated value. Cost: 5 mana (vs Impostor six), no creature itself, still artifact vulnerable to artifact removals, must survive and see temporary body still controlled at end step; blocked/removed before end step means no object to blink. **Redundancy:** approved 100 already includes `Thassa, Deep-Dwelling` with same end-step exile-return retention and different vulnerability profile (indestructible enchantment creature often not a creature with low devotion), making Closet the SECOND enabler for this one role. `Impostor Syndrome` uniquely turns successful stolen-creature combat into temporary/ongoing COPIES and can generate a wider payoff than one retained target; replacing could make deck more consistent but less explosive. In fast pod there might not be five-mana setup before Edea's own five-MV deployment; no paid timeline test establishes superiority.

**Alternative cuts investigated:** `Industrial Advancement` is an expensive enchantment and another end-step effect, but transforms borrowed enemy into your own higher-MV creature while drawing, so cutting it sacrifices distinct conversion even when original returns. `Grim Haruspex` provides draw on other nontoken own-creature deaths and is a more generally functioning draw engine than Closet; `Combat Celebrant` creates extra Edea beginning-of-combat trigger at 3MV if able to attack and exert, but cutting Haruspex loses card flow and Celebrant requires survival/attack, often blocked. `Helm of Possession` 4MV artifact needs paid {2},tap and creature sacrifice and grants control only AS LONG AS it stays tapped; untapping/removing it loses control and consumes sac outlet fodder. `Marchesa, the Black Rose` theft/return-on-death assumptions can conflict with Edea returning object to owner before delayed return effect; cutting genuine Thassa/Nightmare is riskier. The deck already has many cheap/free sacrifice outlets and multiple temporary theft spells, so adding another at cost of colored fixing or board protection may be redundant.

## Adversarial source-paid sequences

A. Edea steals one enemy creature at beginning combat; sacrifice it to Bombardment BEFORE it deals combat damage -> Edea draw trigger and owner's return if still in grave. If creature has opposing ETB trigger, it benefits OPPONENT on return, not you; draw/temporary damage is genuine but opponent receives body. B. Borrowed creature BLOCKED, survives to end step: Thassa or Closet blinks under your control if still legal, retaining beyond expiry; Impostor hit-dependent copy line fails. C. Edea and Black Crystal both see eligible grave event; stack ordering to exile card first can stop original from being returned while retaining Edea draw; graveyard exile response can likewise leave no returned original. D. Multiple beginning combats from one source create multiple Edea control triggers, but the same target creature may have changed controllers and no longer qualify as “opponent controls,” so new legal targets matter. E. Opponents without creatures or holding instant exile removals can blank all theft, showing dependence on opponent boards; another five/six-mana value artifact is unhelpful then.
