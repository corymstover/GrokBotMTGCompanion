## Commander-specific game plan and natural power ceiling

The distinctive engine has **two intentionally different pilfering payoffs**: (A) turn opponents' *actual card-owned creatures* under your control into disproportionately threatening Pirate attackers, and (B) cast opponents' *actual owned noncreature cards* to create two tapped Treasures per cast. The first rewards stealing permanents, reanimating foreign creature cards, and manifesting cards from another player's deck under your control; the second rewards casts from opponents' exile/graveyard/hand by permission. Both require **card ownership**, not merely creating copies of their cards. The Pirates are not just tribal decoration: `Admiral Beckett Brass`, `Malcolm, Keen-Eyed Navigator`, `Breeches`, `Kindred Discovery`, `Coercive Recruiter`, `Ramirez DePietro`, and `Roaming Throne` connect the acquired creatures and attack engine to more value and theft. The most coherent working ceiling is **Bracket 4 optimized**: substantial multiplayer resource and damage upside, but opponent-card dependence and colored mana needs limit reliability versus deterministic Bracket 5 cEDH decks.

**Ways to acquire cards:** `Gilded Drake` exchanges *control* of creatures without transferring ownership, so their gained +2/menace/deathtouch comes from the original opponent-owned creature, not the Drake now controlled by that opponent. `Hostage Taker` and `Hurl Through Hell` exile an opponent's creature card and allow you to **cast** it; if it resolves under your control, Don's *creature* effect applies, but casting a creature spell **does not** create his two Treasures. `Animate Dead`/`Reanimate` targeting an opponent-owned creature card in graveyard can likewise create a strong stolen combat body. `Coercive Recruiter` temporarily steals a creature when it or another Pirate enters; changing control does **not** trigger additional enters-the-battlefield abilities on the stolen creature, even though it now becomes a Pirate.

**Ways to cast opponents' spells:** `Praetor's Grasp`, `Outrageous Robbery`, `Gonti, Night Minister`, `Laughing Jasper Flint`, `Stolen Strategy`, `Plargg and Nassari`, `Thief of Sanity`, `Etali, Primal Storm`, `You Find Some Prisoners`, `Decadent Dragon // Expensive Taste`, `Arcane Heist` and `Wand of Wonder` offer varied card permissions and pressure. Every **cast** of a real opponent-owned **noncreature** card while Don survives creates two **tapped** Treasures; putting a card directly onto the battlefield or simply exiling/revealing a card does not. An opponent-owned creature cast from exile gets no Treasure, though its eventual battlefield body gets the Pirate combat bonus. Cards with timing restrictions, opposing hand/deck composition and land exiles reduce consistency. `Arcane Heist` can cast the original opponent-owned instant/sorcery from their graveyard during resolution, which qualifies; a **cipher-created copy of Arcane Heist is your owned spell copy** and by itself does not trigger Don. It can still resolve to cast another opponent-owned original spell and thereby trigger Don separately. This separation is a major place to avoid a fictitious Treasure engine.

**Nonowned creature pipeline:** `Thieving Amalgam` costs seven (`5BB`) but at **each opponent's upkeep** manifests that opponent's library top card under **your control**, as their owned face-down 2/2. With Don it becomes a 4/4 **Pirate with menace/deathtouch**; if it dies, Amalgam makes its owner lose two and you gain two. The body is expensive and delayed, but is unusually commander-specific repeatable material. `Coercive Recruiter` can use newly entering Pirate creatures; `Admiral Beckett Brass` checks three or more Pirates actually dealing combat damage **to the same player** to steal one nonland permanent at end step (attacker ownership is irrelevant to Pirates you control for that count). The taken permanent, if not a creature you control but don't own, doesn't automatically get Don's creature buff. `Brainstealer Dragon` and `Tasha, the Witch Queen` offer costly but thematic stealing/advantage, not generic discard-matters.

A hostile opponent may exile their graveyard, counter the foreign spell after Don already triggered (Treasure still possible), remove Don before an acquired creature deals combat damage, sacrifice targeted creatures before `Coercive Recruiter` resolves, or prevent multiple Pirates from actually hitting a player. Don's static buffs end if Don leaves; no ongoing +2/+2 or Pirate-grant memory remains.
- `Roaming Throne` choosing Pirate can add **one extra instance of Don's triggered Treasure ability** for an opponent-owned noncreature spell cast, but **cannot double Don's continuous/static +2/+2 ability**. If an opponent with control of your owned creature loses, ownership changes neither permanently nor by layer; the original owner remains you and it isn't one you *don't own*.
- Opponents' deck composition materially changes performance. Creature-light storm/control opponents can leave very few bodies to steal but many noncreatures to cast; creature-dense, spell-light boards provide the opposite. Nonowned cards exiled but uncast produce no trigger. Copied spells can look stolen but are frequently **owned by the player who created the copy**, even when their rules text is identical to an opponent's card.

## Independent challenger: `Mob Rule` versus `Insurrection` (not an approved swap)

**Primary test hypothesis: ADD `Mob Rule` (`{4}{R}{R}`), CUT `Insurrection` (`{5}{R}{R}{R}`).** Both are powerful **one-turn theft** sorceries that untap gained creatures and give haste, making every taken opponent-owned creature receive Don's +2/+2, menace, deathtouch and Pirate type if Don survives. `Mob Rule` is two mana cheaper and requires one fewer red source; on turn 6 it could transform a medium board into immediate lethal attacks while Insurrection remains stranded at eight mana/three red. The lost cost matters in a three-color theft deck with many expensive ongoing engines.

**The decisive counterargument for KEEP:** Mob Rule chooses **one class**—all creatures power **4+**, or all creatures power **3 or less**—on casting and steals only qualifying bodies when it resolves. On a mixed table it **cannot steal both classes**. Power is evaluated for choosing the effect before Don's stolen-creature +2/+2 buff applies, so later growth does not recruit an unselected class. `Insurrection` steals **every creature at once**, denying all opposing blockers and preserving all their attack triggers/combat bodies regardless of printed/current power; Don may turn this board-wide theft into a same-turn multiplayer win. The deck includes nine mana rocks and many Treasure lines that can realistically accelerate eight mana. Cutting the highest-ceiling, most on-theme theft finisher for a weaker partial sweep may lower the real optimized ceiling. Conversely, an eight-mana finisher that is trapped in hand during crucial turns can have zero value. Without representative board and mana data there is no proven swap.

**Alternative challenge:** `Zara, Renegade Recruiter` `{3}{U}{R}` flying Pirate repeatedly raids an opponent's **hand** for a creature on attack, placing it tapped and attacking temporarily (thus Don buffs it). The borrowed body was not declared an attacker, so its own attack triggers do not fire. Zara's summoning delay, need to attack, dependence on opponent's creature-filled hand and five-mana cost may make it worse than existing `Thief of Sanity` or `Wand of Wonder`; choosing a cut would sacrifice either low-curve foreign-card access or noncreature Treasure triggers. It merits second-tier comparison only if the Mob Rule/Insurrection result suggests creature theft is underrepresented.

Test the full eight-mana line rather than assuming a cheaper card is automatically stronger. Secondary possible `Zara` tests only after a genuine gap is evidenced.
