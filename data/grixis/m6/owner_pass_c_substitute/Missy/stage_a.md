## Commander-specific identity and natural ceiling

Missy costs **{3}{U}{B}{R}**, 4/5. Whenever **another nonartifact creature dies**, she returns that card to the battlefield **under our control**, **face down and tapped**, as a nameless colorless **2/2 Cyberman artifact creature**. At the beginning of our end step, opponents sequentially make a villainous choice: each artifact creature we control damages that opponent for one each, **or** we draw a card and chaos ensues. The source is opponent-facing politics/value, not a mandatory damage trigger; different opponents may choose different modes.

The protected native game plan is (a) kill/sacrifice **nonartifact nontoken creatures**, including opponents', converting bodies to Cybermen; (b) stack artifact-creature/face-down infrastructure and morph/disguise flip tricks; (c) punish choices and convert sacrificed Cybermen into aristocrat resources; (d) win via Missy's end-step pressure, artifacts in combat, sacrifice/drain and secondary payoff, not by substituting generic turbo Grixis combo. Historical ceiling source and GrokBot estimates both report **Bracket 4 — Optimized**. This six-mana commander needs U/B/R fixing and usually survives an opponent cycle before snowballing; no evidence supports a forced Bracket 5 recast.

**Critical nonloop:** Missy checks the dead creature's **last battlefield characteristics**. Once returned as an artifact Cyberman, that face-down creature's later death is an **artifact-creature** death and by itself does NOT re-trigger Missy. Nontoken vs token is separate: dying tokens can cause triggers but cannot be returned from the graveyard after state-based actions. Cybermen are **tapped** on entry, not immediate sacrifice fodder for tapping-cost outlets, but sacrifice-without-tap costs may be paid when one receives priority. Opponent commanders sent to the command zone after dying cannot be retrieved from the graveyard by Missy's later trigger; do not count automatic commander theft.

**Key Oracle/rules separation:** A face-down permanent put out by Missy is not automatically a normally manifested/cloaked card. The fact of being face down does not generally grant permission to pay its front-face mana cost and flip it; a printed morph/disguise special action or an outside instruction such as Ugin's Mastery can matter. Conversely face-down morph spells cast for {3} are colorless creature spells and therefore can supply Ugin's Mastery's colorless-cast trigger; do not dismiss that four-mana enchantment as blank. Panoptic Projektor accelerates a later face-down creature **spell**, not Missy's no-cast resurrection, and doubles relevant triggered abilities **caused by turning a permanent face up**, not the end-step villainous-choice trigger itself.

## Neutral candidate challenges and exact alternative cuts

**Preferred first-pass challenger: +Living Death / -Cybership — provisional NEEDS TESTING.** Living Death is a **{3}{B}{B}** sorcery. As its single resolution proceeds, each player first exiles creature cards from their graveyard, then sacrifices all creatures, then puts the initially exiled creatures onto the battlefield. With Missy present when nonartifact creatures are sacrificed, Missy **triggers for other qualifying nontoken nonartifact creatures even if Missy dies simultaneously**; those triggers wait until Living Death finishes and can return the *newly sacrificed* creatures from graveyards under our control as face-down, tapped Cybermen. This may exploit an opposing board without targeting, while recoverable old graveyard creatures enter face up for their respective owners. It is a real identity-conversion hypothesis, different from merely playing a generic reanimation staple.

**Why -Cybership is even arguable:** six generic mana plus crew 4 and **player combat damage** is a slow, telegraphed way to get two face-down Cybermen from an opponent's library, and it competes in high-curve payoff slots with the six-mana commander; five-mana Living Death can impact boards immediately without attack success. However Cybership is a flying 8/8 artifact Vehicle with a genuine unique Cyberman generation and combat victory plan, creates Cybermen on successful hits, supports artifact density, and does not resurrect opposing graveyards. It is a **defended identity card**, not a free cut.

**Strongest counterargument to Living Death:** it is symmetrical and **erases our existing artifact-Cyberman army** while not recycling those dying artifacts with Missy; those previously stolen face-down bodies go to owners' graveyards and do not re-enter from the first exile step. It also resurrects opposing pre-existing graveyard creatures and sacrifices Missy herself, removing future choice pressure unless we recast her with extra commander tax. Against a graveyard-stacked opponent or when ahead on Cybermen, Living Death is plausibly disastrous. This is not a proven upgrade or an unconditional combo. The ordering and last-known-state interactions require case-specific verification, and some noncreature/DFC corner cases must be checked with exact current rules.

**Alternate cuts:** -Ugin's Mastery seems tempting from nominal colorless creature count, **but** morph/disguise face-down casts are colorless spells and Ugin's Mastery flips Cybermen as an attack-threshold effect; replacing it can hollow out the face-down package. -Panoptic Projektor accelerates *cast* face-down spells and multiplies flip-caused triggers, not Missy's resurrection/choice; nevertheless the deck has meaningful flip engines (Pyrotechnic Performer and morph suite), so cutting it sacrifices real uniqueness. -Cybermen Squadron looks mana-inefficient at seven but grants myriad to the exact artifact-creature army Missy constructs; it is therefore one of the **most protected** possible cuts. Test only if challenger beats these lost roles in actual sequences.

**Other additions to inspect without inventing superiority:** Blasting Station ({3}) taps/sacrifices a creature to ping any target and may untap whenever any creature enters. Missy's *nonartifact* deaths can cause Cybermen enters, but the deck already runs Goblin Bombardment (free targeted ping), Altar of Dementia, both colored/colorless Altars and Viscera Seer. Station has different timing (tap/untap trigger) and potentially converts mass-resurrection entries to multiple pings, but requires sacrifice fodder and is not by itself infinite with Missy: sacrificing an artifact Cyberman does not recreate it. A direct Cybermen Squadron cut would trade a unique combat multiplier for redundant sacrifice reach; that is a high burden of proof and a known earlier disclosed hypothesis, not this neutral review's preferred pairing. Massacre Wurm offers immediate -2/-2 opposing sweeper pressure at six mana but tokens do not become lasting Cybermen and it requires BB; an alternative high-end board-reset role, not clearly better than Living Death. Generic replacement-control cards that **exile** opponent death (e.g., Kalitas/Gisa) may block Missy's dies recursion, so cannot be assumed synergies.

## Opponent counterplay, timing cases and tests needed

1. **Target/removal and timing:** kill or exile Missy before a death event to prevent triggers. If the Missy trigger is already on stack, removing her later generally does not cancel it. Opponent can remove the graveyard card before the return trigger resolves; replacement exile effects prevent dying into graveyard; commander-zone movement can deny the object. Resolve the exact Living Death order without granting anyone priority between its instructions.
2. **Three-board stress cases:** (A) opponents control multiple nontoken nonartifact creatures, graveyards empty, our Missy just cast and few Cybermen: Living Death may convert several opposing bodies into our artifacts; (B) we already have six Cybermen, opponent has a reanimation-rich graveyard: Living Death sacrifices our army and revives enemy threats, likely worse; (C) one graveyard-hate effect and board sweeper response: both challenger and Missy's trigger depend on real destination/ability state.
3. **Mana pressure:** we must first pay six including UBR to deploy Missy, then five including BB for Living Death unless drawn first and held. Cybership can be deployed without colored pips as a 6-drop, but crew 4/connecting into player with flyers/removal is not assured. Count realistic colored mana from slow lands, Talismans and fetches and early protection windows; do not simulate unmodeled opponent tables as win rates.
4. **Engine comparison:** on the same representative six/seven-mana board, record number of *nonartifact nontoken* dying cards returning as cybermen, own preexisting Cybermen lost, opponents' resurrected threats, commander tax, and end-step Missy choices forfeited. Cybermen Squadron myriad triggers on declared attackers, not tokens put attacking; myriad tokens exile after combat and are not nontoken resurrection fuel.
5. **Rules corner cases:** dying token trigger with no return; Cyberman artifacts do not recurse; opponent commander moved to command zone; face-down card turned face-up only by valid rule/effect; Living Death's order and Missy's simultaneous death; per-opponent villainous choices performed in turn order. The commander is not present to provide end-step choice after she dies to a Living Death unless restored by another effect.
