## Structure, mana, and battlefield threats

The 38-land count plus roughly eleven dedicated low-cost artifact mana cards is a plausible infrastructure for a seven-mana Grixis commander, but early generic colorless acceleration does not automatically solve U/B/R access. Fetchlands and typed duals give excellent fixing; Bojuka Bog/utility lands, Ancient Tomb, and Rogue's Passage mean some opening hands still defer commander casting. No exact colored-source/curve distribution or mulligan frequency is asserted. A seven-mana commander removed once costs nine on replay, *before* equipping or activating Rogue's Passage; the deck can lose tempo despite above-average mana. Equipment and aura exposure is substantial, especially against exile, sacrifice, sweepers, and countermagic in response to protection/equip sequencing. Opponents can remove Garza **before** Chandra's Ignition/Nova Flame resolves, counter the targeted spell, or remove equipment before combat; two-mana instant mana held up for protection often competes with equipping.

**Real conditional slots:** Inquisitor's Flail offers lethal damage amplification but makes Garza receive double damage in combat, increasing the risk of trading into blockers. Quietus Spike is expensive to cast/equip and its halving is life loss, not commander damage; however, it is one of very few **repeatable deathtouch** sources, so cutting it damages the distinctive death/counter plan. Robe of the Archmagi adds exceptional cards *on connection* but uses three mana to cast and four to equip to this non-Wizard, often competing with immediate protection. Scythe of the Wretched's creature-theft utility is highly situational yet specifically rewards Garza dealing damage before death; it is not generic filler. Hatred risks life and removal blowouts but uniquely supplies surprise commander-damage lethality. Curiosity's inexpensive noncombat-damage trigger synergy counsels against labeling it redundant. The Ozolith/Blade/Soulcleaver are core counter retention/scaling, not free cuts.

## Neutral first-look challengers and alternatives

1. **Primary hypothesis: add Fireshrieker / cut Inquisitor's Flail.** Fireshrieker costs {3}, equips for {2}, and grants double strike; Flail costs {2}, equips for {2}, doubles combat damage dealt **and received** by equipped Garza. On an unblocked 5/5, each produces **10 commander combat damage** (before other modifiers). Fireshrieker creates **two separate** combat damage steps and ordinarily two Garza draw triggers; Flail generates one. With a normal 5/5 blocker and no other modifiers, Fireshrieker lets Garza deal five first-strike damage and potentially kill it **before the return combat-damage step**; Flail doubles five damage dealt to the blocker but also doubles five damage dealt back to Garza and can kill her. Fire costs one extra up front and is telegraphed, so this is a *test hypothesis*, not a measured improvement. Flail's double damage can be particularly valuable with life-changing damage replacement effects, infect and Jeska; assess the actual configured attack state before discarding it.
2. **Alternative cut Robe of the Archmagi:** Removes the heaviest equipment-based card-flow investment and lowers equip demand, but on a five-power connecting Garza the Robe can produce roughly five additional cards **plus her own draw**, significantly improving rebuilding or finding a counterspell. It may be much stronger against attrition. Do not make this cut without turn-by-turn draw/kill evidence.
3. **Alternative cut Quietus Spike:** Reduces the life-halving combat race while retaining the direct commander-damage race. Counterargument: losing one of only two reusable deathtouch lines (Spike and Collar) weakens Garza's personal creature-kill/counter engine and the expensive Thornbite Staff synergy. Disfavored without a replacement source of deathtouch.
4. **Alternative additions: Lizard Blades / Temur Battle Rage.** Lizard Blades's reconfigure double strike may come down earlier than Fireshrieker, at the cost of creature-type exposure and needing an equip-style activation; verify current exact text before claiming better total costs. Temur Battle Rage is cheaper and provides immediate double strike/trample on this base-power-five commander, but it is a one-shot trick, vulnerable to a reply, and overlaps Psychotic Fury. These are **candidate names under consideration**, not previously ranked suggestions, and no unverified Oracle/timing claim should decide the slot.

## Rules-critical deterministic traces (not measured tests)

- **Garza + Basilisk Collar + Thornbite Staff:** Staff grants `{2}, {T}: This creature deals 1 damage to any target` and an untap on **any creature dying**. Collar grants deathtouch/lifelink. One lethal ping to an eligible opposing creature makes it die via state-based actions, triggers Garza's +1/+1 and Staff's untap. Repeating requires **another {2} per activation**, valid targets and the equipment staying attached; it is **not an infinite free-damage combo**. Staff additionally costs {2} to cast and {4} to equip, Collar {1} to cast and {2} to equip, a significant setup investment. Garza's haste allows a same-turn tap if all costs are paid. Indestructible creatures or damage prevention may prevent the kill/untap; an untap can also be triggered by an independent death.
- **Chandra's Ignition / Nova Flame:** The targeted Garza deals the damage, so deathtouch/lifelink matter and creatures she damaged that subsequently die trigger her counters. Ignition's damage to **opponents** is **noncombat**: it does not count as commander damage and does not trigger her combat-draw ability. Nova Flame `{X}{2}{R}{R}` puts X counters on the targeted Garza **before** she deals damage equal to her enlarged power to **each other creature**, including friendly creatures such as Kediss; it does not by itself damage players. Target removal/countermagic is decisive. Current Nova Flame text was checked against Wizards' Marvel Super Heroes release notes; not a simulated result.
- **Double strike:** A Garza that successfully deals combat damage to a player in both strike steps receives two combat-damage draw opportunities; Kediss likewise triggers on each qualifying hit, but Kediss is the **source** of its reflected damage, so neither commander-damage accounting nor Garza's own combat-draw trigger expands to the other opponents. In an infect/Grafted Exoskeleton configuration, damage dealt to players creates poison counters and is still damage; track separately from regular life loss and commander damage. When Garza is blocked, without trample, killing a blocker in first strike does not by itself make her deal regular-step combat damage to the defending player.
- **Quietus Spike:** Its half-life trigger causes **loss of life**, not combat damage and not commander damage. The deathtouch half is still important because killing creatures is one of Garza's native engines.
- **Aggravated Assault + Sword of Feast and Famine:** Sword combat hit untaps lands, but Assault must be activated at sorcery speed in a main phase; it grants an additional combat and main phase. Repeating requires adequate lands that actually produce the five required mana and a player who can still be dealt combat damage. Blockers, removed equipment/creatures and instant interaction stop the live route. No deterministic or measured frequency is claimed.

## Opponent response windows and strongest argument against the primary hypothesis

**Open-board line:** both Flail and Fireshrieker increase an unmodified Garza's five commander combat damage to ten; permanent double strike yields an extra draw on a successful two-step hit, but is **one additional upfront mana**. Both still require connecting, which Regalia/Boots/Rogue's Passage enable with their own costs.

**Blocked-board line:** first-strike damage kills many normal-size blockers before reciprocal combat damage, making Fireshrieker much safer than Flail. Deathtouch plus first strike magnifies this advantage, but deathtouch requires a separate card and equip activation; do not treat the multi-card state as free.

**Adversarial line:** an opponent can counter/kill Garza in response to equip, remove the double-strike equipment before damage, or stop the first-strike damage with fog/prevention. Holding protection costs mana. If opponent blockers already have first strike, indestructible, very high toughness or removal, the first-strike advantage shrinks. Inquisitor's Flail is **cheaper** and doubles any other combat-damage increase, offers the same baseline commander damage in one packet, and may synergize more efficiently with Grafted Exoskeleton or Hatred. The deck already includes a cheap double-strike instant, Psychotic Fury. **It is entirely plausible KEEP is correct** and the new repeatable equipment would be redundant rather than a net improvement; the objective is to test this against actual keep/cut opportunity costs.
