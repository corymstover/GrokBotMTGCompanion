## Critical correction to the immutable Stage A

**Stage A mistakenly identifies Kediss as the source of reflected damage.** That is false. The actual Kediss text refers back to **the commander**: when Garza deals combat damage to one opponent, **Garza herself** deals an equal amount of *noncombat* damage to each other opponent. Wizards' 2020 release notes explicitly identify it as damage dealt by the commander, while saying it isn't combat damage, isn't tracked for the commander-21 combat threshold, and does not retrigger Kediss. Preserve Stage A's error as a traceable independent-first-look finding; correct it here and in final Stage C.

Consequences materially relevant to this deck:

- Garza wielding **Grafted Exoskeleton** has infect; the Garza-sourced reflected damage **also** has infect. One unblocked commander hit that deals at least 10 damage can distribute lethal poison to the hit player and the other opponents if appropriate board state. This is **not** multiple commander-21 kills: only the directly hit player gets actual commander combat damage. Note the full Exoskeleton `unattached → sacrifice equipped creature` hazard.
- **Curiosity** attached to Garza sees each Garza-sourced damaging event to an opponent, including the additional Kediss noncombat damage. In a four-player table, one unblocked Garza combat connection with Kediss can cause **up to three separate Curiosity draws plus Garza's one optional combat draw**, subject to damage/prevention/stack interaction. Robe of the Archmagi, by contrast, triggers only on actual combat damage, not on Kediss noncombat splashes.
- **Fireshrieker** on Garza with Kediss can create a Kediss trigger *per* successful combat-damage step, and each reflected packet is sourced by Garza, including infect if applicable. It creates a meaningful response window between first-strike and normal damage, while Flail's doubled damage can kill in one packet and deny this second window. This corrective fact **strengthens both the primary challenger and the incumbent's single-packet counterargument**.

## Focused effect-aware comparisons (deterministic only)

These are **rules-based scenario calculations**, *not* a randomized simulation, actual 4-player match result or Phase B full certificate:

1. **Unblocked native Garza 5/5 with no other effect.** Flail `2 cast + 2 equip` produces one ten-combat-damage packet to one player, one Garza combat-draw trigger and one Kediss trigger if available. Fireshrieker `3 cast + 2 equip` produces two five-damage combat packets, potentially two Garza combat-draw triggers and two Kediss triggers. Total **10** direct commander combat damage if both strike steps hit; challenger costs one more upfront, increases cards and triggers. If Garza has Curiosity and Kediss in a four-player pod, each direct combat-damage event and each Garza-sourced splash event can trigger Curiosity separately: **up to six Curiosity draws across two full connections** vs **three with one Flail damage event**; as always removal/prevention/hand capacity may matter. No claim of such a full battlefield's frequency.
2. **Blocked 5/5 without first strike/deathtouch and no trample.** Fireshrieker Garza deals five first-strike damage to the blocker, killing it before it can deal normal-step combat damage, so Garza survives if unmodified. Flail Garza deals ten and the blocker deals ten to Garza simultaneously; Garza dies. Challenger's first-strike role materially preserves a taxed commander. The blocked attacker does **not** suddenly damage the player in the regular step absent trample.
3. **Exoskeleton infect / priority.** Assume Exoskeleton-equipped Garza is at least 7 power and unblocked: Flail doubles to at least 14 poison in **one combat packet** and the Garza-sourced Kediss splash also carries infect. Fireshrieker instead deals a first-strike packet of at least 7 poison, then creates a priority window before an additional at-least-7 packet; opponents can remove Garza or equipment in between. Both eventually produce lethal poison only if the relevant damage packets resolve and lands/gear/evasion support them. **The original Flail can be materially better at one-shot resilience** and lower equipment cost. If Garza has 5 native power without Exo, no infect occurs, regardless of double strike.
4. **Opponent removal and commander-tax cost.** Fireshrieker's first-strike survival edge depends on the threat being a blocker actually killable in first strike; indestructible, first strike/double strike, large toughness or damage prevention weaken it. Equipment removal before the first combat damage step defeats both; removal between double-strike steps uniquely punishes Fireshrieker. Flail's damage-received doubling makes fair combat dangerous, while its one less setup mana could leave another protection mana open sooner. Count exact commander cast cost `4UBR` and re-cast tax +2, not just equipment efficiency.
5. **Competing cuts.** `-Robe / +Fireshrieker` reduces equipment equip mana and draw-on-hit burst but may strand a high-pressure control matchup; `-Quietus / +Fireshrieker` loses a deathtouch enabler vital to Garza+Staff and a separate life-total route. `-Inquisitor's Flail` is still the cleaner comparison because it retains the same direct damage-doubling role, but it replaces a one-packet finisher with a two-step finisher. No measured improvement among them.
