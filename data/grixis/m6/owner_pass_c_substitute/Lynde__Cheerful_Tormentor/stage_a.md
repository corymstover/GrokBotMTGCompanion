## Canonical structure and identity

The TXT has **93 name-rows**, physical quantities sum **100** (99 library + Lynde), **93 distinct names**; **35 lands**, ten basics (**Island ×4, Mountain ×3, Swamp ×3**). Only basic lands repeat. U/B/R color-identity visibly fits the list, subject to formal whole-name verification. No designated owner project exception is needed.

Lynde is a **{1}{U}{B}{R} Human Warlock with deathtouch**. When a Curse **you control goes from battlefield to *your* graveyard**, Lynde creates an end-step return attached to you. At **the beginning of your upkeep**, each Lynde upkeep trigger can transfer **one** Curse attached to you to **one opponent**; if successfully attached, draw **two cards**. She rewards carefully recycling hazardous player-Auras, *not simply maximizing unrelated enchantment value*. The shell has roughly twenty-one dedicated Curse/Aura effects, plus Claws of Gix, Despotic Scepter, Braids, Perilous Research, Demon of Fate's Design, Curse of Misfortunes, doubled upkeeps (Paradox Haze, The Ninth Doctor, Obeka), and enchantment-copy engines (Estrid's Invocation, Copy Enchantment, Mirrormade, Clever Impersonator).

Primary victory is accumulated **upkeep curse pressure, targeted player attrition/politics, enchanted-player hand denial, and damage/death triggers**, using Misfortunes to find further Curses and Lynde to recover lost Curse investments. The natural ceiling is tentatively **Bracket 4 — Optimized**, not a universal Grixis cEDH engine, constrained by enchantment timing, targeting, political incentives and four-mana Lynde access. ## Mana and strategic constraints

Thirty-five lands include early untapped dual/fetch/rainbow sources but also Bojuka Bog and Xander's Lounge entering tapped, heavy black Curves (Curse of Thirst/Unbinding, Misfortunes, Doomwake, Demon) and many three-to-six-mana enchantments. The exact **seven nonland rocks** are Arcane Signet, Dimir Signet, Rakdos Signet, Talisman of Creativity/Dominance/Indulgence, and Sol Ring; **Fellwar Stone is not in this list**. Nightscape Familiar helps only qualifying R/U spells' generic portions. Curse of Opulence may help generate Gold only once attackers actually attack enchanted player. The engine can stall with high-end Curses and no Lynde/Misfortunes, or produce excessive self-damage if a returned Curse stays on you.

Curses with an opponent enchanted can generate highly unequal political outcomes: Opulence/Disturbance/Verbosity promote attacks; Surveillance can draw only the controller if that one target is chosen (it is **not forced to draw each opponent**); Bloodletting/Thirst/Realities close games but high mana; Obsession draws the cursed player two extra per draw step **before** they discard their hand at end step, giving them windows to cast spells/instants. ## Adversarial trigger, zone and upkeep trace

1. **Lynde recursion:** A Curse you own/control is sacrificed with Claws of Gix during your **precombat main phase** while Lynde is in play. It goes from battlefield to your graveyard, so the delayed return triggers at beginning of **that turn's** end step, returns attached to you if still in the applicable zone and has a legal attachment. Your next upkeep can transfer **one** such attached Curse to an opponent and draw two cards. Opponent can exile it from graveyard or remove Lynde at a critical earlier window; Lynde's already-created delayed effect isn't retroactively canceled by her leaving.
2. **End-step bottleneck:** If **Braids, Arisen Nightmare** sacrifices a Curse at **the beginning of your end step**, Lynde's delayed return was created **after** this end-step beginning and cannot trigger in the past; it waits until the **next** end step. Braids can still pay off in card advantage/pressure, but it is slower as a Curse-recycling engine than Claws/Despotic before end step.
3. **One-per-upkeep transfer:** With **three** Curses already attached to you and Lynde, a single ordinary upkeep trigger moves at most **one** to another player and draws two; the other two remain on you. Paradox Haze on yourself grants an additional upkeep, allowing a second transfer/draw **if Curses remain** and Lynde survives. It doesn't move all curses on each trigger; effects already triggered at beginning of upkeep may resolve even after transferring their source.
4. **Ninth Doctor condition:** His extra-upkeep trigger requires him to **become untapped during your untap step**. Simply being in play already untapped grants no extra upkeep. The trigger uses the first following upkeep stack and generates a second. Tapping him to attack on a previous turn then surviving to untap is one way; the actual extra-upkeep and Curse count matter.
5. **Obeka condition:** Obeka, Splitter of Seconds triggers on actual **combat damage to a player** (2-power normally); she then creates that many additional upkeep steps after the combat phase (additional beginning phases omit untap/draw). Each one can process a single Lynde-attached self-Curse. Blockers, removal and a lack of self-Curses can make those extra upkeep triggers low-value. Opponents' upkeep-triggered Curses on opponents do **not** trigger in **your** extra upkeeps merely because you get extras.
6. **Accursed Witch DFC:** `Accursed Witch` creature's death can return transformed as `Infectious Curse` attached to an opponent under its own text. If **Infectious Curse** is later put into your graveyard as a Curse while Lynde is in play, Lynde's delayed effect returns the **double-faced card front face (Accursed Witch)** by default under CR 712.14, not a back-face Infectious Curse attached to you. The attachment instruction cannot turn a creature into an Aura. The route is cyclic but takes further death/transform steps; do not count an immediately reattached Infectious Curse.
7. **Copying:** Copy Enchantment/Estrid's Invocation/Mirrormade can become copies of Curses. A copier that is a Curse on battlefield when put into **your** graveyard should be tested with last-known-characteristics for Lynde's leaves trigger; the card re-enters as its printed non-Aura enchantment and chooses copy again, with Aura entering/attachment restrictions. An exiled/blinked Estrid copy **didn't go to graveyard** and does not trigger Lynde; token copies leave GY before any delayed return and cannot be recurrently regained.
8. **Curse controller and attachment:** Curse of Surveillance enchants one player and on that player's upkeep draws cards for **any number of controller-chosen target players other than enchanted** equal to the number of Curses on that player. The controller can choose just themselves, so do not assume free draws for all opponents. Lynde's upkeep move changes attachment but **does not change who controls the Aura**.
9. **Sacrifice/destroy:** Despotic Scepter's `{T}: Destroy target permanent you own` destroys an owned Curse even if enchanting an opponent (absent indestructibility) and seeds Lynde recursion. Claws of Gix pays a mana and sacrifices any permanent for one life. Demon of Fate's Design's enchantment sac outlet costs {2}{B}; its once-per-turn life-instead-of-mana cast ability is conditional and expensive in life. Casting for life remains an alternative cost and cannot be paired with another alternative cost.
10. **Opponent counterplay:** remove Lynde before Curse goes to your graveyard so no delayed effect arises, counter Misfortunes, bounce/exile Curses so they **never hit your graveyard**, grave-hate the delayed return's identified card, enchantment wipes after Lynde removed, steal controlled Curses, keep shields against aura attachment, or force you to self-curse at end step. If a cursed opponent **leaves the game**, the Aura you own normally goes to your graveyard and can cause Lynde's ability to trigger as an official Lynde ruling confirms.

## Weakest actual hypotheses and unranked challenger

- **Curse of Echoes** ({4}{U}) creates *copies of the enchanted opponent's instant/sorcery spells for each other player who wants one*. You get a copy, but so can all other opponents; it's a **political symmetry cost** at five mana, particularly hazardous when enemy spells are removal/counters and opposing players gain free answers. As a Curse subtype it still fuels Misfortunes/Thirst and recycles with Lynde, so a generic 1-for-1 spell evaluation underestimates it.
- **Curse of Obsession** ({4}{R}) gives enchanted player two additional draws at start of their draw step before dumping their hand at end step; it can increase instant-speed response options at the table, including removal before Lynde's own next turn. It is commander-flavor faithful, but actual net suppression varies by table. Compare not simply discard count but **timing of extra resources**.
- **Curse of Vengeance** ({1}{R}) accrues spite counters on opponent casts and pays gain/draw **only on opponent elimination**; strong when accumulated and one opponent dies but conditional as tempo tool. Cheap curse density/political targeting may justify it even if slow.
- **Curse of Chaos** ({2}{R}; player-attack rummage/political interaction) is a plausible lower-curve unranked challenger to **Curse of Echoes**. It improves early attach/Curse-of-Misfortunes fodder and can encourage attacks on the enchanted player, but may give opponents card filtering and lacks high-ceiling instant/sorcery copy bursts. This is a **test hypothesis, not proven strict upgrade**. Competing cut **Curse of Vengeance** loses cheap scaling draw on player elimination, and cutting Nightscape Familiar loses needed generic cost savings.
- **Curse of Misfortunes** and **Doomwake Giant** look expensive but supply the distinct tutor/curse stack and enchantment-entering control axis; do not blindly remove them to homogenize Grixis. **Paradox Haze**, Obeka and Ninth Doctor are different conditional upkeeps, not guaranteed triple trigger on a naked board.

**Strongest counterargument to changes:** The original curated curse density matters immensely—Curse of Misfortunes searches a specific Curse each enchanted player's upkeep, and Lynde turns card loss into repeatable attachments and card draw. A cheaper one-off threat can lower the number of worthwhile Curse targets, while redundant extra-upkeeps magnify the distinctive commander. Most of the identified 'weak' slots have unusual board states that justify inclusion, and no proper opponent-response simulations here show a better exact 100.
