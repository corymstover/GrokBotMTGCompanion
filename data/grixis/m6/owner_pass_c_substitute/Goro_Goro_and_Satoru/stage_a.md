## Exact list structure and commander identity

Approved TXT: **99 printed rows with 100 copies**, because **basic Island appears 2x**; 99 distinct names, commander 1x, all other names 1x; **33 land copies**, **66 nonland library cards**, one commander. The two Islands are valid basic-land repetition, not a singleton failure. No companion, and this particular deck has no separately recorded project exception. Commander identity is U/B/R. The owner's approval reports existing verification; a new live 100-card Oracle/legality sweep was **not** run in this Stage A.

Wizards' [March of the Machine release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/march-of-the-machine-release-notes) establish `{U}{B}{R}` 3/4 legendary Goblin Human **Goro-Goro and Satoru**: whenever **one or more creatures you control that entered the battlefield this turn** deal **combat damage to a player**, create a **5/5 red Dragon Spirit creature token with flying**; `{1}{R}: Creatures you control gain haste until end of turn.` The haste ability affects **the creatures present when it resolves**, not creatures that arrive later in the turn. Its dragon trigger is **one per damaged player per combat-damage event**, not one per connecting creature; double strike can create separate first-strike/regular-step trigger events. New creatures entering already attacking through ninjutsu/sneak/attack triggers do qualify if they later deal combat damage, notwithstanding they were never formally declared attacking. Creatures already on the battlefield from a prior turn **cannot** become fresh merely by gaining haste.

**Preserve this deck's identity:** Grixis evasive ninjutsu and disposable hasty/attacking token threats generate five-power Dragon Spirits; extra combat phases/Dragon haste and Dragon Tempest/Terror of the Peaks turn tokens into a game ender. It is *not* simply Ninja tribal, generic Grixis tempo or an unrelated tutor-win shell. The commander's natural ceiling is strong **optimized combat-engine (provisional Bracket 4)**, constrained by needing Goro present and *fresh* player combat connections rather than any creature hitting any opponent.

## Plausible conditional incumbents, real challengers and adverse argument

**Neutral first-look challenger: `+Bitterblossom / -Bident of Thassa`.** Bitterblossom costs `{1}{B}` and creates a 1/1 flying Faerie Rogue **during your upkeep** at the price of one life per turn. That Faerie **entered this turn**; with a later Goro `{1}{R}` haste activation or Samut team haste, it can attack in this turn's combat and, if it connects, produce a new Dragon. This is a persistent threat after a creature wipe even when Goro temporarily dies, and the faerie's flying makes a real connection path. Bident costs `{2}{U}{U}` but draws **one per connecting creature**, which can be several per combat (and across double strike steps), and can force enemy creatures to attack by paying/tapping; a faster cheaper token source can improve early Dragon reliability but materially harm the deck's hand refill. This is NOT an approved replacement. For an early three-mana commander, holding up interactive blue mana and sustained card flow might be more valuable than yet another token producer.

**Competing cuts/alternatives:** `-Grazilaxx / +Bitterblossom` preserves Bident card quantity but loses blocked-attacker rescue, Ninja reentry and per-player card draw, important against blockers. `-Waterbender Ascension / +Bitterblossom` preserves Bident but loses cheap evasion access, quest draw and forced passage in locked boards. `+Legion Warboss` produces a 1/1 haste Goblin at combat start from a three-mana body and asks less haste payment than Bitterblossom, but the body is exposed to removal and its token has no flying/menace compared with existing Daring Piracy/Thopter/Fire Navy lines. `+Kari Zev, Skyship Raider` is another low-cost tapped-attacking token source, but Kari Zev herself lacks haste and has to attack first; it may only duplicate the existing repeat-creature package. No ranked candidate list or earlier proposed cut was used. Do not force any of these if their role is already saturated.

**True weak or conditional slots:** Bident's four-mana UU bottleneck and redundancy in draw; Grazilaxx double blue and only one card per damaged player (not each creature); Waterbender quest may be too slow against creatureless control; Rogue's Passage uses four mana and a land tap to make only one creature unblockable; Breath of Fury is expensive/mana-set-up and fails if no legal subsequent creature to attach after sacrifice. But each also has a distinct use: protection/reentry, predictable card flow, late evasion, and combo closers. The first priority should be confirming the real rules and comparing at least two realistic opponents, not treating printed MV as weakness by itself.

## Rules-critical, finite, source-paid scenarios (analytical traces, not simulations)

1. **One dragon per player/event:** two newly-entered unblockable creatures hitting the **same** opponent in one regular combat-damage event create **one 5/5 Dragon Spirit**, not two. One fresh creature hitting A and another fresh creature hitting B simultaneously creates **two Dragons**. If the same fresh attacker has double strike and hits A in both first-strike and regular damage, that is **two distinct combat-damage events** and yields two Dragons. Goro must be on the battlefield when the respective trigger would occur; removing him before damage stops new triggers, but existing triggered abilities survive subsequent removal.
2. **Ninjutsu/sneak:** after defenders are declared, return an unblocked small attacker to hand as ninjutsu cost to put Fallen Shinobi or Yuriko tapped and attacking. The Ninja enters this turn and remains unblocked; successful **player** combat damage creates Goro Dragon(s), with its own Ninjutsu damage effect. Raphael's Sneak also returns an unblocked attacker as cost during the declare-blockers step and the fresh Raphael attacks with double strike. Its entrance does not trigger `whenever attacks` effects, but subsequent actual combat damage still meets Goro's trigger. Opponent can interact with spells between declared blockers and damage.
3. **Haste snapshot hazard:** activate Goro's `{1}{R}` ability **before** a newly-created Dragon exists. That future Dragon does **not** gain haste from the earlier ability, per official Wizards release notes. To attack with it in an extra combat that turn, pay another `{1}{R}` **after** it exists or rely on continuous team/Dragon haste from Samut or Dragon Tempest. A Dragon made at the normal combat damage step cannot retroactively attack in that combat.
4. **Breath of Fury / Dragon ordering:** with Breath enchanted on a fresh, unblocked creature and Goro active, the creature's successful player combat damage can trigger both Breath and Goro. Put Breath on the stack first, then Goro to resolve **the Dragon creation before** Breath's sacrifice/reattach instruction. Breath can attach to that Dragon only if it is a legal creature after original is sacrificed; it untaps creatures and creates an additional combat. Newly made Dragon requires haste before the **next** attack. A Dragon Tempest or Samut already on board supplies continuous haste; otherwise Goro `{1}{R}` must be paid each iteration. Defenders, removed enchanted creature/commander, Fog, lack of legal aura recipient, or protection stop the sequence. Breath's sacrifice is a real cost to progress, not a free trigger.
5. **Dragon Tempest + new token:** each 5/5 red Dragon Spirit from Goro is a Dragon for Tempest's haste and ETB ping; Terror of the Peaks also triggers for each entering 5/5 to deal five to a target if relevant damage succeeds. Such noncombat damage from the token/other source does **not** itself trigger Goro's combat-damage requirement. Multiple Dragon pings can win without more attacks if sufficient Dragons enter, but board and timing are needed.
6. **Determined Iteration:** at beginning of combat, populate an existing Dragon token; the new copy gets haste and is **new this turn**, so if it connects it may make another Goro Dragon. If no creature token is present, populate creates nothing. The haste/sacrifice instruction applies to the *new copy*, not the original. A late Dragon made during combat cannot be retroactively populated by a combat-start trigger that already resolved; next extra combat's beginning is a new event.
7. **Token engine timing:** Daring Piracy creates a hasty menacing 1/1 at beginning of combat; Loyal Apprentice makes a flying hasty token conditionally on commander presence; Urabrask's Forge makes temporary hasty artifact threats; Fire Navy Trebuchet creates its tapped-and-attacking flying 2/1 **after you attack**, which was not declared attacking but can deal combat damage and satisfy Goro. Unblocked connection matters; merely attacking, or dealing damage to a planeswalker/battle, does not produce a Dragon.
8. **Real costs of extra combats:** Aggravated Assault activation costs `{3}{R}{R}`, main-phase speed. Karlach requires her first-combat attack trigger; Great Train Heist requires paying chosen mode costs; newly entered Dragons need haste at the correct timing, and multiple combat steps don't automatically produce tokens without more fresh hits. Breath of Fury is distinct and can continue only while a legal enchanted attacker connects. No unbounded infinite win was tested or claimed.
9. **Bitterblossom opportunity cost in two sample states:** in turn-four open-board, Bitterblossom started on T2 makes flying 1/1 at T3/T4 upkeep, and T3 Goro after UBR and T4 `{1}{R}` haste can convert current token's connection into a Dragon. In a board with flyers/cheap blockers or Goro removed before T4 damage, it only adds Faeries at life cost. In contrast Bident T4 with two connecting evasive creatures can draw two immediately on that combat (assuming mana/casting before attack), sustain land/protection and defeat a mana-strained 33-land stall. Neither state proves an aggregate win-rate improvement.

## Strongest counterargument and first-pass decision

**A sturdy KEEP argument:** existing cheap hasty/attacking makers (Daring Piracy, Loyal Apprentice, Urabrask's Forge, Fire Navy Trebuchet, Phoenix Chick), ninjutsu, plus global haste (Samut/Dragon Tempest) and Determined Iteration already saturate the desired fresh-attacker role. Bident's every-connecting-creature card draw helps the deck survive sweepers and assemble protection/combat turns and can scale much harder than Bitterblossom's one 1/1 per upkeep. The deck is only **33 lands**; reducing accessible cards can cause more missed land drops and prevent winning even when a Dragon is generated. Also Bitter's token usually demands a separate two-mana haste grant, so its printed 2MV is deceptive. The current 100 may already be the better focused version.
