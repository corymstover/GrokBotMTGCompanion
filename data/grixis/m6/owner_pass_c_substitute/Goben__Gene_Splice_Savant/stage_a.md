## 1. Commander-specific operating system and natural ceiling

Norman's {1}{U} unblockable front is reliable early access to **connive on actual combat damage**: draw one, discard one, optionally grow 1/1 if the card discarded is nonland. It does not connive on an attack declaration, and it needs a surviving combat-damage event. Unlike generic storm commanders, Norman therefore builds a discard/velocity resource before the combo window. His sorcery-speed {1}{U}{B}{R} transform is a separate four-mana activation; the back is a four-mana face that can alternatively be cast directly as a modal double-faced commander under the Spider-Man rules. The back is not simply +2 mana to a graveyard-spell's generic cost: it **reduces generic costs by up to {2} on spells CAST FROM the graveyard** while granting each nonland card *currently in the graveyard* a mayhem cost equal to printed mana cost. The card must **have been discarded this turn** to use mayhem. Normal timing continues to apply. Putative discounted spell examples: `Big Score` {3}{R} costs {1}{R} through Goblin mayhem *if legally discarded this turn*, plus its mandatory discard; `Unstable Experiment` {1}{U} costs {U}. Previously milled or tutored-to-grave cards cannot be assumed mayhem-eligible.

Natural ceiling is Bracket **5 / cEDH-style turbo**: a quick commander-enabled loot/discard engine plus explosive mana and deterministic alternate wins. Do not confuse a naturally fast competitive ceiling with making commander irrelevant: Green Goblin's unique mayhem-and-reduction window is a real payoff, but two independently capable compact win lines are sensible recovery against graveyard hate or absent commander.

## 3. Adversarial named-card assessment

**Most plausible independently identified challenger: `Cephalid Coliseum` (land) / tentative cut `Unstable Experiment` (instant).** Coliseum taps for {U} while dealing 1 damage to its controller, and with **threshold seven cards in graveyard** can pay {U}, tap and sacrifice itself to target a player to draw three then discard three. The activation can make up to three discarded nonland cards eligible for same-turn mayhem if Green Goblin is live and spells are cast at legal times, turning a mana slot into a burst engine. Unlike a nonland, it also improves opening seven-card land presence and guaranteed blue land availability, relevant to both commander front and back. It does *not* itself recast or make sorcery-speed spells castable at instant speed, and using it sacrifices a land. The one-to-one replacement remains exactly 100 with **24 lands** and same singleton/Grixis identity, pending actual current rules/legality and testing.

**Why `Unstable Experiment` is the candidate weak slot, not a declared bad card:** It costs {1}{U}; draws a card for target player and has up to one target creature you control connive. In an all-in 23-land list its setup may sometimes be incremental; Coliseum potentially makes three discard triggers and improves land access. But the *strongest counterargument* is serious: Experiment is a cheap instant with a draw + a deliberately controllable discard, supports Norman or another creature early, can be cast for effectively **{U} from graveyard with Mayhem+Goblin** after being discarded, and doesn't require seven grave cards or sacrifice a blue source. A dry Coliseum may cause one pain per mana and cannot replace instant-speed interaction. The very cEDH games where the deck wins before grave threshold favor retaining Experiment. Do not call the substitution approved or a demonstrated improvement.

**Other genuinely arguable slots; no autopilot cuts:**
- `Big Score`: four-mana front-load and additional discard can be clunky, but at Goblin-discounted {1}{R} the two Treasure outputs can effectively refund its cast while its draw/discard advances graveyard throughput. A crucial commander engine card, *not* a trivial high-MV cut.
- `City of Traitors`: turbo acceleration and early back-face access can be decisive despite land-sacrifice fragility. A direct replacement with Coliseum would preserve 23 land count but reduce burst mana, so test separately if considered; it is not the chosen cut.
- `Demonic Counsel`: conditional full tutoring with delirium, cheap fail-state card selection otherwise; graveyard filling may activate delirium but not guaranteed in the same windows. Cutting tutor density has opportunity costs.
- `Ghostly Pilferer` / `Psychic Frog`: small creatures are removable and mana-consuming; both are important discarding/selection outlets, and Frog can turn Goblin discounts into real chains. Judge board/hand/counterplay, not only generic card tiers.
- `Will of the Jeskai`, `Yawgmoth's Will`: may clash with or supplement Goblin/Breach alternative costs and zone-exile replacement; precisely separate these text-driven cases before cutting.
- `Mystic Remora` and other reactive tax engines support surviving optimized interaction rounds. One previously encountered heuristic suggestion involving it was explicitly quarantined; no decision is inferred from it.

## 4. Rules, opposing counterplay and strongest objections

1. **Modal DFC commander:** The Spider-Man rules update permits a modal double-faced permanent to transform; Green Goblin can be cast as appropriate face, and the spell/activation's costs are not interchangeable. A transformation costs {1}{U}{B}{R}, sorcery activation only, and requires the front already present; casting the back from command zone follows commander tax. Front-face combat connive can be prevented by removing it before damage.
2. **Mayhem is alternate cast cost; timing applies.** Grant only to *nonland cards in graveyard*. Cards merely milled this turn, cast via Breach/escape, or deposited by Intuition (not in deck) are not automatically 'discarded this turn'; choose one alternative cost per cast. Goblin's {2} generic reduction cannot pay colored pips or fabricate permission. `Will of the Jeskai` flashback is a different permission; do not pay flashback and mayhem simultaneously.
3. **Discard-and-recast order matters:** Wheel/Tolarian Winds/LED can empty hand, but spells are eligible only after the discard and while they remain in graveyard and satisfy card-specific timing; holding mana, the stack, and priority are real constraints. `Underworld Breach` also needs three other exiled grave cards per escape cast. `Yawgmoth's Will` creates an exile replacement for cards moving to graveyard during the turn, which can stop new discard-based mayhem setups.
4. **Opponents:** graveyard exile/hate, countering the key back-face creature, removing/bouncing it after discard, exiling pieces, taxing cheap spells, attacking life totals during Ad Naus, stripping win responses, or protecting their own draw from Oracle/Consult can invalidate casual 'infinite' shorthand. `Thassa's Oracle` resolves with a trigger and players get response windows; `Final Fortune` has its lose-game delayed trigger if a win doesn't occur.
5. **Cephalid Coliseum:** threshold is a seven-card graveyard activation condition, not automatic draw/discard; must have {U} available and untapped land, pay sacrifice, choose legal target player. Opponents can respond to the ability and can exile graveyard cards before/after discard, so do not impute guaranteed chains or exact outcomes.
