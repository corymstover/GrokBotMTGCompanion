## Why this commander naturally reaches the cEDH bracket

[Inalla, Archmage Ritualist](https://mtg.wtf/card/c17/38/Inalla-Archmage-Ritualist) is **{2}{U}{B}{R}**, 4/5 legendary Human Wizard. **Eminence**: whenever another **nontoken** Wizard you control enters, if Inalla is **in command zone or battlefield**, you may pay `{1}` as the trigger resolves to create a token copy with haste, exiled at the beginning of the next end step. Copy tokens have their own ETB triggers but **do not retrigger Eminence**. The trigger occurs from the command zone; Inalla need not be cast for the main combo. Her separate `Tap five untapped Wizards you control: Target player loses 7 life` **can only be activated while Inalla is on battlefield**, but summoning sickness of Wizards tapped to pay that *other object's cost* doesn't stop it. The `tap five` route is an on-board finish if many Wizard tokens exist, not an ability emanating from the command zone.

For a nontoken Wizard ETB that has its own triggered ability, its controller can order that trigger and Eminence on stack. A later sacrificed Wizard may still be copied for `{1}` using last-known copiable values — official 2017 Wizards rulings — *provided Inalla remains in the same qualifying zone object through resolution*. Moving Inalla from command zone to battlefield or the reverse while her Eminence trigger is waiting can cause that particular trigger to do nothing even though she appears in a qualifying zone again. Legend rule applies after a legendary Wizard token enters: a second Kefka legend can generate its own appropriate ETB triggers before one copy is removed, but you don't retain two legendary permanents with the same name without other effects.

**Natural ceiling: Bracket 5 — cEDH, provisional.** Unlike slower Grixis commanders, Inalla supplies a zero-cast-cost command-zone Wizard-copy engine and can turn a resolved **Spellseeker** into recursive tutors and mana. The 27-land, fast-rock/ritual, high-tutor, free/cheap-counter profile fits that native speed. This is not a license to fill every deck with the same Oracle/Consultation combo: Oracle/Consult, Tainted Pact and Breach/Brain Freeze here are backup routes that may matter through Wizard-engine interruption. The main strategy remains native **Spellseeker/Scolar-of-the-Ages/Bloodline Necromancer/Ruthless Technomancer** recursion and Eminence resource generation.

**Native Wizard density and each meaningful trigger:** Spellseeker (2U Wizard) searches **one** instant/sorcery **mana value <=2** on its own ETB, then the paid Inalla copy enters and fetches a *second* if available. Finale of Promise (XRR) has **mana value 2 in library with X=0**, so Spellseeker can fetch it; casting it for X1 costs **{1}{R}{R}** and casts a legal instant/sorcery pair from grave each with actual MV≤1. Bloodline Necromancer is a Wizard and can return a Wizard from graveyard; copied ETB recursion is powerful but if the same object changes zones pending abilities, target legality and sacrifice ordering matter. Nontoken Technomancer is an Eminence source; its own token can trigger Treasure creation but not more Eminence. Thundertrap Trainer is an Otter Wizard ETB top-four find noncreature/nonland spell and optional offspring, but its *token offspring* isn't another Eminence trigger. Kefka, Court Mage is legendary Human Wizard: copying gives extra ETB hand/discard/card-type payoffs but the legend rule removes one, no lasting two-Kefka board. Vedalken Aethermage is a Wizardcycling Wizard tutor for spell creatures; Wizardcycling is not a Wizard ETB itself. Apprentice Necromancer is a Wizard graveyard reanimation body and can exploit Inalla copies but costs tap, one black, its own sacrifice and end-step delayed sacrifice. ## Rules-critical, finite Spellseeker chain (not a fabricated win claim)

**Condition:** Inalla remains in command zone, initial Spellseeker can be paid `{2}{U}`, and at least one generic mana is available when each Eminence trigger resolves; have the relevant card still in library/GY and black/red mana for actual spells. Opponents must not disrupt the stack/GY or block costs. A public technical cEDH Spellseeker snippet was encountered through neutral card research; the following checks its *initial paid/stack segment* against current Oracle, not a full independently replayed lethal proof.

1. Spellseeker enters as **nontoken** Wizard. Stack Inalla Eminence BELOW Spellseeker original ETB, so original tutor resolves first and finds `Culling the Weak` ({B}). **Before Eminence resolves**, cast Culling by paying B and sacrificing that original Spellseeker; add BBBB. This is legal while the Eminence trigger remains on the stack, and official Inalla ruling permits copying a source that already left using last-known values.
2. Pay `{1}` as Eminence resolves from the available mana to make a token Spellseeker with haste. The token ETB searches `Unearth` ({B}) and does NOT trigger a second Eminence because it's a token. Cast Unearth B on original Spellseeker in grave; it enters as a *new nontoken* Wizard with its own Spellseeker ETB and a new Eminence trigger. The token from step1 persists until the next end step unless consumed.
3. Set the ETB/tap ordering similarly: original returns another cheap tutor such as `Burnt Offering` ({B}), and the next copied Spellseeker can find `Finale of Promise` (library MV2 at X0). Burnt Offering can sacrifice the **3MV original Spellseeker** for three mana in B/R colors, placing it back into graveyard for further Unearth/reanimation; Finale X1 costs `{1}{R}{R}` and can target Culling the Weak (instant MV1) plus Unearth (sorcery MV1), casting both from grave without paying their mana costs if targets remain legal and Culling's sacrifice additional cost is actually paid.
4. Subsequent Scholar of the Ages/Entomb/Shallow Grave/recursion segments have multiple target, stack, graveyard and mana constraints; they have **not** been independently executed here to an actual deterministic table-wide win and must be explicitly traced in targeted combo testing. Opponents may exile Spellseeker from grave before Unearth resolves, counter the crucial ritual/reanimation, interact with cost payment or stop trigger copying; a protected Spellseeker first cast does not imply uninterruptible combo.

**Hard rules blockers to avoid overstating:** Eminence copies **nontoken** wizard ETB; token copies do not make subsequent Eminence triggers. `Culling the Weak` requires a *creature sacrifice as additional cost*, so it can't be re-cast without a creature. A card with mana value3 (`Saw in Half`) cannot be fetched by Spellseeker, regardless of it being powerful with Wizard ETB. A spell with X in hand/library has X=0 for mana value but when cast for X1 its actual cost XRR applies. Moving Inalla from command zone to battlefield before queued Eminence resolves can negate that trigger even though both zones are ordinarily qualifying. Inalla's tap-five-Wizards ability cannot be activated from command zone; creature tokens tapped as its costs need not have been controlled from turn start, per Inalla official rulings.

## One real challenger, competing cuts and the strongest KEEP response

**Neutral first-look candidate: `+Dualcaster Mage / -Kefka, Court Mage // Kefka, Ruler of Ruin`.** Dualcaster Mage `{1}{R}{R}`, Human Wizard with flash, copies **target instant/sorcery spell on stack** as ETB resolves (may change targets), and the *paid Inalla token copy* can copy a second appropriate stack instant/sorcery. This is unusually commander-specific protection/tempo and can duplicate an ally tutor/ritual or opposing removal/counterspell in a live stack. Its frontloaded 3MV can matter more than Kefka's 5MV ETB/attack value in racing cEDH, and remains a Wizard Eminence card unlike generic goodstuff. But the source list heavily prioritizes **black and blue** color for cEDH; double red **RR** in a 27-land fast-rock mana base is a material bottleneck, and any copy needs a suitable instant/sorcery **still on stack**. Kefka is a Wizard too, with strong original+token ETB resource swings, though the legendary-token rule limits persistence. Swapping may weaken rebuild/wheel plan; it is **NOT approved**.

**Alternative cuts/challenges:** `-Hexing Squelcher` makes both a protection-role swap and a non-Wizard→Wizard density gain, but removes a creature granting **uncounterable spells and life-payment ward** to the combo board — often more crucial than one new copy spell. `-Hoarding Broodlord` cuts an expensive non-Wizard but removes a tutor/convoke backup that can recover after Spellseeker graveyard hate. `+Snap / -Kefka` supplies a Spellseeker-fetchable cheap instant to reuse the Wizard and untap two lands, but 27 land count and reliance on two available lands may make it a false mana-saver; bounce also exposes Spellseeker to counterplay on replay. `+Defense Grid` protects own-turn spells at cost of also taxing our reactive interaction during others' turns, and is less commander-specific than a copied Wizard. Do not replace a clean 1-mana counter, a Culling/Burnt ritual or critical tutor with casual draw/control options without a paid stack trace.

**Strongest counterargument for KEEP:** This exact approved 100 has already centered Spellseeker recursion, 27-land/fast mana, on-board Wizard engines and free counters. An extra 3MV copy Wizard with **RR** may be stranded in hands that can cast a 2U Spellseeker and B-based recursive chain. Five-mana Kefka is high-ceiling when cheated onto battlefield or copied to disrupt *all opponents* and regain cards; Dualcaster on an empty stack is just an underwhelming creature. Protection from Hexing Squelcher is independent of a stack containing a copyable spell. An unproved challenger could dilute the tested native one-card line rather than improve it.
