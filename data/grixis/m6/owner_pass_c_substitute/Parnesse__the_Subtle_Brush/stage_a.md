## Commander identity and realistic ceiling

Parnesse is a **{2}{U}{B}{R} 4/4 Vampire Wizard**, 5 mana. She (1) makes an opponent targeting *you or a permanent you control* with a spell/ability face a triggered **pay 4 life or the targeted spell/ability is countered** choice; (2) when **you copy a spell**, you may choose **up to one target opponent** who is then allowed (not required) to copy that spell and choose new targets for *their* copy. Her second trigger is **political distribution, not an extra copy for you**. She does not inherently copy your spells, and opponents are allowed to decline. It applies to spell copies (including permanent spells when rules permit copying), not mere copies of a nonspell activated/triggered ability. Parnesse's protection is a 4-life *tax*, not hexproof or a guarantee against an opponent able to pay; untargeted board wipes bypass it.

The exact list is a coherent **copy-first Grixis political spellslinger**, not an unrelated Breach/Oracle package. Copy producers: Galvanic Iteration, Increasing Vengeance, Reiterate, Reverberate, Narset's Reversal, Return the Favor, Chef's Kiss, Dualcaster Mage, Errant Street Artist, Alania, Cormela's spell synergy, Double Vision, Bonus Round, Tempt with Mayhem, Replication Technique, Pyromancer's Goggles, Twinning Staff, transformed Primal Wellspring and Sunken Palace. Larger engines include Thousand-Year Storm and Prismari, the Inspiration giving instants/sorceries storm. Copy payoffs: Professor Onyx and Ral Storm Conduit life/damage, Archmage Emeritus draw, Storm-Kiln Artist Treasures, Sedgemoor Witch Pest production, Veyran and Harmonic Prodigy/Staff trigger multiplication. Win lines include Grapeshot storm, magecraft drain, and conditional ritual/copy loops. The political cost is not flavor-free: giving opponents optional copies may help an enemy more than the Parnesse player, requiring table/game-specific evaluation. Provisional natural ceiling is **optimized Bracket 4**, with high explosive peaks but no basis here to claim reproducible cEDH performance.

## Exact structural mana and velocity critique

**35 lands**, seven basic copies, eight fetchlands and many fetchable UBR typed duals, rainbow sources and painlands supply a plausible early UBR base. Sunken Palace enters tapped and generally supplies U; its powerful mana-paid copying ability requires `{1}{U}`, tapping it, and **exiling seven cards from your graveyard**. It is not a free spell-copy land on early turns. The conventional cheap rocks are Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Fellwar Stone, Izzet/Rakdos Signets and the three Talismans (**eight**), with Dark Ritual, Jeska's Will, Mana Geyser, Big Score and Storm-Kiln Artist smoothing large turns. The main commander costs **five** mana before tax, and Ral/Onyx/TYS/Goggles/Prismari concentrate value at 4–7 mana. Six or more mana sources do not imply turn-five access to *both* commander and a counterspell/copy engine. Treat tempo and colored-source consistency as **unmeasured**, not falsely simulated.

Protection matters because Parnesse's 4-life tax is a soft deterrent at 40-life tables and does not stop untargeted sweepers. Hexing Squelcher makes spells you control uncounterable and shields other creatures with ward—pay 2 life; it cannot make creatures indestructible. An Offer, Counterspell, Mana Drain, Deflecting Swat, Chaos Warp, Feed the Swarm, Blasphemous Act and spot recursion are not interchangeable forms of protection. A turn spent casting Parnesse while opponents hold removal can be lost without enough mana for the next spell-turn; by contrast, engines such as Alania, Twinning Staff or Thousand-Year Storm can still produce value without her. The deck's high natural ceiling does not make her mandatory for every line, a healthy source of resilience but a potential commander-identity dilution point if optimization goes too far.

## Verified copy/cast/stack traces and adversarial interactions

1. **Source Oracle for commander**: https://mtg.wtf/card/ncc/106/Parnesse-the-Subtle-Brush. Parnesse's optional opponent-copy ability triggers when **you create a copy** of a spell. A copy generally enters the stack **not cast**, so it causes Magecraft/Parnesse `copy` triggers but does **not** add to later `number of spells cast this turn` for Grapeshot/Prismari/Thousand-Year Storm. The original cast may add one to storm count; copies do not recursively storm. Verify exact delayed `when you next cast` ordering before declaring a repeatable loop.
2. **Opponent share isn't free for you**: if your Reverberate copies an instant, Parnesse can offer one target opponent a separate copy. Your copy still exists if they decline. If they accept and point their copy at you or your permanents, her first defensive trigger may force **them** to pay 4 life per applicable targeting event or their copy is countered. Giving them a non-targeting draw/ramp spell or a damaging spell aimed at another player cannot be neutralized by that protection. No automatic forced opponent copy or ownership conversion.
3. **Trigger multipliers**: Harmonic Prodigy doubles triggered abilities of your Wizard Parnesse, and Wizard's Staff attached to Parnesse (a **Wizard**, hence its `equip Wizard {1}` is available) adds another instance. For a single qualifying trigger with both, a three-instance outcome is the starting model, not an unquestioned fourfold loop. On target-tax trigger this makes paying 4 life potentially required for each instance while the spell is still on stack; on copy-sharing trigger it can offer additional opponent copies, which may be a drawback. Veyran's condition is different: it increases a trigger caused by **your casting or copying an instant/sorcery**, not every Parnesse defensive trigger when an opponent casts something. Wizard's Staff's current ruling/Oracle: https://mtg.wtf/card/hob/59/Wizards-Staff.
4. **Prismari, the Inspiration** is a seven-mana creature that grants storm to instant/sorcery spells you **cast**. Grapeshot has its own storm, so can have multiple *separate* storm triggered instances, each based on spells cast prior; copies generated by storm aren't themselves cast and don't form unbounded geometric growth. Source current Oracle/rulings: https://mtg.wtf/card/sos/212/Prismari-the-Inspiration.
5. **Bonus Round** provides spell copying to **every player**, not just you; Parnesse's pay-life targeting tax does not protect other opponents or prevent non-targeted effects. `Tempt with Mayhem` likewise lets opponents copy before producing your original + accepted-number of copies: https://mtg.wtf/card/m3c/114/Tempt-with-Mayhem. `Replication Technique`'s demonstrate also grants an opponent a copy if you choose its additional own copy. These are deliberate political concessions, not solely individual card advantage.
6. **Sunken Palace** can copy an **ability** if its produced mana pays to activate one, or a **spell** if spent on casting one; only the spell-copy side triggers Parnesse. Graveyard exile seven is a substantial opportunity cost in a Kess/Mizzix's Mastery recursion shell. https://mtg.wtf/card/m3c/81/Sunken-Palace.
7. **Conditional Reiterate/Mana Geyser loop, not a free assumed combo**: original Geyser {3}{R}{R} yields red equal to opponents' **tapped** lands; Reiterate {1}{R}{R} with buyback {3} costs six red-equivalent per repeat. Once a copy of Geyser resolves for **at least seven red** while the original Geyser remains on stack, each buyback Reiterate cast targeting that original can produce a Geyser copy, return to hand on resolving with buyback, and net at least one red after paying six, subject to priority/interaction. However **the first six-mana buyback cast must itself be funded before any copy resolves** unless another copy effect (e.g., Reverberate or properly funded Goggles) seeds red first. This is not a turn-five automatic infinite or proof of a win without magecraft/grapeshot payoff. Red restrictions, opponent taps and protective responses matter. Reiterate Oracle https://mtg.wtf/card/tsr/182/Reiterate.
8. **Costs/answers**: countering the *original* spell doesn't necessarily counter copies already created on the stack, but removing an engine before its trigger occurs prevents future copies. Target legality applies anew when changing copy targets. If Hexing Squelcher stops your spells from being countered, returning spells to hand via Krark loss still works because returning isn't countering. Extra spell copies can produce treasure and draw but copying a tutor or ritual doesn't automatically pay extra buyback costs on a copied spell.

## Weakest conditional slots and plausible challenger competition

**Primary candidate: `+Krark, the Thumbless / -Pyromancer's Goggles`.** Krark is a two-mana red Goblin **Wizard**, triggering on each *cast* instant/sorcery: a won coin flip copies it (thus triggers Parnesse and magecraft); a lost flip returns original spell to hand and can cost tempo. Harmonic Prodigy makes Krark's Wizard trigger occur twice, which can yield both bounce and copies from a single cast; outcomes are not guaranteed and coin results are not modeled. Krark is explicitly copy-first rather than generic Grixis goodstuff, and early deployment could beat a 5-mana Goggles on efficiency. **Strongest counterargument:** Pyromancer's Goggles is a reliable {5} rock that converts red mana spent on a **red** instant/sorcery into a deterministic copy (not merely when casting any color spell), and can seed Mana Geyser/Reiterate rituals. It survives creature sweeps that kill Krark and does not randomly return key counters/removal to hand in response windows. Stage A cannot determine a better reliability-adjusted ceiling without targeted sequence comparisons. An alternative possible cut is **Double Vision** (five-mana enchantment copying first instant/sorcery cast **each turn**, not every cast) but it generates predictable value across multiple players' turns and is more sweeper resilient than Krark.

**Secondary hypothesis: `+Fury Storm / -Big Score`**, if recast-counted commander access and delayed copy burst outperform the two Treasures + two cards. This trades a concrete, instant-speed discard/draw/treasure fix that funds five-plus-mana copy turns for a four-mana situational copy spell; it may be strictly worse in hands without extra red or commander casts. Lower confidence. Alternately, replacing **Bonus Round** with more asymmetric copy protection would lower opponent explosion risk, but Bonus Round is both cheaper and on-theme; no clean stronger card/cut presently established.

**Do not label as weak without source-paid comparisons:** Primal Amulet grows into a copy-land after four instant/sorcery casts while reducing spells in the meantime; Sunken Palace is a slow but color-producing copy source; Wizard's Staff/Harmonic Prodigy amplify both protection and political sharing, not just generic trigger count. The flagship Prismari seven-drop raises storm-copy ceiling but strains the curve. Storm-Kiln Artist and Cormela are important funding pieces even when they don't copy by themselves. Ordinary cheap cantrips Brainstorm/Ponder/Preordain stabilize spell chains, and the existing counter suite gives live answers before engines resolve.

## Opponent counterplay and test scenarios

- **Early setup**: compare T2 Krark, T3 cheap interaction/cantrip and no Parnesse yet against T5 Goggles with red spell and Parnesse on/off board. Record coin outcomes or random-seed distribution if later testing is authorized; do not claim a fixed success rate as performance.
- **Key counterspell held**: Krark's lost flip returns an attempted Counterspell/Chaos Warp to hand (or after independent multiple trigger windows), which may be tactically disastrous despite retaining card. Goggles guarantees copy only for **red** instant/sorcery when its red mana pays; it doesn't duplicate blue Counterspell.
- **Political choice**: Parnesse offers opponent a draw/burn copy; they can accept/decline and retarget; target tax only shields your objects. Evaluate an ally who accepts a copy beneficial to them vs an enemy who turns a copy on another player. The player making those choices matters.
- **Late Mana Geyser**: count opponents' currently tapped lands, initial seed mana for first copy and Reiterate buyback, red net per loop, ability to respond to original, and a genuine win conversion. No infinite line accepted from card labels alone.
- **High storm**: Prismari/Thousand-Year Storm/Grapeshot copy counts depend on prior **casts** rather than already copied spells. Opponent interruption of source and payoffs changes conversion. Even infinite red doesn't win automatically without outlet.
- **Five-mana commander denial**: Parnesse can be countered, removed through a paid 4-life tax, or swept by untargeted effects; count noncommander engines and how many copy effects still work. Also evaluate Harmonic Prodigy & Staff causing extra **opponent** copies, not assumed net benefit.
