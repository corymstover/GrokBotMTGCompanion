## Commander-first game plan, natural ceiling and engine dependencies

Edward combines **(1) Pirate/Assassin crews and tapped bodies making end-step Treasures** with **(2) Vehicles dealing actual player combat damage to bank opponent-owned cards indefinitely**. This is not merely Pirate tribal or generic Grixis treasure combo; the deck is strongest where one combat setup simultaneously taps typed crewmates, launches Vehicles, accrues treasure and gains foreign-card access. `Luxurious Locomotive`, `Adrestia`, `Deluxe Dragster`, `Hoard Hauler`, `Imposter Mech`, `Subterranean Schooner`, `The Belligerent`, `The Indomitable`, `Jackdaw`, `Nautiloid Ship`, `Skysovereign`, `Smuggler's Copter`, `RMS Titanic` and the other Vehicles are both combat threats and types counted by Edward **only when tapped**. Mere crewing taps the **crewing creatures**, not the Vehicle; to count the Vehicle itself, it usually must attack (or be tapped for another actual ability/effect).

Pirates/Assassins `Adéwalé`, `Admiral Brass`, `Basim`, both Breeches, Captain Lannery Storm, Captain Storm, `Don Andres`, `Fearless Swashbuckler`, `Gemcutter Buccaneer`, `Malcolm`, `Mary Read and Anne Bonny`, `Port Razer`, `Ramirez DePietro`, `Siren Stormtamer` and the commander retain flavor and attack/count/treasure synergies. `Roaming Throne` naming **Pirate** or **Assassin** doubles Edward's triggered abilities and itself gains that chosen type, potentially becoming a tapped end-step-countable creature; it does not double *every Vehicle's* own attack-trigger unless the Vehicle independently meets the chosen creature-type requirement when it triggers. `Don Andres` boosts **opponent-owned creatures we control**, not our owned Pirate bodies merely because our Vehicle exiles an opponent card; Edward's second ability is permission to **play** a card, and a foreign creature we successfully cast can later receive Don buffs.

The meaningful natural ceiling is **Bracket 4 — Optimized**: five-mana three-pip commander with explosive fast mana/treasure/artifact synergies and repeatable opponent-card theft, but combat, crew availability, creature survival, enemy blockers and multi-piece artifact engines prevent treating it as automatically deterministic Bracket 5. `Academy Manufactor`, `Fateful Discovery`, `Time Sieve`, `Coastal Piracy`, `Black Market Connections`, `Basim`, Vehicle attack triggers and tutors can replenish resources. `Chrome Mox`, `Mox Diamond` and conditional `Mox Opal` support early commander but spend cards or depend on artifact metalcraft. No simulated turn curve/win percentages are asserted.

## Critical rules and counterplay

1. **One-per-tapped-permanent count:** a tapped `Edward Kenway` is a Human **Assassin Pirate** yet makes **one**, not two, Treasures from his own qualifying status when the end-step trigger resolves. Untapped Assassins/Pirates or Vehicles that merely were crewed but never became tapped are not counted. Tapped qualifying *noncreature* Vehicles still count, and tapping a creature to crew another Vehicle makes that creature eligible if it is Pirate/Assassin. A summoning-sick creature **can** tap as part of paying a Vehicle's crew ability; crew is the Vehicle's activated ability and not its own tap ability. A creature already tapped cannot be tapped again to crew.
2. **Crew overpay and the Locomotive:** for crew N, the controller may tap more untapped creatures than the minimum total power needed; `Luxurious Locomotive` crew1 is **activate only once each turn** but can use multiple creatures as that one payment. Its attack trigger makes one Treasure for each creature that crewed it **this turn**, including ones no longer present at resolution. If tapped crewers are Assassin/Pirate, Edward also counts them separately at end step; the Locomotive must itself be tapped to qualify as a Vehicle in the end-step count. Thus one paid crew can yield immediate attacking Treasure and later commander Treasure without imaginary doubled counting.
3. **Vehicle combat card theft:** Edward triggers on Vehicle **combat damage to player**, not attacks alone, player damage from an attacking Pirate, noncombat vehicle damage, or combat damage dealt only to a planeswalker. Player whose library is sampled is the **one actually dealt damage**. If multiple Vehicles hit, each triggers. A face-down card exiled this way has ongoing permission; normal costs and timing must be paid, including land-play restriction, unless other rules allow otherwise. Edward does **not** automatically produce new Treasure on every Vehicle combat hit; first ability waits until our end step and tests tapped types then.
4. **Token engine:** `Academy Manufactor` replaces creation of a Treasure with **a Clue, Food and Treasure**. With `Fateful Discovery` `{3}{U}{U}` enchantment active, each resulting artifact entry draws one card. `Time Sieve` `{T}, sacrifice five artifacts` takes an extra turn after current; five token artifacts may fund it. With **two qualifying tapped permanents** at each own end step and Academy Manufactor surviving, Edward's **one trigger** yields two Treasure plus two Food plus two Clues = six sacrifice-able artifacts; Time Sieve can spend five. This is a **conditional repeatable extra-turn engine**, not a guaranteed infinite loop: it requires Edward surviving and payable, enough tapped qualifying permanents EACH extra turn, Manufactor and untapped Sieve recurring through untaps, artifact supply/board access and opponent response windows. Without Manufactor or another token multiplier, only two Treasures fail Sieve's five-artifact cost.
5. **Treasure spending:** Tokens produced at own end step can generally be tapped/sacrificed for mana immediately despite entering that turn because they are **noncreature** artifacts; they are not summoning-sick artifact creatures. `Fateful Discovery` can generate multiple end-step draws; a hypothetical repeated Sieve/Manufactor/Fateful setup with no finish can exhaust our deck, so measurable win-condition completeness is relevant. `RMS Titanic` when dealing player combat damage is itself sacrificed and makes Treasures equal damage; later Edward counts no Titanic if it left before end step, though other tapped Pirates/Assassins still count. Opponents can remove attacker before combat damage or break the artifact engine.
6. **Counterplay:** instant removal before damage stops vehicle exile; destroy or tap-deny crewmates prevents Vehicle becoming attacker; artifact mass removal can remove Sieve/Manufactor/vehicles or artifacts even when land mana intact. Treasure-rich hands without qualified tapped permanents cannot make new Edward Treasures. Edward's own five-mana UBR access, participation in combat and lack of native ward/hexproof create exposure, partially buffered by Force of Will/Fierce Guardianship/Deadly Rollick/Deflecting Swat/Mana Drain and other cheap stack/board answers.

## Neutral challenge — would explicit token-to-kill conversion improve resilience?

**Primary candidate trial (NO owner-approved swap): ADD `Mirkwood Bats` (`{3}{B}`; Flying 2/3 Bat), CUT `Fateful Discovery` (`{3}{U}{U}` enchantment).** `Mirkwood Bats` drains **each opponent 1 life per token created or sacrificed**, directly turning Edward's end-step Treasure count and Time Sieve's five-artifact payment into a multiplayer clock even if Vehicle combat is fully blocked. In the bounded ideal board above, two qualifying tapped permanents plus Academy Manufactor produce **six tokens** (six Bats life-loss triggers) and Time Sieve sacrifices five (five more Bats triggers), for **11 life loss to each opponent per completed iteration** before considering other token effects. Repeated extra turns can allow eventual win despite opponent creature blockers (subject to real survival and stack interruptions). This is payoff for Edward's distinctive token production, not an off-commander generic win package.

`Fateful Discovery`, however, already provides the **card** payoff to those same artifact entries: six artifacts created with Manufactor = **six draws** at an end step. It digs for protection/crews/finishers/answers and works with ANY artifact entering, not just token events. Trading it for a four-mana non-Pirate/non-Assassin/non-Vehicle 2/3 that neither crews as a Pirate nor advances Edward's count may badly erode hand consistency and tribal/Vehicle identity. `Fateful Discovery` could make a prospective token engine actually robust by finding pieces; Bats may be win-more after already assembled expensive Sieve+Manufactor+Edward. Against opponents with artifact removal or no Time Sieve, Discovery's standalone card influx may be much more valuable than a modest token-drain. Most existing Vehicles and Treasure payoffs already offer creature combat victory. This is a genuine tradeoff, not a recommendation.

**Alternative cut to investigate only after the above:** `Adrestia` is a **three-mana Vehicle** with Crew1 and an attack-draw conditioned on an **Assassin having crewed** it that turn; replacing it with Bats preserves Discovery but sacrifices an early, on-flavor Vehicle that triggers Edward's second ability on player damage, counts when tapped and enables more low-cost attack access. Evaluate the subtype-density loss rather than assuming a generic token drain is better. Other core Vehicles with high impact, notably RMS Titanic and Luxurious Locomotive, should not be removed merely for mana value or rarity.

**Strongest KEEP counterargument:** the list already has an owner-approved powerful extra-turn Treasure engine and many ways to access cards and attack with Vehicles. `Fateful Discovery` turns the same artifact creation into a handful of interaction, while pirate/vehicle density makes Edward's unique abilities more reliable. A non-typed Bat that requires additional artifacts to finish may dilute engine consistency, even if its late-game deterministic draining line looks powerful on paper. This may be the best optimized version precisely because its main reward is combat/theft/resources rather than copied off-theme aristocrat drain.
