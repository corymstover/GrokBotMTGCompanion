## Source-bound commander-first ruling and ceiling

Be'lakor {3}{U}{B}{R} flying 6/5 Demon Noble: when he enters, draw X and lose X life where X is actual Demon count **on trigger resolution**, including Be'lakor if still present; he does NOT trigger Lord on his own entrance. Whenever another Demon enters under your control, the **entering Demon** deals power damage to any legal target, not necessarily 6 Be'lakor damage. Reanimation, Sneak Attack, cost reductions, demon-specific enchantments, mass/legend-conscious clones and protection serve this identity.

**Finite kicked self-Rite rules case**: Be'lakor already battlefield, no other Demon/no doubling effects. Pay nine total ({7}{U}{U}) kicked Rite of Replication legally targeting him; five legendary Be'lakor token Demons enter simultaneously. Original Lord sees five, each clone sees four **other** incoming clones: **5 + (5×4) = 25 Lord triggers**; entering creatures 6 power each yield up to **150 targetable damage** across those trigger objects under unchanged-power/prevention assumptions. Five new Princes also trigger. The legend rule makes only **one** same-name legendary Be'lakor survive before priority; Lord triggers exist and use entrant last-known power if it left, while five Princes compute live X only when each resolves, e.g. five total cards/five life lost if exactly one Demon survives. Removing Rite's target before resolution fizzles the Rite and prevents token creation. Opponent target-protection, damage prevention and interaction on the stack matter. This was **already correctly documented** in source reconciliation, so Stage A's provisional mechanical uncertainty is resolved through existing evidence, not new measured simulation.

Spark Double copies Be'lakor without legendary status and has a +1/+1 counter, allowing a second persistent Lord; kicked Rite token clones do *not* leave five different Demon names for Liliana's Contract. Molten Echoes doesn't chain off its own token creations. Panharmonicon/Roaming Throne amplify ETB triggers (also Prince's life-loss risk). Whip of Erebos gives creatures lifelink so damage **dealt by Demon** can regain life, but direct Prince life loss is no damage and enchantment Warstorm Surge deals its own damage. Bloodletter of Aclazotz doubles *opponents'* life loss during your turn without doubling direct damage amount or offsetting your Be'lakor draw-life payment.

**Natural ceiling:** Bracket 4 Optimized Demon ETB/combo, strongly distinctive 25-Demon printed identity, 36 lands, colored mana, selected cheating/reanimation, flexible stack defenses, powerful but six-cost commander; not certified competitive tournament percentage. Real counters: ETB hate, targeted removal pre-Demon, Rite target protection/removal, graveyard exile, broad creature wipes and self-inflicted Prince/Vilis draw-life danger.

## Exact challenger judgment and strongest objection

`+Displacer Kitten / -Warstorm Surge` was an unranked Stage A challenge, **not owner-approved**. Kitten at 3U repeatedly blinks Demon or Be'lakor after a **noncreature spell cast** and has exciting low-mana, high-trigger potential. But it is non-Demon, requires spells and an existing Be'lakor + other Demon for immediate Lord damage; blinking Be'lakor alone gives more cards plus dangerous life loss, not Lord self-trigger. Six-mana Warstorm Surge independently converts entering creature power to damage when Be'lakor is absent and reinforces every Demon ETB if present, preserving a fallback through commander tax and interaction. Existing Ghostly Flicker, Spark Double, Reflections, Rite and Molten Echoes already offer dense entry multiplication.

Strongest counterargument: an extra cheaper repeat blink could accelerate key turn kills and offset top-heavy hands more often than redundant Surge. That is plausible but not demonstrated under paid mana, protection, noncreature spell density and real counterplay; removing commander-independent Surge is not an obviously positive exact change. The earlier Sakashima-versus-Demon question and M5 rank-based cuts also lack decision-grade net advantage. Thus **no exact owner-ready addition or cut**, and no forced same-shell optimization.
