## Commander-first agreement and newly exposed distinction

**Core agreement with prior reviewers:** Edea is `{2}{U}{B}{R}` 2/5 Human Warlock with ward2; she steals a creature **an opponent currently controls** at the **beginning of each combat on your turn**, gives haste/untaps until end turn, and when an **opponent-owned creature currently under your control actually DIES**, she draws and returns that card to the **owner's** battlefield if still present in graveyard. This generates stolen-body sacrifice resources, not unconditional permanent theft; an opponent ETB can be replayed when their original returns. The natural optimized ceiling remains Bracket 4 and **Theft / Sacrifice / Control**. Full Throttle, Karlach and copied/combat triggers strongly favor multiple thefts, while the accepted source uses 35 lands/six cheap rocks for three-color access; generic colorless Tomb/Altar mana does not pay UBR pips.

**Stage A's `Conjurer's Closet` challenger was not a wholly new discovery.** Both Oct14 closeout and the deep/reconciliation specifically evaluated the artifact and found Thassa duplication its most consequential downside. The neutral Stage A independently reached the same mechanism, but proposed a **different exact cut `Irma, Part-Time Mutant`**, whereas the original primary match was `Impostor Syndrome`, with `Industrial Advancement` a secondary alternative. It is vital to preserve this actual new alternative rather than claim newly found card technology.

**Stage A's timing emphasis needs a finer adjudication.** Official Wizards TMNT notes show `Irma` targets another creature **we control at trigger placement**, and both she and Edea are beginning-of-combat triggers. Even with Edea's theft resolving first, Irma cannot target **the same-combat future victim** in a trigger already on stack. This is a valid opponent-control targeting gate, rather than a simple "copy Edea for extra immediate steal". Yet the old original decisions correctly defended Irma on a **different, legal line**: she copies Edea (or another owned value creature) in the first combat, her copied Edea beginning-of-combat ability exists in time for the **next combat or next turn**, and extra combats from Full Throttle/Karlach can yield additional theft triggers. Copying an already-held foreign creature or a precombat-main borrowed creature is also legal; copying a token may give enduring token-identity benefits. Hence the rules clarification **does not itself prove Irma is weak or invalid**. It narrows a hypothetical shortcut the approved author did not necessarily rely on.

**Closet versus specific cuts now has three distinct opportunity costs**:

1. **`-Irma` (Stage A):** Closet at `{5}` and end-step delayed converts one surviving temporary stolen creature into permanent controlled object without dealing combat damage; Irma `{2}{U}` provides recurring *combat trigger/copy potential* and cannot same-combat target the newly stolen body. Under extra combats, the retained Irma copy may create theft/draw greater than any single Closet blink. This pair has **no measured exact-source head-to-head** and is genuinely different from the prior preferred paired test.
2. **`-Impostor Syndrome` (old primary):** Closet costs five vs six, needs no combat damage to retain an opposing creature, even when blocked. Impostor's combat damage creates **our owned nonlegendary token copy** of a nontoken creature that hit, sometimes many tokens if multiple attackers connect, giving a higher wide-board ceiling and ETBs even though own copies cannot trigger Edea's "don't own" death draw. Closet makes one lasting **opponent-owned** creature directly that can still later yield Edea draw on death. Existing Thassa covers this retention already; nobody proved which outcome is better in actual pods.
3. **`-Industrial Advancement` (old secondary):** Industrial at beginning own end step turns temporary creature sacrifice into Edea draw plus a **higher-MV own-deck creature deployment**; it can work even when opponent's body does not connect in combat and converts into our permanent creature value. Cutting it for Closet turns repeatable sacrifice/tutoring into blink, a distinctly different game plan, not straightforward optimization.

**Additional prior hypothesis** `+Combat Celebrant/-Grim Haruspex` remains weaker-evidenced, not a Stage A priority: Celebrant's exerted attack creates another combat and Edea steal, but requires a ready creature and attack; Haruspex draws from actual **nontoken creature** deaths independently of opponent creature ownership. Full Throttle and Karlach already create multiple combat windows. No owner request for such replacement.

## Finite source-paid scenarios, not a Phase B experiment

- **A — Irma target at same combat:** Our Edea and Irma start combat, opponent controls A, we also control our B. At trigger placement Edea targets A; Irma may target B or Edea, **not A**. Edea steal can resolve first, but Irma's legal target B remains her target and she copies B after resolution. Choosing A for Irma is illegal upfront. If Irma copies Edea, her newly gained theft ability **doesn't retrospectively trigger** on the combat start already passed; it may trigger at a future combat if her copy state persists.
- **B — extra combat upside for KEEP:** Precombat main cast Full Throttle, Edea and Irma ready, 3 enemy creatures accessible. At combat 1 Irma copies Edea; at a later extra combat Irma's new Edea theft ability triggers in addition to original Edea (subject to controller/copy state/ability staying on Irma), enabling more targets and potential borrowed sacrifice/draw. Closet inserted instead has zero during-combat theft trigger; only one end-step blink if installed/alive. Any removed/copied creature/target may change legality, so this isn't a guaranteed three or four borrowed creatures.
- **C — blocked steal to Closet:** Edea steals opponent-owned 6/6, attacks, gets blocked and survives, still under our control at beginning own end step. Existing Thassa **or** proposed Closet can target, exile then return under **our** control, resetting old until-EOT effect. No dies trigger/draw from that blink. Without Thassa/Closet, EOT cleanup returns control to the original opponent. Impostor Syndrome yields **no** token if creature didn't deal player combat damage; Industrial might sacrifice it at end step instead, then Edea draws/owner gets returned body and Industrial recruits a larger own creature.
- **D — death ordering:** Edea takes A, we sacrifice it to Ashnod's Altar, add `{C}{C}`, trigger one Edea death draw and its owner return (if still in grave). If `Chaos Shrine's Black Crystal` or `Nightmare Shepherd` legally exiles original card before Edea's death ability resolves, the return can fail while the card draw still happens, with other payoff per source. No permanent theft from merely dying. An opponent with no creatures gives Edea no legal combat target.
- **E — two Edea ability sources plus Throne:** Original Edea and nonlegendary Spark Double Edea are both Human Warlock, Roaming Throne named Warlock. Sac borrowed A: each source's dies ability triggers an extra time = **four** resolving draw instructions from four abilities if none countered, but at most first eligible resolving ability can actually return *same A* to its owner; the subsequent three still draw if allowed to resolve. No extra death events or infinite draw from the returns.

These are manual rules-dependent test cases, **not stochastic games, measured turn-five colored-pip distributions, or opponent AI outcomes**. Stage A correctly flagged narrow Irma targeting, but no measured performance demonstrates Closet beats Irma, Impostor or Industrial. Previous comprehensive manuscript examined 114/12 candidate pairs; do not rerun broad analysis simply to claim new measurements.
