## Commander-first identity and realistic power ceiling

Edea is **combat-window theft, sacrifice, and recursive card advantage**, not generic theft/copy goodstuff. Her characteristic line is (i) steal an opponent-owned creature untapped and hasty at beginning of combat, (ii) optionally attack or use the body for another resource, (iii) sacrifice it to an independent outlet **while we still control it**, (iv) trigger Edea to **draw** and put that card back onto its **owner's battlefield**. The return may **benefit** the opponent with a fresh enters trigger or reset their creature; it denies permanent loss of the creature, so repeat theft or a permanent theft conversion is strategically important. The returned card is a **new battlefield object** and isn't still under our temporary control.

The current 100 makes this identity unmistakable:

- **Cheap sacrifice outlets with varied payoffs**: `Viscera Seer` (free scry), `Goblin Bombardment` (damage), `Ashnod's Altar` (colorless mana), `Phyrexian Altar` (colored mana), `Warren Soultrader` (Treasure at another resource cost), `High Market` and `Phyrexian Tower` (lands/mana), plus `Corrupted Conviction`, `Village Rites`, `Deadly Dispute`, `Diabolic Intent`, `Saw in Half` and larger `Industrial Advancement` / `Kethek` engines. Edea's card draw triggers on the original **opponent-owned** creature dying even though it returns to their side; our own sacrificed creatures can fuel outlets but do **not** trigger that special draw.
- **Additional theft and full-board turns**: `Captivating Crew`, `Dominus of Fealty`, `Zara, Renegade Recruiter`, `Seize the Spotlight`, `Mass Mutiny`, `Mob Rule`, `Insurrection`, and `Don Andres, the Renegade` capitalize on controlling genuine opponent-owned creatures. `Insurrection` takes all creatures and makes an enormous sacrifice/attack window, while `Mob Rule` selectively takes either power4+ or power≤3, never both groups on one resolution. A stolen creature is still owned by the opponent regardless of who controls it.
- **Multipliers and theft retention**: `Roaming Throne` named **Human** or **Warlock** can cause Edea's beginning-of-combat theft trigger AND her nonowned-death trigger to trigger additional times, not convert opponent ownership; `Strionic Resonator` and `Gogo, Master of Mimicry` copy stack abilities for mana/tap; `Spark Double` and `Sakashima of a Thousand Faces` can give another Edea version without ordinary legend sacrifice. `Thassa, Deep-Dwelling` at **beginning of end step** can exile a currently temporarily stolen creature we control and return the card **under our control** as a *new object*, so we now keep it beyond end of turn, though no dies/draw trigger occurs from this blink. Extra combats via `Karlach, Fury of Avernus` and `Full Throttle` create new Edea beginning-of-combat triggers on **your turn**; they do not conjure new targets if enemy creature boards are empty.
- **Punishment and recursion**: Grave Pact/Mayhem Devil/Pitiless Plunderer, Grim Haruspex/Skullclamp/Braids, Rakdos, the Muscle, Nightmare Shepherd, Victimize/Reanimate and creature-to-artifact mana conversion can yield value from bodies. Note an opponent-owned stolen creature that dies returns to **that opponent**, so their creature's ETB may repeat; `Nightmare Shepherd` can potentially exile a dying non-token card before an Edea return and still leave Edea's draw to resolve, but precise same-event trigger order and whether it is already moved must be source-checked in any proposed loop.

This is plausibly **Bracket 4 — Optimized**: repeatable theft without an extra card each turn, many sacrifice payoffs, multiple additional combat and stack multipliers, numerous free protective interactions; nevertheless the commander costs five with U/B/R pips, needs a live opponent creature at the combat boundary and a meaningful outlet, and ordinarily returns killed borrowed material to opponent. No cEDH ceiling or win-rate assertion is supported by a 100-card inspection alone.

## Curve, mana, opponent counterplay, failure states

**35 lands** plus early **six ordinary rocks** (Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Fellwar Stone, 3 Talismans), Ancient Tomb, Phyrexian Tower and situational Altars/Warren Soultrader are more robust on baseline quantity than 31-land five-mana peers, but Tomb/Sol Ring cannot supply the three distinct `{U}{B}{R}` pips. The expensive finishers `Insurrection` (8), `Full Throttle` (6), `Impostor Syndrome` (6), `Dominus of Fealty` (five hybrid blue/red), costly X outlets and creatures create meaningful draws where there are insufficient targets or colored sources. This is a source-based vulnerability, **not measured mulligan/color percentages**.

Protection includes ward2, Lightning Greaves, Fierce Guardianship, Deflecting Swat, Deadly Rollick, Mana Drain, Arcane Denial, Swan Song, An Offer You Can't Refuse, and removal sweeps. Ward2 only matters when an opponent's spell/ability targets Edea and pays ward; it doesn't prevent global wipes, the opponent denying targets by sacrificing their creature, or instant creature removal. An empty opposing board yields a triggered Edea theft ability with **no legal target** when put on stack, so ability cannot steal; the deck needs additional stolen-body access. Stealing a commander and sacrificing it may result in its owner using the replacement choice to put it in command zone (or zone move after death), changing what is returned; do not assert guaranteed bounce of a stolen commander.

Very important exact timing: **`Irma, Part-Time Mutant` and Edea both trigger at BEGINNING OF COMBAT on your turn**. Irma's ability targets **another creature you ALREADY CONTROL at trigger placement**. Edea's triggered theft also selects an enemy target before either resolves. It is **not legal** for Irma's same-combat triggered ability to choose the enemy creature Edea *will* steal later, because we do not control that creature yet when Irma's target must be announced. Stacking Edea's theft to resolve first cannot retroactively legalize a target Irma never could select. Irma can still copy Edea, owned synergy pieces, a previous-turn retained nonowned creature, or a creature stolen during the **precombat main phase** by another card; and in **later extra combats**, new copied abilities can matter. This tempers, but does not nullify, Irma's synergy.

## Neutral challenger: permanent-retention density (not approved)

**Primary trial:** ADD **`Conjurer's Closet`** (`{5}` colorless artifact), CUT **`Irma, Part-Time Mutant`** (`{2}{U}` three-mana Human Mutant Shapeshifter). Closet at **beginning of your end step** exiles a **target creature you control** then returns that card **under your control**. A body temporarily stolen at combat start is still controlled by Edea's controller at the beginning of their own end step, so it is a legal Closet target. After exiling and re-entering, the new object remains under *our* control indefinitely; the temporary control-changing effect on the old object is no longer relevant. This converts Edea's short-lived theft into **real long-term ownership-independent board access** even without sacrifice or a second opponent capture. Its control-retention ability duplicates the key service already offered by `Thassa, Deep-Dwelling`, potentially increasing the chance of drawing an independent theft-retention engine.

**Pro-Closet argument:** without a sac outlet when Edea steals a premium creature, it reverts to its opponent at cleanup with no extra Edea draw. Closet can permanently convert those otherwise-low-yield turns, blink a stolen ETB creature under us to gain its ETB, and preserve a critical target until next combat. It is colorless and every Edea steal could set it up; Irma's ability, often overstated as copying the same Edea-theft victim in the same trigger window, cannot do that. Edea's natural plan does want lasting consequence from repeated short steals.

**Strongest counterargument for KEEP Irma:** Closet is a five-mana **noncreature artifact** that produces nothing on cast, waits until end step, requires maintaining control until then, overlaps a **four-mana indestructible Thassa** already in the 100, and is vulnerable to artifact hate. More importantly, permanent theft via blink **does not cause a death**, so it denies the immediate Edea draw and several Grave Pact/Mayhem/Altars benefits; the original loop's payoff is sacrifice-and-draw, not always retaining opposing bodies. Irma is *two mana cheaper*, can become another Edea-like theft/draw trigger engine over subsequent combats, copies a payoff or our already-controlled borrowed creature, and grows from her own trigger; `Full Throttle` and `Karlach` increase her future copied-trigger opportunities. Her incorrect same-combat target assumption must be avoided, but she might be far more strategically efficient than a fifth-mana redundant blink. The approved deck may correctly prioritize her.

Secondary bounded question: **does the extra permanent-retention mechanism actually matter** relative to just a higher density of independent sacrifice outlets? Existing Thassa already gives permanent control, and Edea returns sacrificed cards to opponents in exchange for a draw. If typical opposing boards are hostile to retained artifacts, retaining a body may not win over sacrifice. No other speculative changes are recommended now.

## Exact mechanics and test design

1. **Same combat Irma/Edea:** With Edea, Irma and an enemy creature A in play, at beginning combat put both triggers on stack. Edea can legally target A (enemy-controlled), but Irma **cannot** target A because at placement we do not control it. If Irma targets our creature B instead and Edea resolves theft of A first, Irma copies B on resolution (if B still legal). To let Irma copy A in later combat, first use another legal theft effect during precombat main or retain A from earlier turn. No shortcut from arranging stack differently.
2. **Thassa/Closet:** Edea steals opposing A during combat. With Thassa or Closet already on battlefield, at beginning of **our** end step, target A while we still control it. If it resolves, return A under **our** control as new permanent; it was **exiled, not killed**, so Edea's dies trigger doesn't fire. Opposing instant removal can prevent blink if it removes A before the trigger resolves; protection/timing matter. At end of turn a different, not-blinked borrowed creature reverts.
3. **Death and owner return:** Edea alive, sacrifice A (opponent-owned) via Ashnod's Altar while controlling it. The sacrifice produces `{C}{C}`, Edea triggers and (if the card still in its graveyard at resolution) returns it under its owner's control and draws exactly one, before multipliers. Newly reentered A may trigger the opponent's ETB, and Edea cannot continue using it as our sacrifice resource absent fresh theft.
4. **Full Throttle:** Sorcery played during precombat main creates **two additional combats after that main phase**, with untap of attackers at each combat. Edea can trigger at the beginning of the resulting combats and select additional enemy creatures, but if she or a target is removed the corresponding triggers/casts are not guaranteed. There is no new main phase between the extra combat phases for sorcery-speed Crew activations; `Captivating Crew` can't activate in the tiny between-combat window, and another noncombat mana source cannot solve sorcery timing.
5. **Paired comparison:** Run approved SHA baseline against `+Conjurer's Closet/-Irma` using identical legal draws, enemy board types (low-creature combo, big ETB toolbox, token-wide, creature-dense), availability of Thassa and sacrifice outlets, actual number of retained stolen creatures and Edea draws, Edea survival through start-of-combat and end-step windows, time/colored pips to cast costs, and extra-combat sequence. A proper experiment must quantify how often each card is active and who benefits from returning owned creatures. **No gameplay simulation or measured win-rate performed here.**
