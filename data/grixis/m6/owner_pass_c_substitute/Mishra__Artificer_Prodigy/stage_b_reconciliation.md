## Critical rules agreement with author and reconciliation

1. **Mishra is not blank in singleton.** Cast trigger can rescue the very same physical artifact card if the controlled counter trigger sends it to graveyard *before* Mishra resolves; `Possibility Storm` can place it into library first. The GrokBot's “generic artifact goodstuff” diagnosis does not survive stack analysis. `Blood Funnel` is arguably the deck's most important identity enabler, so removing it to enable Scepter is a large protected-core loss.
2. **Scepter/Reversal works in a different deck/state, not automatically here.** Scepter imprints an MV≤2 instant from hand on ETB; its `{2},{T}` activated ability casts a *copy* of the imprinted card for no mana. Reversal untaps nonland permanents; if they make strictly more than 2 repeatable mana and the spell resolves, the loop can gain mana. But Reversal is a nonartifact instant, Mishra cannot recover its countered free copy, and activation mana does not evade Bauble/Mirror's spell-cast payment checks. The two exact swaps leave those permanent conflicts intact. Under Nether Void the spell also needs an additional {3} per copy, destroying the advertised three-mana loop. No empirical outcome was claimed. Source-independent Stage A did not suggest the Scepter pair and agrees with rejection after unblinding.
3. **Main white-space is early colored access versus long lock ceiling.** The original comprehensive report quantified U/B/R pressure and manually enumerated 29 lands; `Mishra's Workshop` makes artifact-only colorless and cannot cast Mishra. The Stage A `+Lotus Petal / -Nullstone Gargoyle` hypothesis differs from prior comparisons to `Mox Amber` (repeatable after legend) and generic utility lands. The author and reconciliation both *recognize* Gargoyle's nine-mana liability and Lotus Petal's colored turn-2/3 benefit, but neither tested that precise competing tradeoff. No documentary KEEP converts the pair to proven inferior.
4. **Gargoyle has unique restoration potential.** A nine-mana artifact creature normally sits above efficient early-game curve; however `Goblin Engineer` can place it into graveyard, `Goblin Welder` can exchange it for a controlled artifact after timing/targeting and survival requirements, and abundant colorless artifact mana can occasionally cast it. Once on field it counters the first noncreature spell every turn, and Mishra can use this to recover a countered artifact spell. That is real engine preservation and the strongest challenge to the Petal hypothesis.
5. **Petal has no continuous mana output.** It costs {0}, can add a missing colored pip for a first Mishra cast, counts as an artifact cast for Jhoira/Reservoir/Mishra triggers, can support metalcraft before sacrifice, and works as Welder/KCI material while present. It does *not* replace recurring mana and ordinarily does not provide value by itself once drawn after an established lock; sacrifice removes it from metalcraft and doesn't itself count as casting another artifact. Statements about opening frequencies would require actual matched draws and removal-aware timelines.

## Evidence-bound head-to-head examples — no simulations

**Fast-colored configuration favoring Petal:** Take `Badlands`, `Island`, `Ancient Tomb` as three successive land drops. By turn 3, they produce B/R choice, U and CC: abundant generic but only two of Mishra's three needed colors on the same turn. With zero-cost Petal available to contribute the *other* B/R color, `1UBR` becomes payable. With nine-mana Gargoyle in that same nonland slot, Mishra is stranded without a separate fixer. This demonstrates a possible *tempo mechanism*, not a measured likelihood; it has card-selection and mulligan assumptions.

**Established engine favoring Gargoyle:** Control Mishra, Goblin Welder, another artifact, plus a graveyard Gargoyle placed there earlier by Engineer. With Welder untapped and valid targets, a swap can put the otherwise nine-mana prison onto the battlefield without paying nine, threatening a first-noncreature-spell-per-turn counter and giving Mishra recovery asymmetry. Petal is only a one-time mana source in this established-board scenario. Opponents can remove Mishra/Welder, exile the graveyard target, counter an enabler or remove Gargoyle; no automatic locked win follows.

**Defense Grid alternative:** costs 2 and can shield the turn you assemble Mishra/Top/Reservoir by taxing opponent instant-speed responses by 3; but tax also hits **your** counters during opponents' turns, directly conflicting with a five-plus-counterspell responsive package. It isn't equivalent to the first-spell counter or Mishra rescue. This alternative does not independently prove Nullstone should go.
