## Commander identity and actual engine

Davros rewards opponents **losing at least 3 life individually within your turn**, then at the beginning of **your end step** the trigger can create *one* 3/3 menacing Dalek artifact token if **any** opponent reached the threshold. Each opponent independently meeting it faces a villainous choice: you draw a card or **that opponent** discards one. Life *lost*, not only combat damage, is what counts; it is not the net change in a life total. The opponent chooses, and an opponent with an empty hand may choose the no-op discard option. The effect is **one triggered ability**, not independent token-making and choice triggers. Triggering it twice makes up to two Daleks and repeats relevant choices; it does not automatically draw cards.

The deck's proper identity is **Dalek fabrication + artifact pressure + table-wide life-loss threshold + coercive draw/discard choices**, with a Doctor Who villain contingent, not Grixis control or generic fast combo for its own sake. An optimized **Bracket 4** is the natural working ceiling, pending the existing project's bracket classification; no evidence here supports converting Davros into a Bracket 5 homogenized shell.

- **Threshold sources:** `Boltwave` alone deals three to each opponent before the end step for one red mana; `Loyal Subordinate` loses three for each opponent at beginning of combat if Davros is controlled; `Dalek Drone`'s ETB removes a target creature and causes its controller to lose three; attacking Dalek/Cyberman menacing artifact boards can chip damage. `Cyberman Patrol` grants artifact creatures afflict 3 when blocked. These are real, varied enabling routes; their availability/turn sequence remains unmeasured.
- **Choices and payoff:** `The Valeyard` makes opponents face each relevant villainous choice twice (not duplicate Dalek tokens). `Waste Not`, `Geth's Grimoire`, `Tinybones, Trinket Thief`, and `Entropic Battlecruiser` reward actual discards; `Sycorax Commander`, `The Dalek Emperor`, `Cult of Skaro`, `Perforating Artist`, `Court of Ambition`, and `Doomsday Confluence` sustain coercive pressure. Opponents controlling the choices is an important failure mode: discard-dependent payoffs may fail as hands empty or as opponents opt into the other outcome.
- **Token/artifact layer:** Daleks fuel `The Dalek Emperor` affinity, artifact count for `Mox Opal`, `Tezzeret, Master of the Bridge`, and artifact-wide tools. `Cybermen Squadron` gives nonlegendary artifact creatures myriad, providing multiple artifact-attacker bodies in multiplayer; tokens enter attacking, so their *own* attack-triggered abilities do not fire. `Ashad, the Lone Cyberman` gives casualty 2 only to the **first nonlegendary artifact spell cast each turn**; it does not copy an artifact *trigger* or any future spell without paying casualty. `Auton Soldier` can provide nonlegendary artifact copies but costs six and requires existing worthwhile creatures.
- **Protection/control:** `Lightning Greaves`, `Swiftfoot Boots`, `Padeem`, `Deflecting Swat`, `Fierce Guardianship`, `Swan Song`, and removal protect time-dependent value. `Blasphemous Act`/`Toxic Deluge` may also clear the Dalek board; `Their Name Is Death` similarly has a meaningful artifact-symmetry profile. This identity permits interaction but values engines actually producing recurring Daleks.

## Independent adversarial challenger — not an approved swap

**Primary test hypothesis: ADD `Fiery Confluence`; CUT `Weeping Angel`.** `Fiery Confluence` is `2RR` sorcery, choose three modes, repeat modes allowed: 1 damage to each creature; **2 damage to each opponent**; destroy a target artifact. Two opponent-burn modes guarantee 4 loss to each opponent (barring prevention), immediately qualify their Davros choices that turn, and leave a third flexible mode; three burn modes deal 6. This is a flexible threshold enabler at the same four-mana region as the commander, and breaks dependence on persuading opponents to opt into damage. It also helps fight opposing artifact engines and token clusters.

`Weeping Angel` (`1UB`) is a 2/2 flash first-strike/vigilance artifact creature that can shuffle creatures it would damage in combat, a valuable flavorful defensive utility body. However, it **does not** itself directly cause an opponent to lose life when it is blocked; its prevention-and-shuffle clause replaces combat damage to a creature. Its temporary noncreature state on an opponent casting a creature can remove it from combat. In the current artifact and proactive threshold build it is a plausible marginal slot, **not** objectively dead. Cutting it lowers artifact density, unexpected blocker quality, and thematic representation while adding a sorcery non-artifact that does not recur. That is the strongest counterargument to the hypothesis, and only a matched comparison can resolve it.

**Alternative cuts** worth pitting against `Weeping Angel`: `Geth's Grimoire` (four mana, conditional on real opponent discards, but has potent repeat-draw ceiling) or `Perforating Artist` (three mana conditional raid/end-step tax, opponent may discard/sac instead of losing 3, but repeats and creates board/hand pressure). **Do not cut `Cyberman Patrol` casually**: 2-mana global artifact-creature afflict 3 is a commander-specific threshold engine. Likewise `Boltwave` already achieves three to every opponent for only `{R}`, so `Sizzle` at `{2}{R}` is an almost certainly inferior candidate unless a materially different role is demonstrated. `Flame Rift` would give 4 damage to each *player* for `{1}{R}`, including its caster — faster and broad, but the self-life cost is especially dangerous in a high-threat multiplayer pod. It is a secondary nonpreferred challenger, not an automatic add.

**Testing question:** Does Fiery Confluence's guaranteed across-table threshold and removal utility outperform a 3-mana flash artifact defender enough to pay the extra colored red pip, sorcery timing, loss of artifact density, and foregone board stabilization? Compare exact baseline and each individual cut under same hands, turn order, opponents' life/hand states and removal, particularly turns 4–7 and against creature-centric tables. ## Material interaction and response-window audit

1. **End-step trigger ordering:** Davros and `Wound Reflection` both trigger at the beginning of your end step. If an opponent lost 2 earlier, you may stack Reflection to resolve **first** and cause two more life loss before Davros resolves; then Davros recognizes the threshold. If Davros resolves first, it cannot create a token from that later Reflection loss. Davros's trigger occurs at the step regardless of threshold; the conditional instruction checks on resolution. This is a timing-sensitive positive synergy, not an infinite loop.
2. **`Perforating Artist` timing:** Its raid end-step trigger requires an attack earlier that turn. Stack Artist's trigger to resolve before Davros if its damage choice is to help reach three. But each opponent may choose a discard or nonland sacrifice instead, so the 3-loss threshold is **not guaranteed**. This is an opponent-response weakness of that enabler, not proof the card is bad.
3. **Copy mechanics:** Davros is an **artifact creature Alien Scientist, not a Dalek**. `Roaming Throne` should name **Alien** or **Scientist** (if specifically doubling Davros), never Dalek merely from the commander's name; it generates an additional trigger, not a copy. `Strionic Resonator` copies the existing whole Davros end-step trigger for `{2},{T}`, and `Scientist Supreme of A.I.M.` may copy an artifact-source trigger at the cost of two life once on your turn. On resolution each copy checks the opponent-loss thresholds; additional choices are still made by affected opponents. `The Valeyard` increases their choice count rather than replacing the trigger with guaranteed card draw. Opponents may remove a copier before use, counter a copied ability, or remove Davros *before end step* so no trigger is generated. Removal **after** a triggered ability reaches the stack generally does not erase that ability.
4. **Combat and counterplay:** `Cybermen Squadron`/`Auton Soldier` myriad tokens do not trigger abilities keyed to being declared attackers, but can deal combat damage and cause combat-damage triggers. Menace forces at least two blockers. A single chump blocker plus another may prevent a given Dalek's damage, while the blanket afflict 3 from `Cyberman Patrol` still makes blocks costly. Opponents can sacrifice tokens in response to relevant copy/trigger activations, use instant creature removal before damage, prevent damage, or empty their hands to reduce Davros's choices.
5. **End-step life tracking and opponents' agency:** An opponent gaining life after losing 3 does not erase their historical life loss this turn. A player with an empty hand may choose Davros's discard branch and discard nothing. The Dalek token creation is at most one *per resolution* even if all three opponents independently lost at least 3; the choices are individual, not combined into one group decision.
