## Commander identity and real game plan

**Sol'Kanar the Tainted** is an original {2}{U}{B}{R} 5/5 Elemental Demon. At the BEGINNING OF YOUR END STEP, choose a mode not previously chosen on that object: draw a card; make each opponent lose 2 and you gain 2; deal 3 damage to up to one **other** target creature or planeswalker; or exile Sol'Kanar, returning it under an opponent's control. WotC Dominaria United release notes specify that under an opponent's control it triggers on that player's end step; THAT player chooses the new mode and receives the benefit of its draw/life modes. The fourth-mode transfer performs exile/re-entry and creates a NEW object with no previous mode history, so it is not a harmless "temporary" control exchange.

The commander-first challenge is therefore **collect the first three incremental modes, safely reset/retain a five-power body, or weaponize the inevitable control exchange**, while using ownership/control-changing spells to transfer *harmful liabilities* rather than give opponents a free value creature. `Demonic Pact` promises early benefit but eventually presents a lose-the-game mode; `Immortal Coil` can lose its controller the game if its graveyard is emptied; `Greed's Gambit` supplies immediate hand/life/tokens but creates future end-step discard/life loss/sacrifice obligations. `Donate`, `Harmless Offering`, `Puca's Mischief`, `Fateful Handoff`, `Role Reversal`, `The Trickster-God's Heist`, `Wrong Turn`, `Shifting Grift`, `Sudden Substitution` and similar exchanges let the pilot assign those liabilities—or seize value—from opponents. `Homeward Path` can retake an owned creature after an effect hands it to an enemy; it also can undo desired theft of opponents' creatures, so timing and **owner** matter.

`Conjurer's Closet`, `Thassa, Deep-Dwelling` and `Planar Incision` provide resetting; `Gilded Drake`, `Ertai Resurrected`, `Baleful Strix`, `Humble Defector` and `Coveted Falcon` provide ETB/transfer/card flow. `Assault Suit`, `Blim, Comedic Genius`, `Jon Irenicus, Shattered One`, `Kardur, Doomscourge`, `Archfiend of the Dross`, `Perplexing Chimera` and `Kain, Traitorous Dragoon` deepen the unusual lending, theft and attack-redirection identity. This is NOT a generic Grixis spells combo shell. Bracket 4 optimized is a defensible natural ceiling: strong counters/tutors/mana, but the five-mana commander yields only one end-step increment per cycle absent resets; donation timing often needs a second card and a safe target.

## Role, access, curve, and vulnerability audit

There are **35 land-card slots**, including utility `Homeward Path`, `Bojuka Bog`, `Forbidden Orchard` and `Exotic Orchard`, six fetches, typed duals and ten basics (Island3/Mountain3/Swamp4); utility/tapped lands and generic-only fast mana can constrain immediate {U}{B}{R} for the commander. `Sol Ring`, `Mana Vault`, `Arcane Signet`, Dimir/Izzet/Rakdos Signets and three Talismans provide acceleration, but `Assault Suit` costs {4} plus {3} to equip, `Conjurer's Closet` costs {5}, `Demonic Pact` {2}{B}{B}, `Thassa` {3}{U}, and one-shot control exchanges require multiple colors or additional mana. Tutor suite (Demonic/Vampiric), counter suite (Counterspell absent; `Mana Drain`, `Force of Will`, `Swan Song`, `Fierce Guardianship`), and removal (`Cyclonic Rift`, `Toxic Deluge`, `Deadly Rollick`, `Rakdos Charm`, `Chaos Warp`, `Vandalblast`) protect a fragile multicomponent plan but not every resolved spell. Don't count `Deflecting Swat` / `Fierce Guardianship` as free without controlling commander.

Conditional or comparatively weak slots worthy of skepticism, **not authorized cuts**: `Conjurer's Closet` is five mana and creates no first-turn ETB by itself; `Assault Suit` is expensive equipment and opponents may decline useful attack paths; `Humble Defector` risks gifting a two-card draw engine; `Greed's Gambit` can hurt us if donation unavailable; `Spiteful Banditry` is board control rather than donation engine; `Planar Incision` is one-time rescue at a card cost. Conversely each covers a real role and no one is filler merely because it is slow. The best cuts must preserve a **safe, repeatable end-step commander reset**, a **reliable liability donation**, and enough colored access/protection.

## Strongest neutral challenger — research-only

**Hypothesis A: `+ Displacer Kitten / - Conjurer's Closet`**. WotC's Commander Legends: Battle for Baldur's Gate notes verify Displacer Kitten {3}{U} 2/2: whenever you CAST a NONCREATURE spell, exile up to one target NONLAND permanent YOU control and immediately return that card under its OWNER's control. Returning is a **new object**. In this spell-rich Grixis deck, it can reset Sol'Kanar's exhausted mode selections **before** the dangerous fourth end step, turn control spells/counters into protection against commander removal, and blink own `Demonic Pact` to erase chosen modes before the lose-the-game fourth choice. It can also reuse `Ertai Resurrected` or `Baleful Strix` ETB, provided the targeted permanent is ours to control when Kitten triggers and it survives to resolution. Cost {3}{U} instead of Closet's {5} may improve turn-four sequencing, and reactive noncreature casts potentially trigger more than once per turn.

**Strongest counterargument against preferred cut:** `Conjurer's Closet` is a **repeatable unconditional end-step** blink on each of our turns after setup and returns the creature **under OUR control**, even one we controlled only until end of turn; this can retain stolen bodies permanently and reset Sol on the exact step where its forced donation mode threatens. Kitten is a fragile 2/2 and demands an actual noncreature **CAST** while it is present, plus suitable legal nonland target; copies or activated abilities do not trigger it. At a late end step, normal sorcery-speed artifact/spell casts cannot provide an emergency trigger without special permission; need an instant or flash enabler and priority to blink in response to the existing trigger. Critically, Kitten returns a creature to its **OWNER** rather than "under our control," so it often undoes temporary theft instead of making it permanent. Removing Closet may destroy a highly commander-specific theft mode despite Kitten's generic combo potential. `Thassa, Deep-Dwelling` already supplies another recurring end-step blink; Closet redundancy may be the right resilience. Thus **not a proven upgrade**.

**Alternate cuts for Kitten to compare**, not assumed weak: `Planar Incision` (2-mana reactive one-shot artifact/creature exile-return; a critical immediate commander rescue, plus +1/+1 counter if creature), or `Spiteful Banditry` (reliable damage/treasure/board tempo in aggro pods but not donation-themed). Replacing one-shot Incision reduces instant-speed emergency effects; replacing Banditry cuts anti-aggro and resource generation. `Assault Suit` is expensive, but anchors the "give them a bad creature" persona and blocks sacrifices, so cannot be dismissed via mana value alone. Lower-priority challenger `Teferi's Time Twist` ({1}{U}, exile target permanent you control, delayed return at next end step) can reset OWN `Demonic Pact` rather than just creatures but sacrifices immediate Sol reset, may miss an end step and does not protect an already donated permanent.

## Verified interaction constraints / opponent windows

1. **End-step stack:** Sol and Closet/Thassa controlled by the same player can trigger at beginning of that player's end step. The controller orders their simultaneous triggers; blink may reset the creature before the preexisting modal trigger resolves. Mode selection for that earlier trigger is already made when put on stack. A later new Sol is NOT the old permanent addressed by its source-exile fourth mode, so that old effect cannot gift the new object. The new permanent does not retroactively trigger again in the same step. Opponents can remove the blink target before blink resolves or remove the commander/engine earlier; the specific target/counter window matters.
2. **Opponent gets the modes:** If fourth mode really exiles and returns Sol under opponent control, the opponent chooses future modes for THEIR own end-step triggers. It is not automatic doom for them: they may enjoy draw/life/damage three times. Donation as tactical benefit requires an explicit different plan (e.g. `Homeward Path` retake or enemy-player constraints), not an invented loss trigger.
3. **Pact liability:** Demonic Pact chooses one unchosen mode on upkeep, not one each turn whenever convenient; WotC Foundations notes say chosen mode remains chosen even if its trigger is countered or target becomes illegal, and if last option is "lose" it must be chosen. Donate/swap **before** that player faces the dangerous upkeep; if no legal target for a target-dependent mode, that mode choice may not be available. Sol has its own separate modal history, which cannot be conflated with Pact's.
4. **Ownership and return:** Kitten returns under **owner** and Closet/Thassa return under **controller** as each spell says. `Homeward Path` returns each owned creature to its owner but doesn't move donated enchantments like `Demonic Pact`. An exchange of control generally does not change the physical owner of a card. A handoff of a losing trigger may be beaten by sacrifice, enchantment removal, or a replacement effect.
5. **Immortal Coil / Greed's Gambit:** Coil's no-graveyard losing condition depends on the **CURRENT controller's** graveyard after control transfer, not our graveyard. Opponent may exile/replenish graveyard in response; generic mill is not instant victory. Greed's Gambit ETB gets three cards, six life and three bats; its own end-step liabilities belong to its controller then. Swapping it to opponent can create danger to us first if timing is wrong; safe interaction must model controller and triggers through current end step.
6. **Planar Incision vs blink timing:** Immediate exile/return can dodge the old commander fourth-mode ability if appropriately timed on stack; a phasing trick would not erase "modes already chosen" because it is the same object; a blink with delayed return may skip a cycle. Kitten requires a fresh NONCREATURE spell cast to make its blink trigger and must not be treated as a free instant-speed activated ability.
