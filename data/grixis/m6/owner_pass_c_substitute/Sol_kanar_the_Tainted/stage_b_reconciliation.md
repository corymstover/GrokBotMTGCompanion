## Exact new challenger and finite rules trace

**Proposed copy-only challenger:** `+Displacer Kitten (3U 2/2) / -Conjurer's Closet (5 artifact)`. Kitten triggers from any NONCREATURE SPELL actually CAST while it is in play; resolves by exiling one target **nonland permanent we CONTROL**, then returning it as a new object under its **OWNER'S control**. This can reset Sol'Kanar chosen modes or own `Demonic Pact`, which Closet (creature-only) cannot blink. Other targets `Ertai Resurrected`, `Baleful Strix`, owned artifact/enchantment value permanent may be valid. Kitten's two advantages over Closet are cheaper initial cost and on-demand noncreature-cast triggers; no continuous control of another player's object is granted by its return.

**Source-bound case A — three modes already spent:** Suppose Sol in play has chosen draw, drain and damage on prior own end steps; Kitten in play, opponent has not killed it, and during main phase controller casts `Arcane Signet` as a noncreature spell. Kitten's triggered blink can target Sol to reset mode memory. At this turn's end step the **new** Sol chooses any first fresh mode. This is paid 3U Kitten before the spell + cost of Signet (not an instant-speed free activation). Closet already on field would reset Sol during end step each turn without new cast; its timing can prevent the final gift if properly stacked.

**Case B — late emergency after fourth choice on stack:** Sol trigger at own beginning end step is forced to choose gift, Kitten is present. Controller must possess and legally cast an **instant / flash noncreature** during this end step (e.g. Swan Song only if a legal noncreature spell to counter; `Rakdos Charm` with relevant legal mode; `Chaos Warp` with legal target). Kitten blink resolves above the old fourth-mode trigger. Old trigger cannot exile the new Sol object after original changed zones, so no gift; the new object will not get a second end-step trigger that turn. Merely holding sorcery-speed `Arcane Signet` does not enable an end-step rescue. If Kitten is destroyed in response to the noncreature cast after its trigger is created, removal of Kitten alone doesn't undo the already-stacked trigger; removal of the **target Sol** before it resolves can prevent blinking. Avoid conflating source removal with targeted trigger legality.

**Case C — stolen creature until end of turn:** Controller temporarily borrows opponent-owned creature via `Reins of Power`. `Conjurer's Closet` at OWN end step targets that controlled creature and returns it under **our control** (even as effect giving control until end of turn would expire in cleanup), permitting indefinite retention. Kitten can target the controlled opponent-owned creature, but returns it to its **opponent owner** instead, losing the theft. If Kitten's blink is used instead on Sol, this does not do the Closet theft capture either. `Thassa` already performs controller-return end-step blink, but removing Closet halves access to this role and makes artifact/creature hate allocation important.

**Case D — Pact liability:** With `Demonic Pact` currently controlled/owned, its first three modes recorded, and Kitten in play, casting a noncreature instant/sorcery on previous turn creates Kitten blink that can reset the Pact as a new object; chosen modes reset. Closet cannot blink Pact (not creature); therefore Kitten could reduce dependence on having a `Donate` / Offering / Puca / Heist handoff before losing fourth upkeep. If Pact is **already controlled by opponent**, Kitten cannot target it; it may be safer to let Pact curse that opponent. Enemy removal of Kitten before next legal cast leaves no protection and source user still faces own upkeep loss if Pact held.

**Case E — no available spell:** Kitten + Sol with exhausted first three modes and no legal noncreature instant/flash before beginning end step forces gift (barring other reset/outlet). Closet/Thassa each reset at end step even with zero spells. This distinct commander-failure state might dominate many grindy turns.

**Case F — temporary opponent spell exchange:** `Perplexing Chimera`, `Sudden Substitution`, `Fateful Handoff`, `Jon Irenicus`, `Gilded Drake` have different owner/controller transitions and control-duration terms. Kitten is not a universal permanent-theft engine. On own nonland permanents it may work repeatedly; blinking opponent-owned but temporarily controlled creature returns it to opponent owner. Always check exact timing and whether the transfer can be reversed by Homeward Path or Coveted Falcon. These are **worked action traces**, not randomized simulations or measured wins.
