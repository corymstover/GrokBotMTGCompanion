## 2. Distinct commander strategy and realistic ceiling

Obeka is {1}{U}{B}{R}, 3/4 Ogre Wizard; **{T}: The player whose turn it is may end the turn.** When that player chooses yes, spells and abilities currently on stack are exiled, combat ends, the game skips to cleanup (with rare new cleanup triggered abilities handled normally), and **"this turn" / "until end of turn" effects expire**. The person whose turn it is decides, NOT Obeka's controller, so on an opponent's turn they may decline; on our own turn we reliably control that choice.

The design exploits an important event distinction:
- A **one-shot delayed trigger** established by Sneak Attack, Feldon, reanimation/temporary theft and extra-turn risk ("at beginning of the *next* end step, sacrifice/exile/lose") can be put on the stack at that very end step, then exiled with Obeka's end-turn ability. It has triggered already and will **not be rescheduled** at a later end step. This can permanently preserve otherwise fleeting creature/token effects or safely survive a lethal extra-turn delayed trigger.
- If the turn is ended **before** that next end step begins, the one-shot trigger waits for a subsequent end step and still fires, so proactive tapping in main phase is not the same safe operation.
- Static duration "**until end of turn**" still expires in cleanup no matter how abruptly the turn ended; a copied face, temporary control modifier or stat buff does not become permanent simply by skipping the end step.
- An **ordinary recurring trigger** intrinsic to a permanent, e.g. Avaricious Dragon ("at beginning of *your* end step, discard your hand") occurs again on every later appropriate end step, even if an individual trigger is exiled. Similarly Delina's token copy is granted a normal end-of-combat triggered ability ("At end of combat, exile this creature"), which can fire at a later combat; removing one occurrence does NOT establish permanent token safety. Wizards explicitly distinguished it from a one-shot delayed triggered ability in 2022.
- Ending the turn exiles *all* current stack objects, including our own payoffs that have not resolved, not merely the undesirable trigger. On an opponent's turn, the active player can decline Obeka's option. Commander tap is a scarce resource with summoning-sickness, haste, untap and priority limitations.

The natural ceiling is **Bracket 4 / Optimized**, potentially explosive turn loops and sticky ETB/combat value with strong interaction, but 4-mana commander access, end-step trigger dependence and protection/untap limits do not independently establish a generic bracket-five deterministic cEDH shell. The 99 contains effective fast mana, tutors and top stack defense to push this commander-first plan, not a requirement to erase it with generic Grixis Breach/tutor piles.

## 5. Independently chosen challenger, alternative cuts and strongest counterargument

**New conditional challenger independent of the handoff dispute: +`Mimic Vat` / -`Avaricious Dragon`.** This preserves 100/100 and UBR singleton/identity in a hypothetical version, no canonical change. Mimic Vat is a {3} artifact that can imprint a nontoken creature card upon death and pay {3},{T} to create a hasty token copy. It instructs exile token **at the beginning of the next end step** (a one-shot obligation). WotC Lost Caverns of Ixalan release notes state the exile happens then regardless of Vat or imprint-card state. If Obeka exiles that triggered instance *after it triggers* at beginning of next end step, token may remain indefinitely, making Vat a repeatable commander-themed way to preserve high-value imprinted attackers/ETBs. It also benefits from mass deaths generated by a board wipe, unlike Avaricious's conditional extra draw. Crucial costs: Vat needs a **real nontoken creature death to imprint**, then three additional mana and a tap, and an untapped willing Obeka at the correct end step; initial six mana (spread across turns) is slow and opponents can deny suitable deaths. It cannot preserve token text with ongoing recurring sacrifices or "until end of turn."

**Why cut Dragon only conditionally:** At {2}{R}{R} with flying, an extra card every draw step is legitimate efficient repeatable value if Obeka can continually exile its recurring discard trigger. But then Obeka must dedicate taps on every Dragon turn, threatening the highest-value one-shot delay or extra-turn safety. In tempo/interaction-heavy games, an unanswered Dragon without Obeka discards protection and reanimate cards before next turn; its rate becomes volatile. That makes it a real conditional slot, not an automatically bad card.

**Best counterargument AGAINST the favored trade:** Mimic Vat is also slow to start and dead without either a creature death or access to Obeka; the deck already packs ample permanent-value engines (Feldon, Flameshadow, Saheeli, Jaxis, Kiki-Jiki, Rionya, Sneak Attack). Avaricious may deliver extra drawn cards one turn earlier and does not need an opponent graveyard or a creature die. Replacing proactive raw card draw with a reactive six-mana token setup could reduce consistency, increase artifact exposure and make an optimized Bracket 4 deck weaker. The presealed hypothesis is **NEEDS TESTING**, baseline KEEP.

**Other plausible cuts/competitors NOT chosen automatically:**
- `Identity Thief`'s temporary copied face resets in cleanup, but its delayed *return opponent's creature* trigger can be exiled to keep it away. This is meaningful special removal; cannot classify as flavor-only filler.
- `Avaricious Dragon` versus `Psychic Vortex`: Vortex can upkeep cumulative draws but demands sacrifices/hand discards, another repeated schedule commander can influence only at a cost. Both have unique upside/downside and need source-bound comparison.
- `Delina` makes attacking copy tokens via dice, high variance and recurring end-of-combat exile; a speculative cut can be discussed only after accurately verifying repeating trigger semantics, but **the handoff disclosed its historical cut pair in advance**. Keep it outside the initial independent exact pair.
- `Discontinuity`, `Sundial` and extra turns are not redundant generic slots: they insure against Obeka tapped/removal and may permit safe delayed-trigger exiling. Reducing backup end-turn effects because one commander taps once would overstate access.
- `Thousand-Year Elixir`/Minamo/haste equipment are not luxuries if commander must tap for every safe extra-turn; protection also matters more than adding unconditional expensive token engines.

## 6. Three concrete adversarial finite cases (no simulation claim)

1. **Sneak Attack**: Creature put into play hasty; ability creates a *next end step* sacrifice trigger. On that end step, move sacrifice trigger to stack. Now Obeka untapped with legal priority? Activate; own active player chooses end turn. Sacrifice trigger is exiled, permanent remains. If Obeka activates during second main phase instead, end step skipped and one-shot sacrifice happens at a later end step; failure to preserve. If opponent removes Obeka in response and no redundant end-turn source, sacrifice resolves.
2. **Extra turn**: Final Fortune resolves and extra turn occurs. Its specific delayed lose-game trigger is put on stack at beginning of the *extra turn's* end step. Activate Obeka, choose end turn and exile. If Obeka summoning-sick/tapped or opponent destroys it before activation and no Discontinuity, losing is real. Multiple one-shot obligations can stack, and the activated ability exiles all of them if the timing is correct.
3. **Delina**: On attack, choose target creature, roll d20, create attacking token with **its own regular "at end of combat exile this creature" triggered ability**, not a one-time delayed trigger. At combat-end step it triggers, Obeka can end the turn to exile *that instance*. Unlike Sneak Attack, on a later combat the surviving token's regular ability triggers again. The token is only conditionally retained for a window, not automatically permanent. WotC Sept2022 Oracle change directly established ordinary-trigger nature. This inference is neutral rules application despite handoff exposure; **no original prior verdict opened**.
