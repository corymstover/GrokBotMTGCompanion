## Source-backed error corrections — leave prior artifacts intact

1. **Blizzard Specter has NO haste.** Its official WotC *Iconic Masters* release notes (https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/iconic-masters-release-notes-2017-09-11) list `2UB`, 2/3, **flying**, and combat-damage choice: affected player returns a permanent *they* control to its owner's hand **or** discards one. No haste. The two-model reconciliation's claim that it is a `4MV U/B flying haste` specter is wrong. The **opponent chooses their permanent** to bounce if that mode is selected, so this does not force removal of the specific troublesome permanent; replaying a land may still cost tempo. This affects the alternative-cut ranking. Stage A's 4MV slow-contact concerns are substantiated, but **the original prior haste claim cannot be reused in M6 testing**.
2. **Stage A imprecisely grouped Teferi's Puzzle Box with wheels causing discard.** Box instructs a player to put their hand on the **bottom of their library**, then draw that many; bottoming is **not a discard**. The Box alone triggers none of Waste Not, Megrim, Liliana's Caress, Geth's Grimoire, or Tinybones' discard abilities. Its relevant synergy is draw replacement by Notion Thief *after the normal first draw* and hand-size disruption, plus separate sources that actually say discard. This correction belongs here; Stage A's sealed text must not be edited.
3. **Dauthi Voidwalker does not remove existing graveyards.** While on the battlefield it *replaces later would-be entries* into opponents' graveyards with exile/void counters. Prior graveyard cards remain until another effect moves them. It can prevent **Drown in the Loch** from gaining future target scope; it does not itself shrink the number already present. Official WotC *Modern Horizons 2* notes confirm discard triggers still occur under its replacement, but **Tinybones, Bauble Burglar** cannot exile that card *from the graveyard* or add a stash counter and **Tergrid** generally cannot recover that discarded permanent from that graveyard. These are substantive conflicts, not superficial 'goodstuff' criticism.
4. **Megrim vs Liliana's Caress:** Megrim for `{2}{B}` does **2 damage** per discarded opponent card; existing Caress for `{1}{B}` causes **2 life loss** per card. These distinctions matter for damage prevention, lifelink, source-target protection and non-commander-damage accounting. Both require an actual discard event after they exist; Painter color modification and Puzzle Box bottoming alone do not count.

### Deterministic effect bounds, NOT empirical tests

- Crosis with Painter connecting against one opponent holding six cards, and trigger cost 2B paid: **six discard events** if Painter remains and chosen color matches. Megrim already on table gives six separately triggered instances × 2 **damage** = 12 (plus 6 commander combat damage). With existing Liliana's Caress also present, another 12 **life loss** is possible. Neither extra payoff exists without its own permanent surviving, and damage/life-loss events can be prevented or interacted with as normal.
- Wheel of Fortune causing three opponents each to discard seven cards yields **21 Megrim triggers total**, dealing 14 to each player if all resolve and no prevention; the total 42 is **distributed** noncommander damage, not one player taking 42. Geth's Grimoire could separately queue 21 optional draws; no trigger interrupts the wheel resolution.
- Aggravated Assault plus Sword is **not** two-card unrestricted infinite combat. Need at least a sword-bearing combatant to actually hit a player and an ability to produce 3RR from lands after Sword untaps them; the activation can occur only at sorcery speed during a main phase, not from any combat damage trigger. Sword's untap and Crosis hand-discard triggers must be stacked/paid correctly. The combination can loop combats if these conditions persist, but the first Sword connection, equipped attacker and each opponent's ability to interact remain key dependencies.
- Dauthi plus a mass discard can collect void-counter exiled spells, while Geth's Grimoire / Waste Not still see opponent **discard actions** (WotC MH2 notes). However Bauble Burglar's stash ability and Tergrid's graveyard recovery don't gain the moved cards. No actual sequence/win-percentage sample was run.

## Deliberate final comparison and targeted Phase B test prescription

The newly exposed **Megrim** pair has a more direct Crosis identity rationale than the initially preferred Dauthi: Megrim magnifies what the approved deck already does without interrupting its own Bauble Burglar, Tergrid or Drown. However the preferred **cut** is contested by an actual Oracle correction and engine-vs-payoff balance. Strongest pro-Megrim case: multiple paid full-hand purges/wheels turn every opponent discard into a much shorter game. Strongest anti case: after opponents empty their hands, this third punishment enchantment is inert, while an on-hit Specter still pressures board/hand and carries Sword; losing the independent card-discard creator may harm the very chance of triggering Megrim. In either cut, actual interaction and protection access may matter more than nominal 12 damage.

**Priority 1 pairwise test**: locked baseline vs `+Megrim/-Sedraxis Specter` vs `+Megrim/-Blizzard Specter`, with realistic opposing initial 3/5/7-card hands, early deployment/removal, color-aware opening turns, and 0- vs 1- vs 2-wheel access. Explicitly count turns where **Megrim was on battlefield before the discard** and where Specters actually connected. Include response windows. Use a simulator only when it models per-card discard, combat and damage; otherwise deterministic rule traces/hand-scenario logs and human strategic judgment should be labeled as such, not as simulated win rates.

**Priority 2 if material:** verify Aggravated Assault/Sword hit-trigger order and how often a naturally protected, sword-equipped Crosis gets ≥5 mana of lands and two red sources repeatedly; check sweepers, removal and graveyard hate. **Priority 3:** bounded Dauthi/Tinybones/Tergrid conflict and Bloodchief three-end-step clock if the primary Megrim comparison does not resolve the meaningful weakness. Existing comprehensive review evidence is reusable. Do not initiate the entire research pipeline or blanket 62-deck rereview.
