## Commander-first judgment

At **{3}{U}{B}{R}** and 6/6 flying, Crosis is a comparatively slow, high-threat combat-damage discard commander. A hit offers an optional further **{2}{B}** payment *as the trigger resolves* to choose a color and purge that one damaged opponent's matching hand cards. **Painter's Servant** naming the same color turns a single hit into a whole-hand purge while it survives, but does not affect Commander color identity. Brotherhood Regalia/Boots/Greaves, Shizo, Sword of Feast and Famine, thirty-six lands, efficient mana acceleration, and robust interaction make that specific plan plausible. Waste Not, Geth's Grimoire, Liliana's Caress, Tergrid, multiple Tinybones, Specters and actual wheel/discard spells convert attrition into advantage, a commander-centric deck distinguishable from Grixis cEDH goodstuff. The provisional **natural ceiling remains Bracket 4 optimized** rather than forcing a Bracket 5 combo shell.

His first turn of combat typically requires six-mana deployment, survival/evasion, a player still holding colored cards, and the additional paid black mana. If the affected player's hand is already empty, neither the purge nor additional discard payoffs accomplish much. Losing Painter before resolution, removal/bounce before combat damage, stax, fast combo and artifact/enchantment hate are material checks. Returning a card to a player's hand with a selected Blizzard Specter mode can still produce tempo, but the opponent chooses which permanent to return.

## Exact proposed pairs requiring evidence — NOT card approvals

**Highest priority consequential dispute:**

1. **Add Megrim / cut Sedraxis Specter** (existing reconciliation hypothesis). Megrim `{2}{B}` turns an actual Crosis/Painter full-hand discard of six cards into **twelve noncommander damage** (plus six Crosis combat damage), and a seven-card wheel discard from each of three opponents into **14 damage to each opponent**, assuming triggers survive and no prevention. Unlike Liliana's Caress, this is *damage* rather than plain life loss. But removing Sedraxis deletes an independent UBR three-power evasive discard threat with a once-per-graveyard-use unearth attack and Sword-carrying capability. With hands depleted, Megrim becomes inactive.
2. **Add Megrim / cut Blizzard Specter** (this review's necessary alternative). Blizzard is actually 4MV with **no haste**; removing it preserves cheaper Sedraxis and replaces a later-turn creature-contact effect with a global discard payoff. Its real cost is losing a modal on-hit non-targeting bounce/discard tool that can pressure permanents or land tempo; the recipient picks which permanent to return. This pair may dominate the previous cut **only if** the effect-aware payoff-vs-enabler comparison supports it. A rules correction alone is not win-rate proof.

**Mechanically relevant secondary comparison:** **+Aggravated Assault / −Blizzard Specter**. With Sword of Feast and Famine equipped to an attacker that can keep hitting a player, the Sword trigger must untap lands able to provide at least 3RR, then Assault's **sorcery-speed** activation in a postcombat main adds another combat followed by a main; repeat only while the Sword-bearing attacker connects and mana can be refreshed. Crosis does not gain free 2B payments or automatic connections. This is a plausible commander-damage win route, and deserves an accurate rules trace and access/interruption test if material, but is not a proven upgrade.

**Lower-priority sealed first-pass hypotheses:** **+Dauthi Voidwalker / −Blizzard Specter**; **+Bloodchief Ascension / −Blizzard Specter**. Dauthi sees cards that *would enter* opponent graveyards and exiles them with void counters; WotC Modern Horizons 2 notes confirm discard triggers still happen. However this prevents Tinybones, Bauble Burglar from stashing those newly discarded cards from the grave, generally prevents Tergrid from returning them, and inhibits further Drown in the Loch threshold growth—although Dauthi does not erase already present graveyard cards. Bloodchief can be a powerful one-B finisher but requires **three qualifying end-step triggers** and card movement into actual opposing graveyards; it has a timing risk and conflicts with Dauthi's replacement. Neither is currently a preferred unconditional add.
