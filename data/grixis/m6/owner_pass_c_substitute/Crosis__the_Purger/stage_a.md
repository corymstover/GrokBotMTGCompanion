## Distinct commander identity and ceiling

**Crosis costs {3}{U}{B}{R}**, flies as a 6/6, and triggers *on combat damage to a player*, not on attack: the controller may pay an **additional {2}{B}**, then choose a color and force **that damaged player** to reveal and discard every card of that color. No payment before combat can guarantee the effect and no second targeted opponent is affected by a single trigger. A 6-power commander can alternatively threaten 21 commander damage over four unblocked attacks. This six-mana evasive hand-purge commander is substantially different from generic Breach/Oracle Grixis.

The defining interaction is **Painter's Servant**. Its chosen color is added to **all cards not on the battlefield**, including opponents' hands. After Crosis connects, choosing the same color with the paid trigger normally discards *every card then in that one player's hand*, irrespective of the card's original color. Painter must still be applying when Crosis resolves: an opponent can remove it while the combat-damage trigger is on the stack. Painter does not rewrite Commander **color identity**. Under no circumstances claim that this reaches all three opponents with one combat trigger or works if the opponent has no cards.

This deck protects the actual plan with **Brotherhood Regalia** (equip a legendary creature for {1}, ward {2}, cannot be blocked), Lightning Greaves, Swiftfoot Boots, Shizo, Death's Storehouse, and a robust interactive suite. The discard engine then pays off through **Waste Not**, Geth's Grimoire, Liliana's Caress, Fell Specter, Tinybones variants, Tergrid, the Raven Man, and lower-hand payoffs Bandit's Talent and Tinybones, Trinket Thief. Wheel of Fortune, Windfall, Dark Deal, and Teferi's Puzzle Box provide multi-opponent discard even without a surviving six-mana commander. Painter and Crosis emphasize a hand destruction identity; fast compact Grixis combo packages would homogenize this design.

**Natural provisional ceiling: optimized Bracket 4**, not assumed cEDH. A six-mana commander plus a three-mana damage-trigger payment, combat survival and evasion support are meaningful timing liabilities against early combo and removal. Zero measured win-rate evidence is claimed. Exact game-changer/bracket status should be reevaluated with contemporary Wizards rules at formal certification.

## Whole-deck structural and access audit

**Mana.** Thirty-six lands include eleven basic slots and flexible original duals/shocks/fetches, Command Tower, City of Brass, Mana Confluence, Exotic Orchard, Xander's Lounge, and utility Shizo/Geier Reach. Thirteen named repeat/one-shot acceleration or cost-support pieces include Sol Ring, Mana Vault, Lotus Petal, Chrome Mox, Mox Diamond, Arcane Signet, three Signets, three Talismans, and Jet Medallion; Dark Ritual adds temporary black burst. Cavern of Souls and Plaza of Heroes can help resolve/defend the commander in their applicable conditions but are not unrestricted colored spell sources. Exact opening-hand colored-source odds and activation-turn rates have **not** been run. Crosis requires {U}{B}{R}, with an extra black pip and three mana held or produced at the damage trigger; casting Crosis on curve without follow-up mana will not immediately purge a hand.

**Curve and access.** Vampiric/Demonic/Mystical Tutor, Gamble and high-quality raw draw/steal engines fetch or find a narrow vital piece; the library also has significant four-plus-mana discard creatures and artifacts. Black Market Connections trades life for resources; Geth's Grimoire and Waste Not are excellent into real opponent discards but can be bad topdecks into already empty hands. The plan must develop both mana and repeatable disruption before opponents empty hands naturally.

**Interaction/defense.** Force of Will, Fierce Guardianship, Mana Drain, Swan Song, Deflecting Swat, Cyclonic Rift, Chaos Warp, Feed the Swarm, Recoil, Drown in the Loch, Damnation and Toxic Deluge address a range of fast spell, creature and permanent threats. Some free counters require a real commander or pitch resource. Recoil's nonland-permanent bounce plus discard and Feed the Swarm's enchantment coverage are not disposable just because a new payoff looks cheaper. Drown in the Loch scales with target controller's graveyard density; it weakens when that graveyard is emptied or exiled.

**Synergies/tensions.** Geth's Grimoire creates separate draw triggers per opponent-discarded card; opposing responses can occur between triggers, not during resolution of a Wheel of Fortune. Waste Not triggers distinguish land, creature and other discard. Notion Thief plus Wheel/Windfall denies opponent draw and draws those cards instead, but opponents may respond to or remove Thief, and this can strongly shift table threat assessment. Teferi's Puzzle Box with Notion Thief still permits the ordinary first draw of the draw step before the Box trigger resolves; later replacement draws can be stolen. **Bottomless Pit** and **Oppression** affect Crosis's own controller too, making protection and answer cards less reliably retainable. Shizo's fear-granting line and Painter choosing black can conflict because black creatures then block fear; Regalia remains distinct unconditional evasion.

**Counterplay.** Remove Painter before the trigger resolves; kill or bounce Crosis before damage; counter the commander, protect the hand with instant-speed activation, deny attacks with flying/reach/blockers; graveyard exile weakens Kroxa, Reanimate, Bauble Burglar and Drown in the Loch; artifact/enchantment wipes hit the draw and discard engines; fast spell combos can go under a six-mana threat; discard is comparatively weaker once opponents have deployed all their cards. The same targeted hand purge can provoke a multiplayer coalition. These are significant liabilities, not evidence for forcing cEDH staples.

## Open challenger hypotheses — neutral, unranked

1. **Dauthi Voidwalker for Blizzard Specter** (alternative cut: **Sedraxis Specter**). For {B}{B}, Voidwalker gives an early shadow attacker, exiles opposing cards that would enter graveyards, and can spend its tap-and-sacrifice activation to play **one opponent-owned exiled void-counter card that turn** without its mana cost, subject to normal timing. WotC explicitly confirms discard-triggered abilities still function on exiled discarded cards. It converts Crosis/Painter and wheels into high-upside stolen-card access and suppresses graveyard recursion. **Serious internal cost:** Dauthi's replacement keeps victims out of their graveyards, often shrinking **Drown in the Loch** and preventing **Tinybones, Bauble Burglar** from exiling a newly discarded card *from the graveyard* with a stash counter. It can contest Tergrid's graveyard-entry and reanimation routes as well. Blizzard Specter is slower, but contributes on-hit choice of a permanent bounce or a discard, and losing a repeated commander-themed Saboteur weakens the deck's distinct texture. Sedraxis has a lower {U}{B}{R} body and unearth redundancy; cutting it has a different downside. This is a **real hypothesis, not a proven upgrade**.
2. **Bloodchief Ascension for Blizzard Specter** (alternatives: Sedraxis Specter or the comparatively narrower single-shot Recoil, whose removal utility argues against cutting it). The {B} enchantment can convert mass discarded cards entering opponents' graveyards into repeated drain/lifegain **only after three separate qualifying end-step quest counters**. WotC specifies a single opponent must have lost at least two life that turn for each end-step trigger; several small 1-life losses to different opponents do not combine. It is an on-theme alternate closing engine with wheels and Painter-assisted purges, unlike a generic off-theme infinite combo. However it does nothing immediately, cannot gain three counters in one end step, competes with other setup, and has very little effect on already empty or graveyard-replaced hands. If Dauthi is also present its replacement effect substantially suppresses Ascension's graveyard-put triggers; these are **competing packages**, not independently additive improvements.
3. **Whispering Madness for Syphon Mind**, or **Memory Jar for Blizzard Specter**: additional mass-hand churn would improve Waste Not, Grimoire, discard-trigger payoffs and access to a win condition. But Whispering Madness is a four-mana sorcery (cipher wants a protected damage source), while Memory Jar costs five and normally needs to survive for a tap activation. Syphon Mind already discards one card from **each** opponent and draws one for each, making the cut material rather than an obvious weak slot. Existing Wheel/Windfall/Dark Deal/Puzzle Box may be sufficient density.

### Strongest counterargument to the preferred direction

The tempting **Dauthi Voidwalker / Blizzard Specter** pair has a genuine tempo and graveyard-hate advantage, including a potentially game-swinging stolen spell after a wheel. But the incumbent offers repeatable hand or board pressure without taxing {B}{B} early; Dauthi can partially turn off two currently approved engines—Bauble Burglar's graveyard stash and Drown in the Loch's threshold—and a one-time free opponent spell need not equal long-run card value. Conversely, retaining a four-mana 2/3 combat-damage Specter while an efficient {B}{B} hate/thief engine exists can be overprotective of theme. Both arguments deserve a bounded gameplay/rules test before suggesting an owner-approved swap.
