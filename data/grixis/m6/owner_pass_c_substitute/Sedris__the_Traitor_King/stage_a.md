## Rules trace and opponent response windows

1. **Sedris activation:** After Sedris is on the battlefield, each creature card in *your graveyard* has a separate {2}{B} unearth activated ability. Activate only with sorcery timing and priority; paying the three mana does not cast a creature spell. The activation uses the stack. Targeted graveyard removal is not always necessary: an opponent can exile the specific card in response, causing the ability to do nothing on resolution. The ability already on stack does not depend on Sedris remaining on the battlefield.
2. **Unearth exit conditions:** The returned object gains haste; a delayed trigger will exile it at the beginning of the next end step. A separate replacement effect exiles it instead if it would leave the battlefield for a zone other than exile. Thus Ashnod's Altar/Goblin Bombardment can get their activation payoff, but not a dies trigger, nor return that creature to the graveyard for a second ordinary unearth.
3. **Sundial timing:** To preserve an unearthed creature, allow the **beginning of your end step** trigger to go onto the stack, then activate Sundial for {1},{T} while that trigger is pending. Ending the turn exiles that one-shot trigger from the stack and it will not trigger again. Activating Sundial *before* the end step instead skips the trigger's first opportunity, risking the next end step. The continuing leave-the-battlefield replacement exile remains operative. The analogous Obeka end-turn activation must actually be agreed to by the active player, not automatically accepted on an opponent's turn.
4. **Blink loophole:** If a spell or effect specifically exiles the unearthed creature and returns that card, the returned permanent is a new object, with no unearth tether. Deadeye Navigator can enable a paid repeatable route after pairing; a single targeted blink card would be cheaper but demands another card and an uninterrupted priority window. If the flicker effect targets, opponent removal in response can deny its intended save.
5. **Drake / Navigator:** A pair with five lands that collectively tap for at least three mana can pay {1}{U} to blink Drake and untap those lands. For positive net mana, account for the actual land-producing colors and Drake's triggering, and establish Navigator being paired again as needed. Infinite **mana** from a repeatable legal configuration is not automatically infinite **Goblin Bombardment damage** (no infinite disposable creature is created). A protected Terror of the Peaks can convert repeated creature ETB into finite arbitrarily many damage triggers if it is on the battlefield and targeting is legal.
6. **Convergence of Dominion:** With Sedris controlled, its {2} cost reduction applies to the unearth activation of a creature **card in the graveyard**, reducing {2}{B} to {B} while preserving a one-mana minimum. It does not substitute generic for black mana, and if commander is removed before later activations the discount disappears. This is a signature enabler and not a careless cut.

## Independent challenger hypotheses and meaningful cuts

**Hypothesis H1 — Displacer Kitten IN, Panharmonicon OUT (primary candidate for targeted comparison).** Displacer Kitten (3U, 2/2) can flicker up to one nonland permanent you control on each **cast** noncreature spell. This could transform low-cost rocks, tutors, or interaction into a one-time exile-and-return reset of an unearthed creature while retriggering an ETB, rather than merely doubling ETB once on every such entry. Against the incumbent, Panharmonicon enhances many creature ETBs immediately and needs **no follow-up noncreature cast or Kitten surviving**, while the 2/2 kitten is vulnerable to ordinary removal, and some spells want to be held for protection rather than spent as blink triggers. Kitten cannot save a creature if the noncreature spell is not cast before the exile event, and the effect does not target cards in graveyards. The add/cut is **not yet established as a net upgrade**. Check legal Oracle, sequencing, spell density, opponent answers, and presence of immediate ETB targets.

**Hypothesis H2 — Scholar of the Lost Trove IN, Etali, Primal Storm OUT (secondary).** A seven-mana blue graveyard payoff that casts a suitable artifact/instant/sorcery in graveyard without paying its mana cost on ETB would gain *immediate* value from Sedris's three-mana unearth, whereas six-mana Etali needs a successful attack trigger and exposes stolen exile-cast cards to opponent-dependent variance. However, Scholar demands a matching noncreature graveyard target, is comparatively narrow when the bin contains creatures alone, and Etali with unearth haste does get an immediate attack trigger without hitting a player. Need current Oracle and exact target/spell permissions before recommending either direction. This challenger is less certain than H1.

**Other alternatives:** Thassa, Deep-Dwelling as a repeatable one-body end-step blink effect (but overlaps Navigator, costs four, and its own end-step trigger ordering must be checked); additional cheap instant blink as a one-shot body reset (historical Ghostly Flicker hypothesis exposed administratively, therefore excluded from independent discovery credit). **Do not cut Sundial solely on assumption it fails:** the checked end-step rules support a live multi-creature preservation function. **Do not cut Convergence** without first accounting for the real B-only unearth economy. **Do not cut the graveyard enablers** merely to fit abstract card-advantage staples.

**Alternative incumbent cuts:** Panharmonicon (strong but board/ETB dependent); Etali (attack-dependent and high MV yet uniquely rewarding haste); Feldon (activating it also needs tapping/mana and copies, but provides commander-independent recursive access); Jin-Gitaxias, Core Augur (very expensive to hardcast, but an enormous single unearth payoff and source of disruptive card asymmetry). Comparing H1 against Panharmonicon is strategically closer than reflexively cutting a signature value creature.

## Counterplay and stress scenarios — qualitative, not measured tests

- **Turn four/five three-color access:** Opening hands with colorless Vault/Monolith and one or two lands can appear fast but still fail to make UBR on schedule; the 31-land source count needs matched-hand probability and mulligan audit. A good three-color rock hand may deploy Sedris earlier but still lack {2}{B} afterward.
- **Graveyard hate at the stack:** With Archon in the graveyard, Sedris active and three mana open, the opponent can exile Archon in response to unearth. No creature returns, and the three mana has been paid. Instant-speed broad graveyard exile is an especially serious failure mode.
- **Stax / removal:** Counter or remove Sedris before any activation, forcing eight mana for a recast; a retained Reanimate/Necromancy line can still function. A Voidwalker or Rest in Peace before Entomb changes payoff calculation and exposes overreliance on bin access.
- **Preservation window:** One unearthed Sepulchral Primordial reaches end step, its exile trigger goes on stack; a legal Sundial activation can exile the trigger but sacrifices remaining end-step utility. If the opponent responds to Sundial with removal of the creature, it leaves to exile instead and the preservation fails. With two unearthed creatures, the same activation can clear both eligible triggers if each is on stack.
- **Proposed Kitten:** With Kitten and an unearthed Archon on field, casting a cheap noncreature spell lets Kitten target/exile/return Archon, creating a fresh object with a new ETB. Opponent removal of Kitten before a noncreature spell is cast or removal of the target in response can stop this path. If only creature cards and mana rocks are drawn, the trigger density may be too low.

## Strongest counterargument and provisional judgment

The strongest argument **against** my leading Kitten proposal is that this is a graveyard-ETB deck already supporting numerous direct reanimations. Panharmonicon doubles the payoff of each ETB without spending a second spell, whereas Kitten is a fragile 2/2 that requires casting a noncreature spell while the right permanent is present. Kitten's promised one-shot unearth reset may be win-more when Deadeye, Sundial and Obeka already provide preservation. An optimizer that measures merely additional triggers without missed windows will overstate Kitten.
