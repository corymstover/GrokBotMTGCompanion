## Commander mechanic and identity

Admiral Beckett Brass is {1}{U}{B}{R}, a 3/3 Human Pirate that buffs **other** Pirates +1/+1. At the beginning of your end step, it can gain permanent control of a target nonland permanent belonging to a player who was dealt combat damage by **three or more distinct Pirates this turn**. The relevant three Pirates must hit the **same player**; three total hits spread over three opponents do not satisfy any one opponent's condition. Double strike by a single Pirate counts as one Pirate for the threshold. The commander need not have been present during the damage, but must be on board for its end-step trigger. The theft is not an until-end-of-turn effect.

This deck should maximize the realistic frequency of **three Pirate combat connections to a single opponent**, turn those connections into theft, draw/treasure, and protect the crew, not turn into generic UBR combo. Warhammer cascade, wheels, Breach/LED are not intrinsic to this commander. The natural ceiling is likely a high-powered optimized combat/tempo theft deck (working Bracket 4, not a proven cEDH-speed combo); this is an analytical estimate pending the existing project bracket source after sealing.

Primary rules source: Wizards official Ixalan Release Notes `https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/ixalan-release-notes`. Roaming Throne's official 2023 release notes `https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/the-lost-caverns-of-ixalan-release-notes`.

## Exact canonical 100 and structural review

The approved file contains **100 by quantity**, **97 distinct card names**, with Island x3 and Mountain x2 as the only repeats; one Abaddon-free Beckett commander and 99 library cards, Grixis UBR identity, no project exception. There are **35 lands**: three Island, two Mountain, one Swamp, three conventional fetches (Bloodstained Mire, Polluted Delta, Scalding Tarn), duals/tri sources, creature-restricted Cavern of Souls/Secluded Courtyard, one always-tapped Path of Ancestry, always-tapped Crumbling Necropolis and Xander's Lounge, and one direct colorless Ancient Tomb. Drowned Catacomb, Shipwreck Marsh, Stormcarved Coast and Sulfur Falls have conditional tap restrictions. These source counts do not amount to a modeled three-color turn-four cast rate.

**Early Pirate density and connectivity:** Changeling Outcast (evasive one-drop that counts as Pirate), Spectral Sailor, Siren Stormtamer, Spyglass Siren, Ragavan and other cheap bodies provide early attack lanes. Cover of Darkness, Zephyr Singer and Storm Fleet Sprinter threaten evasion, while Warkite Marauder can suppress blockers. Evasion on one attacker does not satisfy three-Pirate combat damage by itself: turn-three/four board spread, haste and blocker management matter. The presence of multiple very cheap Pirates means untapped early colored mana has outsized identity value, not just generic ramp value.

**Extra bodies, tribal payoff and theft layering:** Black Market Connections can generate changeling bodies, Daring Piracy generates temporary Pirate attackers, Coercive Recruiter temporarily steals and changes a creature into a Pirate, Double Down helps copy outlaw/Pirate spell development, and Roaming Throne choosing Pirate both qualifies as a Pirate and causes another Pirate's triggered ability (including Beckett's) to trigger an extra time. When an eligible same-player three-hit condition exists, two Beckett end-step instances can target two eligible nonlands. Crucially, Roaming Throne doesn't make two triggers if no legal Pirate/combat condition exists. Bident of Thassa and Kindred Discovery reward creature connection/attack with card volume; Malcolm, Keen-Eyed Navigator and treasure-making Pirates fuel deployment. Three hit attempts into blockers are still a gating resource.

**Response and recovery:** Deflecting Swat/Fierce Guardianship protect when commander controlled; Counterspell, Mana Drain, Cyclonic Rift, Chaos Warp, Feed the Swarm, Toxic Deluge, Fiery Cannonade and Raise the Palisade provide mixed interaction. Fiery Cannonade is designed to spare Pirates while clearing smaller non-Pirates; Toxic Deluge remains a symmetric emergency wipe even if it resets one's own attackers. Lightning Greaves safeguards the commander and supplies haste but its shroud can complicate friendly targeting. March of the Drowned and Admiral Brass, Unsinkable provide Pirate recursion, yet restoring **three separate attackers plus commander** after a wipe can be slow. Under Rule of Law, sweepers, ghostly prison/tax, or flying blockers, the stolen-permanent plan stalls; the deck should preserve ways to advance attackers and protect board position.

**Non-obvious attack mechanics:** Kari Zev's generated Ragavan token is **not automatically a Pirate** simply because Kari Zev is, whereas the legendary Ragavan card itself is a Monkey Pirate. The distinction matters when counting the three Pirates. Port Razer can grant additional combats on connection, but a single Pirate connecting multiple times during one turn still only counts as one of three for a given opponent under the Ixalan rulings; distinct Pirates must connect. The commander has no triggered theft at combat damage itself; it occurs at end step after opponents may answer Beckett.

**Risk categories:** the commander is combat-gated, a 4-mana threat with no native protection, asks to have three attackers connect with the *same* player, and makes its best theft after the combat window. Its unusual payoff remains high if realized (indefinite control of any eligible nonland permanent, potentially twice with Throne). Tradeoffs center on keeping low-curve Pirate pressure, evasion, enough protection and untapped early colors without bloating generic control.

## Candidate investigation with competing cuts

**Leading hypothesis — City of Brass IN / Crumbling Necropolis OUT.** The named pair was exposed in administrative metadata; evaluate on actual function. Both can produce each of U/B/R for any spell. City enters **untapped** and tapping it for mana inflicts one damage on its controller; Necropolis enters tapped but supplies tri-color mana without life cost on later turns. Beckett's turn-one evasive Pirate/turn-two enabler/turn-four UBR commander pattern directly values an untapped tri-color source in the first three turns, and City does not restrict mana to Pirate spells (unlike Cavern/Courtyard). This is an identity-relevant consistency/tempo challenger, not an arbitrary power card. If a hand contains City as its only land and a one-mana Pirate, it can cast that Pirate immediately; Necropolis cannot on the first turn. Later with sufficient land drops both produce the same color set, except City trades life each time. Strongest downside: in longer attrition games, City repeatedly costs life, and it is vulnerable to effects punishing damage; one tapped land may often be sequenced turn one with negligible cost. A static count does not prove a measured net win-rate change.

**Alternative lands:** Reflecting Pool also enters untapped and avoids life loss, but needs another suitable mana source and can be dead/off-color beside Ancient Tomb or type-restricted lands; Plaza of Heroes supports the commander/legend-heavy core and potential protection but casts ordinary nonlegend Pirate spells with conditional color, and Unclaimed Territory fixes Pirate creature casting but not the critical interaction/enchantments. City is more universal while protecting same UBR access. Already-present Mana Confluence illustrates that one-life rainbow sources are accepted in the baseline; this is relevant coherence, not a license to make all duals painful.

**Alternative cuts to City:** Path of Ancestry enters tapped but has commander-typed scry on casting Pirates, a *distinctive tribal draw-selection* effect; Xander's Lounge also enters tapped but has basic land types that some fetches can find and can cycle when flooded, giving it different late/early value. Drowned Catacomb is sometimes tapped but often untapped with a Swamp/Island, making it a smaller penalty than Necropolis while losing red. Secluded Courtyard is a creature-restricted source that supports Pirates and chosen tribe; cutting it would remove creature-density fixing while preserving slower noncreature mana. Crumbling is therefore the least strategically unique tapland if color-source equivalence is preserved.

**Nonland challenger — more evasive one-/two-mana Pirates versus high-end board engines.** A low-curve unblockable or flying Pirate could improve the three-hit test more than an expensive second theft engine, but it would cost real card quality. For example, The Indomitable (vehicle/resource support) and Bident of Thassa (four mana, depends on creatures connecting) are worth testing as *conditional* slots, while cutting Kindred Discovery, Roaming Throne, Cover of Darkness or cheap Pirates to add generic staples undermines unique commander leverage. A true candidate needs verified Pirate typing, evasion, mana, and a named cut; none independently establishes a strict improvement at this stage.

**Rejected generic shell direction:** substitute compact Breach/combo tutors for multiple Pirates just to elevate nominal competitive bracket. That reduces the probability of three different Pirate hits, strips theft identity and compares power outside the commander's natural ceiling. Keep premium interaction when it helps the pirates connect, not as a reason to transform the deck's objective.

## Precise mechanic trace and opponent answers

Example: on one combat step Pirate A/B/C each deal actual combat damage to **opponent P**. At beginning of your end step while Beckett is controlled, its triggered ability targets a nonland permanent P controls and if legal upon resolution transfers control indefinitely; if Throne chose Pirate, an additional triggered instance may target another eligible P-controlled nonland permanent. With A/B/C hitting three different opponents, the condition is not met and no theft target is eligible. If A alone has double strike and deals damage twice, it remains just one distinct Pirate for this threshold. If Beckett is removed between combat and end step, no triggered ability; if removed after triggers are on stack, that normally does not erase those stack objects, though legal targets must remain. If no valid nonland target exists (or control changes before resolution), theft fails. A control-change effect cannot retain an opponent-owned card once that opponent leaves the game.

Opponent counterplay: cheap blockers/tokens prevent at least one of three connections; single instant-speed removal on one attacker before damage lowers count; late removal of Beckett before end step denies theft; board wipes reset three-body assembly; Ghostly Prison-type taxes strain mana per attacker, and a faster combo table may end before three-Pirate damage. Cover of Darkness reduces many blockers but fear doesn't evade black/artifact creatures. City land speed may improve opening development but does not solve all these matchup problems.

## Strongest opposing interpretation

Even if City is a functional tempo upgrade, Necropolis guarantees painless three-color production and a tapland is sometimes strategically beneficial as a safe turn-one development with other one-drops absent. This list already runs Mana Confluence, 35 lands, eight traditional two-mana rocks and numerous early treasures; marginal improvement to color access may be too small to matter compared with the variance of drawing the required attackers, protection and opposing blockers. Overloading painlands can undermine the endurance needed to reach late end-step theft. A responsible KEEP is plausible if the source-oriented mana testing shows reliable early UBR through the present lands, or if City life paid is consequential in the target metagame.

Conversely, claiming the deck is optimized without reviewing early tapland friction is weak: Beckett's three-Pirate hit is a tempo objective, and two always-tapped nonfetchable/conditional tri sources can forfeit an early attacker. The cleanest candidate keeps all distinct Pirate cards unchanged while upgrading the timing of a broadly equivalent source.
