## Commander's identity and ceiling

Jeleva is a 4-mana Grixis 1/3 flying Vampire **Wizard**. When she **enters**, each player exiles the top X cards, where X is the *actual mana spent casting that Jeleva object*, including mana spent on commander tax. When she attacks, her triggered ability may **cast** one instant/sorcery spell exiled with that object without paying its mana cost. Free-cast value comes from rigging upcoming libraries, fast casting Jeleva with real mana spent, and granting her haste or protecting a full turn cycle. Higher tax makes subsequent casts more expensive but deepens the exile pool. Casting without paying the cost does not make the free spell a copy; spells have the normal cast consequences. This is not a universal theft commander or a passive spellslinger: resolving a four-mana creature without an immediate attack or a useful exiled spell can accomplish very little.

The 100 is a plausible **Bracket 5 cEDH/turbo-combo** strategy with a distinctive Jeleva attack line. High-mana-cost hit quality: `Peer into the Abyss`, `Mind's Desire`, `Mnemonic Betrayal`, `Doomsday`, `Yawgmoth's Will` and opposing instants/sorceries; `Doomsday` needs a legitimate pile and enough life/mana/draw to finish, not a standalone win. Efficient noncommander win access: `Thassa's Oracle` with `Demonic Consultation`/`Tainted Pact`, and `Underworld Breach` + `Lion's Eye Diamond` + `Brain Freeze` loops when graveyard, mana and opposing interaction permit. These backups improve competitive resilience but risk the deck presenting generic Grixis turbo while Jeleva is simply a delayed value line; native exile setup must remain material in any cut decision.

## Exact deck resources and failure windows

- **Mana:** 27 lands, many fetch/dual/rainbow sources, plus `Ancient Tomb`, `City of Traitors`, `Gemstone Caverns`, `Sol Ring`, fast Moxen/Petal/Vault/Monolith/LED, rituals, and three 2-mana fixing rocks. The precise distribution is aggressive and depends on mulligan quality, artifact availability and ritual sequencing. Red is often an early secondary requirement for haste/`Jeska's Will`; Jeleva needs **U+B+R plus a generic** at the same time. `Lion's Eye Diamond` normally sacrifices the hand when activated and is not free unconditional colored access for a Jeleva cast from hand. Colored-source reliability and keepable one/two-land openings have not been measured.
- **Curve:** the turbo shell contains one-mana tutor/filter/protection and mana acceleration, but also intentionally stranded high-MV payloads (`Peer into the Abyss`, `Mind's Desire`), 3-MV `Long-Term Plans`, `Mnemonic Betrayal`, `Yawgmoth's Will`, and the four-mana commander. A hand with several payloads, protection and no haste is vulnerable to losing a turn cycle.
- **Commander-native setup:** `Personal Tutor` topdecks a sorcery, `Scheming Symmetry` puts a chosen card on top of each selected library, while `Long-Term Plans` can put any chosen library card *third from top*; a normally cast Jeleva with X>=4 will exile that third card before an opponent can draw it, if the intervening order remains intact. `Brainstorm`, `Scroll Rack`, and `Sensei's Divining Top` manipulate what Jeleva sees. `Torch Courier`, `Lightning Greaves`, and mana from `Arena of Glory` give real ways to attack promptly. `Harmonic Prodigy` enhances a Wizard Jeleva's ETB and attack triggers when on field at their trigger events; duplicated ETBs may create different exile pools, and attack triggers may cast two spells, subject to legal choices and timing. An old Jeleva's exile pool is not freely inherited by a later new Jeleva object.
- **Answers / counterplay:** Countering Jeleva prevents her ETB and taxes future casts; removal before combat prevents new attack triggers; haste equipment is weak to instant-speed creature removal and artifact hate; exile replacement/land denial slows low-land keeps; `Drannith Magistrate` and `Rule of Law`-type restrictions obstruct free exile casts and storm lines; graveyard hate (`Endurance`, `Soul-Guide Lantern`, `Dauthi Voidwalker`) weakens Breach lines. A removed Jeleva after attack triggers are placed on the stack must be handled by precise last-known/exile-link rules rather than assuming all triggers disappear. `Force of Negation` cannot be cast for its alternate free cost on the controller's own turn; `Fierce Guardianship`/`Deflecting Swat` require commander control for alternate free cost. `Hexing Squelcher` helps prevent counters, not a universal protection shield against removal.
- **Free-cast details:** Jeleva's choice and casting happen when her attack trigger resolves, before blockers; ordinary sorcery timing permission is overridden by the ability, but additional costs, mandatory costs and casting restrictions still matter. `Mind's Desire` has storm because Jeleva *casts* it: count previously cast spells; storm copies are not cast, and a naked Jeleva-hit Desire does not automatically generate huge storm. `Mnemonic Betrayal` obtained free from Jeleva subsequently offers casts from opponents' graveyards under its own normal payment restrictions, not free casts of those graveyard cards.

## Adversarial challenger and cuts

**Strongest challenger hypothesis:** `+Intuition / -Long-Term Plans`. `Intuition` is also a 3-mana instant, can locate a 3-card package chosen by the pilot and put two into the graveyard, while one goes into hand; `Underworld Breach`/`Lion's Eye Diamond`/`Brain Freeze` form an apparent natural package with redundancy, but opponent choice, timing and post-search graveyard hate could stop an assumed guaranteed finish. Intuition is interactive, live without Jeleva and good with graveyard recursion; `Long-Term Plans` is slower as a conventional tutor and requires two draw steps if Jeleva is not next. This proposal tests the tension between independent cEDH resilience and native Jeleva exile engineering.

**Alternative cuts genuinely considered:** `Scheming Symmetry` (one-mana efficient Jeleva rig, but grants a chosen opponent a top tutor and exposes opponent-routed countermagic), `Mind's Desire` (can be dead in low-storm hands, yet is exactly the sort of premium free-cast spell Jeleva makes exciting), or `Fellwar Stone` (generic fixing and acceleration at 2 mana; losing it worsens 27-land colored-source reliability). None is safely inferior without a workload-aware opening-hand and effect trace; the preferred initial cut is **Long-Term Plans only as a challenger**, not a recommendation for immediate action.

**Counterargument against Intuition:** `Long-Term Plans` is one of the deck's only **unconditional third-from-top Jeleva exile tutors**; removing it could turn a carefully rigged commander strategy into another Breach/Oracle combo list. If Jeleva can be cast in the same sequence, 3 mana to place a high-MV spell third is a uniquely efficient way to cast a 7-MV spell for free on attack. Conversely, Intuition can fetch Breach/LED/Brain Freeze but puts only one in hand; without Breach online and sufficient mana/graveyard, the pile is neither deterministic nor automatically playable. A commander-first deck might legitimately prefer keeping Long-Term Plans despite lower generic tutor power.
