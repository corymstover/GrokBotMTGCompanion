## Decision-grade commander-first reasoning

Jeleva spends `{1}{U}{B}{R}` to cast, exiles X top cards from **every** library upon entering where X is **actual mana spent on that instance of the spell**; her declared attack casts one instant/sorcery from **that object's linked exile pool** without mana cost, before blockers. Cast-versus-copy, summoning sickness, linked abilities, alternate/additional costs and opponent priority materially constrain output. `Personal Tutor`, `Scheming Symmetry`, `Long-Term Plans`, `Brainstorm`, `Scroll Rack` and Top rig top cards to make Jeleva uniquely reliable; `Torch Courier`, `Lightning Greaves`, `Arena of Glory` pay for timely attacks; `Harmonic Prodigy` doubles her Wizard triggers when already present. Compact Oracle/Consultation/Pact and Breach/LED/Brain Freeze lines give competitive resilience, but removing the identity-specific tutor risks another generic Grixis list.

**Strongest contested add/cut:** `+Intuition / -Long-Term Plans` — **reject as immediate change**. Intuition's 2U opponent-chosen three-card pile is a strong graveyard-combo tool. Yet 2U Long-Term Plans places the selected sorcery at position three and a normally paid Jeleva X=4 captures it immediately; GrokBot's 'wait two draws' premise was incorrect. This is a proven functional distinction, but no measured outcome delta supports calling either globally better. A potential `+Intuition / -Scheming Symmetry` alternative saves Plans but sacrifices a fast B topdeck rig and needs testing. `-Mind's Desire` loses a hallmark free-hit and `-Fellwar Stone` endangers red/blue/black in 27 lands; neither is a clean substitute.

**Separate commander-native challenger:** `+Galvanic Iteration / -Borne Upon a Wind`. Jeleva really **casts** its free spell, so a previously cast Galvanic Iteration can copy that spell; a copy is **not** cast and doesn't raise storm count. Added UR tempo and response windows matter. A copied Mnemonic Betrayal may be redundant, a copied Doomsday dangerous, and Borne provides off-turn flexibility independent of Jeleva. The old authored review offered reasoned cases, not win-rate proof. Preserve Borne while testing.

## Practical opponent response and risk

1. **Before entry:** opponent counters Jeleva or strips U/B/R; a four-mana 1/3 doesn't create the card-exile engine. Paid tax may increase future X, but strains fast resources.
2. **Before declaring attack:** targeted or untargeted removal can strand the linked exile cohort; no new attack trigger. Haste tools remain conditional and fragile.
3. **Once attack trigger is on stack:** removal does not automatically erase the trigger, but legal spell selection is tied to the correct Jeleva exile set; `Drannith Magistrate`/Rule of Law-like restrictions can stop some casts. `Force of Negation` cannot free-pitch on the Jeleva controller's own attack turn.
4. **After attack free cast:** opponent countermagic/interaction can counter the spell if not otherwise protected; copied spells have distinct target/stack behavior. Ad Nauseam, Peer and Doomsday all consume life; grave hate can break Breach/Intuition, and 27 lands plus artifact dependence makes rebuild fragile.
