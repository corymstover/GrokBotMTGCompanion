## Commander-first strategic verdict

Six-mana 7/6 Sauron, the Dark Lord grows an Orc Army when **an opponent casts** a spell, converts Army **combat damage to a player** into a Ring temptation, then optionally discards the entire hand and draws four whenever the Ring tempts its controller. The approved 100 integrates seven Nazgûl Wraith counter triggers, Ring-bearer enabling, The Great Goblin's Orc/Goblin counter damage, Orc Army attack enablers, Sheoldred/Archfiend of Ifnir payoffs for draw/discard, and the selectable Wraith-versus-Avatar Roaming Throne. This naturally supports resilient midgame optimized Bracket 4, not a generic cEDH storm transfer. Army connectivity, colored mana UBR by turn six, turns required to establish Ring events, opponents who avoid casting on Sauron's watch, removal and full-wipe recovery remain actual constraints. Ward asks the targeting opponent to sacrifice a legendary artifact/creature; it is not universal protection against sweepers or opponents with disposable legendary permanents.

## Preferred challenger, interaction boundaries and strongest objection

**ADD Arcane Adaptation ({2}U); CUT Saruman, the White Hand ({1}UBR).** Adaptation naming Wraith causes existing Orc Army, Sauron, The Great Goblin and other creatures we control to have Wraith in addition to existing types. Each Nazgûl triggers independently when the Ring tempts us and puts one counter on *each Wraith*, meaning the converted Army now receives a counter from each live Nazgûl. The existing Roaming Throne naming Wraith doubles these Nazgûl triggers, and with Adaptation converts both Sauron and The Great Goblin into compatible Wraiths for the same Throne choice. It is not equivalent to forcing Throne always to name Avatar, nor an infinite loop. The Great Goblin deals a fixed TWO damage for each applicable counter-placement **event**, not 2 per counter or per creature affected.

A deterministic **one Ring temptation sourced independently from Sauron**, with three Nazgûl, one Army, The Great Goblin, Arcane Adaptation on Wraith, and no Roaming Throne: three Nazgûl counter events each grow Army once and generate three Great Goblin triggers (**six** total damage if legal targets and abilities remain). Without Adaptation the Army is not a Wraith and receives none of those Nazgûl counters. Add Roaming Throne choosing Wraith: six counter events grow Army and The Great Goblin's now-Wraith triggers are each doubled, giving twelve separately targeted two-damage events (**24 total damage**, subject to responses and correct triggering order). If the original Ring temptation is produced by Army combat damage while Sauron is a Wraith with Wraith-naming Throne, Sauron's trigger itself is doubled, making **two Ring temptations**: do not conflate that with the single independent temptation trace or claim the 24-damage trace is the final output in that distinct state. Creatures, Nazgûl, Sauron and the enchantment must actually be cast/retained; these high-end states are **not** measured wins.

**Strongest counterargument:** Saruman adds independent Orc amass on our own noncreature spellcasts and ward {2} to Orcs/Goblins. It meaningfully develops/protects an Army in games where opponents stop casting or where Ring-bearer damage is blocked, whereas Adaptation costs a card/mana, requires existing Nazgûl and Ring events, does not increase token size absent that engine, and is removable at instant speed before Nazgûl counter triggers resolve, preventing Orc Army from being a Wraith at resolution. Sacrificing Saruman for win-more multi-piece upside may decrease early Army access and defensive uptime. The alternative **+Arcane Adaptation/-Sauron, Lord of the Rings** keeps Saruman but removes a flavorful expensive on-cast mass-amass/mill/reanimation recovery line; curve benefits are not proof of net improvement. Both are genuine opportunity costs, so **no unconditional upgrade** is asserted.
