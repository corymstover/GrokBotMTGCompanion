## Commander-specific plan and natural ceiling

Admiral Brass mills four on entering. At the beginning of combat on each of OUR turns she can return a TARGET Pirate creature card from our graveyard with a finality counter, base power/toughness 4/4 and haste until end of turn. The deck is therefore a Pirate graveyard-tempo and combat-value strategy, not merely Pirate tribal swarm or generic Grixis reanimator. Strong return targets include Angrath's Marauders (damage amplification), Port Razer (conditional extra combats), Hostage Taker (interactive ETB), Coercive Recruiter (temporary theft), and other Piratical value engines. Entomb, Buried Alive, Faithless Looting and Brass's own mill put targets into the graveyard; Reanimate, Animate Dead, Victimize, Living Death and Patriarch's Bidding recover creatures through alternate routes. Shared Animosity, Wonder in the graveyard with an Island, and Pirate combat triggers convert board access into pressure.

Working ceiling: optimized Bracket 4 creature-centric recursive combat, subject to further bracket verification; not justified as a Bracket 5 deterministic cEDH shell. The deck can produce explosive high-damage turns but normally needs a five-mana commander, available Pirate card, combat phase, board presence, and opponents unable to fully interrupt. Preserving the Pirate-type restriction and graveyard-to-combat tempo is central.

## Mana / curve / tempo stress

34 lands plus Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, three two-color Signets, three Talismans, Ancient Tomb, Jeska's Will and treasure-producing Pirates provide a functional acceleration skeleton for a five-mana commander. The fetch/dual/shock/triome configuration has extensive U/B/R access; Cavern of Souls/Secluded Courtyard require correct tribal use (do not assume colored mana is unrestricted). Crumbling Necropolis, Path of Ancestry and Xander's Lounge enter tapped under ordinary conditions, and some other duals are conditionally untapped. Specific turn-three/four/five U/B/R sequence probabilities have NOT been measured. Double-blue Counterspell/Mana Drain and four-mana The Indomitable impose blue requirements while black tutors/reanimation and red combat payoffs impose early multi-color demands. An untested colorless utility land is not a free inclusion in this 34-land shell.

High-end value creatures are acceptable when being cheated into play by Brass, but hand-casting Angrath's Marauders without enabling the graveyard is slow. Kindred Discovery and several 4–5 mana Pirates/vehicles also compete with casting/protecting Brass. Opponent commander removal increases recast costs and substantially reduces the deck's power before combat.

## Strongest adversarial challenger hypotheses (not accepted swaps)

1. Add Nesting Grounds / cut Crumbling Necropolis, or compare another least-cost tapped utility land. Nesting Grounds can move a finality counter off our revived Pirate onto another permanent during our main phase for 1 mana and tapping the land. A land-based repeatable reset could outlast artifact removal; however this loses a tri-color source, offers only colorless mana, is sorcery-speed, requires another target, and may be worse at casting Brass. Most consequential potential challenger; do NOT presume net gain without U/B/R access testing.
2. Add Conjurer's Closet / cut Admiral Beckett Brass as a low-baseline-fit hypothesis, with Merchant Raiders as an alternative cut. Closet can exile a revived Pirate at our end step and return it as a NEW object without the finality counter, potentially repeating its ETB; its five-mana cost, no immediate combat effect and reliance on keeping a creature until end step make it vulnerable and potentially inferior to an on-theme Pirate. It removes the 4/4 base-setting effect on the blinked new object. Admiral Beckett Brass's three-different-Pirates-damage condition is not the commander engine, but it is a real Pirate theft/combat subtheme, not an automatic bad card.
3. Keep Power Conduit: its tap/remove-a-counter activation can take a finality counter off our permanent as a COST, then place a charge counter on an artifact or +1/+1 counter on a creature using the chosen target. It needs legal target for the chosen mode, survives only if it can be activated, and removes at most one counter per untap under ordinary conditions. Do not assume every reanimated Pirate can be saved simultaneously or that Power Conduit is itself a Pirate reanimation target.
4. No change: preserve all existing role compression, fast lands and high-impact reanimation; the apparent weaknesses may be intrinsic commander constraints rather than poor card selection.

Plausible competing cuts for the expensive Closet include Admiral Beckett Brass and Merchant Raiders, but both have positive Pirate synergies; cutting a draw engine, 2-mana rock, mill/tutor, or efficient interaction is substantially less defensible. Nesting Grounds also might replace a redundant colorless utility land—none is obvious from this 34-land source list. A second finality-removal tool is an engine resilience option, not a demonstrated replacement for colored land quality.

## Timing, zones, and contested interactions

- Brass's triggered ability begins at the start of OUR combat, targets a Pirate creature CARD in our graveyard on the stack, and its target may be removed in response. The returned Pirate enters AFTER the trigger resolves with the finality counter and haste, as a 4/4 base creature. Casting Brass only after combat began does not retroactively generate the trigger; instant interaction can remove Brass before the beginning-of-combat trigger would occur.
- With an extra combat phase that begins on our turn, Brass can trigger again if on battlefield at that phase's beginning. Port Razer must deal combat damage to a PLAYER for an extra combat, and cannot attack a player it already attacked this turn; it is not an unconditional infinite combat machine. Roaming Throne choosing Pirate doubles qualifying Pirate-triggered abilities, but Throne itself is not a Pirate CREATURE CARD in the graveyard, even though it is Pirate on the battlefield after its type choice.
- A finality counter is a replacement effect if its permanent would move from battlefield to graveyard, not a universal exile on bounce, blink, or phasing. Removing it before the death event restores normal graveyard destination. If finality is present on a would-die permanent, ordinary death-recursion lines cannot assume it hit the graveyard. The counters do not erase themselves on a simple phase out/in.
- Nesting Grounds's ability is sorcery speed and TARGETS both source and destination permanents; it can be disrupted. Power Conduit removes its counter as activation COST, which cannot be undone by responding once paid, but the activated ability and its target can still be interacted with. Closet's flicker returns a new object, breaking old targeting/counters and original base-P/T overwrite, but can be answered before end step or in response to the triggered ability.
- Living Death affects opponents too: graveyard exiles then board sacrifice then return exiled creatures, so treating it as a one-sided Pirate mass reanimation is unwarranted. Wonder requires an Island and Wonder in OUR graveyard for flying, not merely Wonder existing in the deck.

## Counterplay / scenario challenges

A graveyard exile response to Brass's triggered target (e.g. instant-speed graveyard hate) can prevent a key return. Destroying/exiling Brass before beginning combat denies that turn's trigger; removing her after trigger is on stack does not necessarily stop the ability, but may make future turns fail. A single spot removal spell can exile a finality-marked Pirate permanently unless its counter was removed first. Graveyard hate, repeated creature sweepers, taxing commander removal, noncombat prevention/forts, and opposing fliers each expose distinct weaknesses. In a goldfish with a ready graveyard, extra-combat Port Razer looks spectacular; versus open mana and grave-hate it is conditional, not guaranteed. Closet and Nesting Grounds must be judged under these adversarial windows, not just uninterrupted play.

## Best counterargument to the preferred provisional conclusion

The strongest argument AGAINST assigning NEEDS TESTING is that 34 lands with full tricolor access, high tutor density, multiple standalone reanimation spells and an existing finality-removal Power Conduit may already be near the commander's realistic ceiling. Nesting Grounds makes a demanding five-mana U/B/R opener less reliable; Conjurer's Closet delays pressure and competes with commander mana. A KEEP today is rational if their marginal failure-state improvements do not beat their tempo costs. Conversely, claiming KEEP without testing could miss the real weakness of one finality-counter answer in removal-heavy pods. No observed win rates, mana simulations, or effect-aware paired trials support either strong claim.
