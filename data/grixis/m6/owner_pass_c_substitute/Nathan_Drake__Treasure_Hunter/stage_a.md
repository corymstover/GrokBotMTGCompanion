## Native identity, natural ceiling and victory path

`Nathan Drake, Treasure Hunter` is **{U}{B}{R}**, a **3/2 legendary Human Rogue** with **first strike**. He permits mana to be spent as though any color for spells **we do not own** and activations of permanents **we control but do not own**. **Whenever he attacks**, the top card of **each player's library** is exiled; **one spell from among those cards may be CAST** as part of resolving that trigger, with its normal payable cost. This is not 'play any of four cards later for free': lands cannot be played via the one-spell permission, unchosen cards receive no lasting Nathan permission, and a chosen spell must have a payable total cost. The special instruction allows casting during the trigger's resolution, including sorceries/creatures, with legal choices and real available mana. Copying his attack trigger may allow one separately paid spell per resolving copy.

The commander-first plan is deliberately **combat-driven, multi-library heist and payoff**, not ordinary Grixis Storm or a list that happens to have powerful theft cards. On attack Nathan samples several libraries; protection/evasion keeps his next attack alive, treasure/firebending/limited special-purpose mana finance spells at combat, trigger copying and additional combat phases provide repeated heists, and `Passionate Archaeologist`, `Nalfeshnee`, `Wild-Magic Sorcerer`, `Prosper`, `Don Andres`, `Tasha` and other cards reward using opponents' cards. Secondary theft engines maintain value through commander removal, though they do not replicate the exact attack/one-immediate-cast event.

Historical blueprint bracket is **4 — Optimized** and remains a reasonable *provisional natural ceiling*. A three-mana UBR commander can snowball quickly with attack-trigger/extra-combat support, but must resolve, receive haste or wait a turn, attack without losing functional access, reveal a castable spell, and hold mana at **combat**; exile volume is not an observed or deterministic win rate. Generic cEDH turbo combos that bypass heists would reduce identity rather than raise Nathan's genuine native ceiling.

## Primary off-packet challenge and alternative cuts

**Provisional first-pass: NEEDS TESTING — ADD `Dolmen Gate` / CUT `Dark Ritual`.**

`Dolmen Gate` is a {2} colorless artifact whose Oracle text prevents **all combat damage dealt to attacking creatures we control**. Its relevance is specifically Nathan's **attack trigger**, which occurs whether he deals damage or not. If Nathan attacks each turn through a big blocker and remains alive, he provides another four-card multi-library heist on the next turn; Gate supports other attack-based theft creatures, `Etali`, `Grenzo`, `Thief of Sanity` and treasure-producing attackers as well. Gate can be deployed for two before Nathan's third-turn arrival, asks no U/B/R pips, and preserves a crowded combat even if Regalia is equipped to a different attacker. Cutting `Dark Ritual` relinquishes only one burst of **BBB**, which cannot alone fix U and R and does not stay around to fund successive combat-trigger spells. This is an identity-positive *survivability* hypothesis, not generic cEDH conversion.

**Strongest counterargument:** Gate does **not** grant haste, evasion or protection against exile/destroy before attackers; if opponents remove Nathan in response to his attack trigger, the trigger exists but Nathan's on-battlefield mana-color replacement is gone when the exiled spell is cast. Already included `Brotherhood Regalia` makes the commander unblockable and gives ward 2 for a cheap equip; `Greaves`, `Boots`, `Shizo` and first strike reduce the marginal combat casualty cases. Gate does not increase the number of spells Nathan can cast per trigger or pay their costs, whereas Ritual can fund an early black-heavy permanent, leave mana after deploying support, or help play through tax in some hands. Removing a fast one-shot for a defensive two-drop may **decrease** successful third-turn UBR deployment or post-attack casting despite greater combat survival. No head-to-head observed opponent game supports the swap yet, and `Dolmen Gate` adds another nonland artifact vulnerable to artifact sweepers (its static prevention ability, however, is **not** disabled by Null Rod).

**Competing cuts / candidate tests:** `-Stolen Strategy` would lower a slow value enchantment and retain Ritual, but removes an excellent **commander-absent**, three-opponent exile value channel not requiring combat. `-Dream-Thief's Bandana` avoids another combat-damage-dependent card but removes long-duration cast permission that compensates for Nathan's single immediate choice. `-Etali, Primal Storm` removes six-mana redundancy but discards a free, multi-library attack payoff and strong post-commander plan. These are not cleaner cuts. `Port Razer` for a high-end value slot and `Key to the City` for Ritual offer other extra-combat/evasion hypotheses; they have **not** passed exact current Oracle and cost/connection validation in this neutral stage and must not be represented as validated improvements. `Dolmen Gate / Dark Ritual` is the one exact comparator worth a bounded test if opponents commonly block rather than remove Nathan before combat.

## Paid timing, opponent response and failure-state stress

1. **Baseline turn-3/4 mana:** With U, B, R lands in play on T3, Nathan costs all three and normally cannot attack until T4. At the T4 attack trigger the top of **four** libraries is exiled (in a four-player pod). With four untapped mana, one selected 0–4-cost spell may be paid and cast **during** resolution; lands are not castable. If all four are lands or too expensive, **no spell** is cast and the exiled cards do not remain available via Nathan alone. The mana model's T3 colored-presence proxy is not a mulligan/attack chance. Gate T2 competes with early signets/equipment; Ritual may net two black but still needs U/R for Nathan.
2. **Mana and trigger stacking:** If Fire Nation Palace was legally activated to give Nathan firebending 4 before attackers, the attack-generated red mana may be ordered to resolve **before** Nathan's exile/cast trigger; while Nathan survives, that red may satisfy any color of an opponent-owned spell in this same combat. Order the triggers the opposite way or have Nathan removed before permission and one may lose this route. `Thieving Varmint` mana is restricted to spells not owned, `Lotus Petal` sacrifices, and `Sol Ring` provides colorless. A duplicated Nathan trigger can let us choose **up to two spells** across independent resolutions, but both still need real mana.
3. **Opponent reaction windows:** Nathan removed **before attackers** produces no trigger. Nathan removed **after he attacks while trigger is on stack** does not erase the trigger but may erase his unique color-spending permission; respond with currently legal mana/protection as needed. A blocker can kill Nathan later in combat after the triggered spell was cast but before the next turn's repeat. Gate answers only that combat damage. `Brotherhood Regalia` solves blocking if equipped but still faces artifact removal; `Greaves` shroud conflicts with later target equips. A mass wipe/reset or graveyard interaction can still strand expensive theft payoffs.
4. **Spell cast vs copy:** Azula copying a spell cast during attack does not itself count as casting a second spell, so it does not double cast-trigger `Passionate Archaeologist` by itself. `Nalfeshnee` can copy eligible **cast-from-exile** spells each time its trigger occurs; any such spell copies are not cast. `Wild-Magic Sorcerer` cascade on first exile cast can generate an additional cast that obeys cascade rules and its actual mana-value condition, not Nathan's one-spell restriction. `Roaming Throne` choosing Rogue creates two separate Nathan attack triggers; `Firebender Ascension` only conditionally creates a copy after enough quest counters. Do not collapse all these into 'four free spells every attack.'
5. **Board/interaction comparison:** (A) Nathan with Regalia and an empty attacking lane gains effectively nothing from Gate—Ritual's earlier tempo can dominate; (B) Nathan without equipment facing a single 5/5 ground blocker can declare attack and still cast one exile spell, then usually dies to block (first strike 3 damage is not lethal to 5/5), whereas Gate preserves Nathan for the next turn but does not push combat damage; (C) facing Swords to Plowshares or Cyclonic Rift on attack trigger, Gate cannot help and the lack of on-board Nathan may deny off-color spell cast; (D) attacking with Etali and other theft creatures simultaneously Gate can save several bodies if all survive precombat removal, a strong native upside that can't be measured from the deck list alone.
6. **Hate and resource ceiling:** Graveyard replacement/exile effects can alter grave-based theft and `Dauthi Voidwalker` interactions, but not Nathan's top-library exile. Rule of Law can limit spells cast and might consume capacity before the attack; Drannith Magistrate disallows casting from exile for the affected controller, Null Rod can disable artifact mana/equipment activations but not Gate's static prevention. Creatures casting during Nathan trigger can be countered normally, and a stolen card's owner retains ownership even if we cast it.
