## Commander-first identity and natural ceiling

Kefka's front face is `{2}{U}{B}{R}`, 4/5 Human Wizard: *entering or attacking* makes **each player**, including controller, discard **a** card if able, then controller draws one card **for each different card type** among all cards actually discarded by that one ability. Four discarded single-type cards all creatures yield just **one draw**, not four; an artifact-creature discard can contribute two types. Basic/legendary/snow are supertypes, not counted card types. Opponents choose their own cards and may empty their hands before attack; drawing may fall to zero. `Harmonic Prodigy` makes Kefka Wizard triggers happen a second time (two distinct rounds, not a single doubled draw based on identical cards). Discarding own `Anger` supports haste if its owner controls a real Mountain subtype land; `Waste Not` rewards **opponents'** discard by type, and `Tinybones, Bauble Burglar` stashes opponent discards for later mana-paid use (it can also leave fewer cards for `Mnemonic Betrayal` to exploit). Repeated Kefka ETB from `Essence Flux`, `Displacer Kitten`, cloning and Saw in Half adds repeat pressure. `Gogo, Mysterious Mime` can copy Kefka at the *beginning* of combat, retaining the separate name Gogo to avoid the legend rule, grants both haste and requires attacks if able; if Kefka still present, two Kefka-pattern attack triggers can occur when attackers are declared.

The eight-generic **sorcery-speed** transformation `{8}` makes each opponent sacrifice a permanent they choose *and then transforms Kefka* to 5/7 flying Avatar Wizard. The transformed face draws cards whenever an opponent loses life **during your turn** equal to life lost; losing 4 life is four cards, not one (may be catastrophic under opponent life-loss prevention/Notion Thief-type draw replacement). `Orcish Bowmasters` can generate life-loss pressure; many normal combat lines do too. Transformation demands eight mana *in addition to* already having Kefka on board: it is a resilient late-game ceiling, not a credible normal turbo turn-two engine or an automatic transform from doing discard. Gogo copying the back face has a different copied ability, only after valid transformation; it doesn't itself pay the eight.

The deck is an owner-approved high-power **Bracket 5 cEDH** strategy with turbo cEDH kill packages (`Thassa's Oracle` + Consultation/Pact, `Underworld Breach` + LED/Brain Freeze), mana rocks/rituals and Ad Nauseam, while Kefka's forced symmetric discard/diverse card-type draws, self-discard haste, multiple ETBs and Gogo pressure make it distinguishable from a generic combo list. It has a real native value ceiling, but reaching Kefka at five mana plus protecting/swinging through a table is substantially slower than the compact wins. Avoid cutting commander-specific effects just to concentrate generic cards.

## Mana and resource reality

- 28 lands, 1 basic Island, numerous untapped fetch/dual/rainbow sources plus `Starting Town`, `Cavern of Souls`, `Arena of Glory`, `Ancient Tomb` and `City of Traitors`. Untapped-color access is not guaranteed by the sheer count: Kefka needs **U+B+R simultaneously**, plus two generic; City/Tomb/Monolith/Vault/Sol Ring mainly supply generic, Cavern can fix one colored creature pip if Wizard or Human is named, and `Mox Opal` needs metalcraft, `Mox Diamond` needs another land discarded. `Gemstone Caverns` depends on pregame condition. 28-lands Ad Naus turbo has risk of one-land hands and stranded 5-mana commander when answers consume rocks.
- Haste from `Anger` in grave needs control of a land with the **Mountain subtype** (e.g. Badlands/Blood Crypt/Steam Vents/Volcanic Island), not merely a land that taps for R; `Arena of Glory` grants haste if its restricted exert mana helps **cast Kefka**, and misses a later untap. If `Anger` goes to graveyard from Kefka ETB, haste can enable immediate attack in the same turn before moving to combat; if not discarded from hand, the 4MV creature is stranded. Gogo can force haste/attack only after its own combat-start ability resolves and may still be too late to gain an extra first-turn Kefka ETB.
- Other native resources: `Diabolic Intent` needs creature sacrifice, `Culling the Weak` sacrifices creature to get BBBB, `Saw in Half` destroys a creature and only makes copies if it *dies this way*. Opposing exile, indestructible and removal can make it fail. Casting `Demonic Counsel` for 1B without four types in graveyard searches **only a Demon**; there is no obvious Demon creature among the exact approved names, so it may be a nonfunctional pre-delirium spell, not an unconditional tutor. Kefka's discard and sorcery/instant/artifact spills can eventually meet four types, but timing matters.
- Game-ending protection must respect exact timing: `Force of Negation` zero-pitch only on an opponent's turn, `Fierce Guardianship` free mode only while commander controlled, `Pact of Negation` has next-upkeep mana/loss debt. `Final Fortune` is an extra-turn gamble with delayed game loss. `Valley Floodcaller` makes noncreature spells flash but **does not** make Kefka's sorcery-speed transform activation legal at instant speed.

## Provisional challengers and actual contested slots

**Best neutral challenger: `+Entomb / -Demonic Counsel`** (one-mana instant vs two-mana sorcery). `Entomb` can put `Anger` into graveyard before Kefka arrives, turning on immediate attack with a Mountain subtype source; can select other reanimation/Breach resources, as well as add a chosen card type to graveyard. In a shell where grave-type delirium often comes on late, Demonic Counsel at 1B has a real **floor of failing to tutor an accessible card** if four distinct types aren't present. Entomb advances a Kefka-native haste/discard loop without requiring Kefka on the battlefield. This is **a hypothesis**, not a strict upgrade: replacing Counsel removes a premium `Demonic Tutor` analogue *once* delirium is online and has less flexibility for Oracle/Consultation/interaction in hand. An early Entomb cannot tutor a spell to hand and may expose Anger to grave hate or be useless without a Mountain.

**Alternative cuts:** `Mnemonic Betrayal` can be underpowered after Tinybones has already stashed opponent discards and demands available mana to cast grave spells, yet it offers extraordinary free late-game opponent resources in typical pods; `Borne Upon a Wind` overlaps Valley Floodcaller's instant-speed noncreature permission, yet is an instant costing only 1U, can function without a three-mana 2/2 surviving, and supports off-turn wins; `Fellwar Stone` is an apparently generic two-mana rock but colored Kefka mana in 28 lands argues strongly to keep it. `Gogo` is an expensive body but strongly commander-native; cutting it to force conventional turbo homogenizes the deck. `Tinybones` itself competes for slots but is a commander-trigger payoff, not simply filler.

**Best counterargument against my favorite:** Kefka naturally self-discards on ETB, fueling `Demonic Counsel`'s delirium alongside fetchland, Chrome Mox cards, creatures, rituals and enchantments; late Counsel then selects **any** missing win/protection piece directly into hand for 1B. `Entomb` cannot do this and may pay B to set up an Anger haste line that still requires a Mountain, 5 Kefka mana and a safe attack. The list already contains `Anger` to discard from hand, Gogo haste and `Arena of Glory`; Entomb may be redundant while Counsel supplies coveted universal tutor redundancy. No scoring or sample played hands was used to force a conclusion.

## Rules and adversarial test conditions

1. **Kefka ETB with Prodigy:** if opponents and controller all have cards, first triggered discard yields its own types/draw; second repeats with newly chosen cards. If player hand now empty, that player does not discard second time; cannot count four guaranteed cards per trigger. Kefka's *own* discarded card goes to owner's graveyard before draw unless a replacement effect intervenes.
2. **Saw in Half on Kefka:** Kefka must be destroyed and actually **die** for tokens to appear. Two copied **legendary Kefkas** enter; ETB abilities trigger, then state-based legend rule requires one to remain before triggers are put on stack. Both triggered abilities remain pending, but do not call this two permanent living Kefkas. If controller routes actual original commander from graveyard to command zone, the trigger history still depends on actual zone events. Delirium and resources must be reconsidered after death.
3. **Gogo:** its combat-start copy resolves before attackers are chosen, gives haste to itself and chosen Kefka and attack requirement if able. Because its **name differs**, both can coexist while copied. No extra Kefka *ETB* occurs merely because Gogo copies, nor does a Kefka not declared as attacker trigger.
4. **Tinybones vs Mnemonic:** Tinybones's discard trigger exiles from opponents' graveyard with stash counters after discard; it still lets Kefka see the card types actually discarded, because Kefka examines the event it caused. `Mnemonic Betrayal` later cannot target/stage cards already in exile; Tinybones still requires normal payment and own-turn permission to cast those cards.
5. **Interaction:** `Drannith Magistrate`, Rule of Law, graveyard exile, repeated commander counters before Kefka can ETB, fast opposing combos, and low-life Ad Nauseam hands all can stop native plans. Even if the first Kefka ETB happens, not every player having a card to discard means draw can be 0.
