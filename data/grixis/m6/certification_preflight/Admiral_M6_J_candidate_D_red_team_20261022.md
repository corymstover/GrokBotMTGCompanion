## Hardest concrete threats to the five-swap portfolio

1. **Cut Forerunner versus Broadside:** Forerunner of the Coalition searches a **Pirate card to TOP of library** on entry, then drains **each** opponent 1 life whenever another Pirate enters. With Forerunner remaining on board for three Daring Piracy combat token creations, three opponents each lose 3 life (9 combined), including turns when all tokens are blocked. This damage **does not** count as Admiral's required Pirate combat damage. Broadside's three-mana haste/menace Pirate more readily contributes *this turn's* attacker and has conditional removal/reach, but sacrifices another artifact/creature to boast; a Treasure's mana value 0 yields **only 2 damage**, and a sacrificed Arcane Signet may yield 4 at real ramp cost. Menace still loses to two legal blockers. Forerunner is plausibly stronger at slow attrition tables and under permanent token engines; D favors immediate three-source window. Judgment: **SELECT Broadside over Forerunner, moderate confidence**, not empirically proven. If the local game is about repeated life-drain rather than early theft, revisit the research choice before owner approval.
2. **Cut Poisoner versus Skyship:** Dire Fleet Poisoner's flash/deathtouch and entry pump+deathtouch for an *already attacking* Pirate are real interaction. Skyship Plunderer instead flies over ground blockers and can add a kind of counter that already exists, including ordering its trigger before Malcolm, Alluring Scoundrel's chorus trigger. It cannot create the first counter of a kind; if a flier or reach creature blocks Skyship, Beckett's third hit may fail. A flashed Poisoner does not retroactively join the current attack, but can block or trade with a large non-first-strike attacker. Judgment: **SELECT Skyship** in ordinary ground-stalled theft planning, **retain Poisoner** as a legitimate alternative under aggro/flying-heavy tables.
3. **Cut Captain versus Cloud:** Dire Fleet Captain's attack-trigger power rises with the number of other attacking Pirates and can quickly become a secondary lethal threat. Cloud Pirates is only a 1/1 flying body and can block only flyers. More power from one attacker **does not substitute for three distinct Pirates connecting**; the flying one-drop improves early source presence, although flyers/reach and removal turn it off. Judgment: **SELECT Cloud**, moderate confidence, accepting a real damage-race and defensive cost.
4. **Cut Saboteur versus Deckhand:** Daring Saboteur's 2U unblockability activation and on-hit draw/discard can provide both evasion and filtering when spare mana exists. Departed Deckhand's body is naturally hard to block for only 1U and can pay 3U to help **another** creature, but sacrifices itself whenever targeted by a **spell**, including friendly protection spells. It remains blockable by Spirits; equipment **equip ability** is not a spell. Judgment: **SELECT Deckhand** to reduce recurring three-mana evasion tax during the commander turn, but don't present it as truly unblockable or indestructible.
5. **City versus Necropolis:** City of Brass can supply a fourth immediately usable source to cast Admiral on schedule; Necropolis entered tapped. City's tap-trigger damage can accumulate in longer games and interacts adversely with opponent aggression. Other conditional lands and typed fetches do not make ideal colors automatic. Judgment: **SELECT City** for precise UBR development and accept measurable *per-use* life cost, not a measured probability advantage.

## Threat sequences the existing fixtures explicitly disallow

- Two Pirates damaging an opponent, three Pirates each damaging *different* opponents, noncombat Forerunner life loss, or duplicate damage by only one Pirate **cannot** satisfy Admiral's one-opponent/three-distinct-Pirates theft requirement. Fog and blockers that remove any necessary third hit defeat it.
- A single opposing flying blocker may intercept Cloud or Skyship; a creature with reach can do likewise; a Spirit can block Departed. All three are real counterplay. The new finite assignment fixture shows two ground blockers are insufficient against the three proposed D evasive bodies, while a flyer, reach or Spirit individually can reduce D's hits to two. It does not assign deckwide connection frequencies.
- Removing Admiral before the beginning-of-endstep trigger or preventing legal nonland targets matters. Removing Admiral **after a trigger has already been put on the stack** does not by itself cancel that triggered ability; no game-wide stack simulator was run.
- A Broadside boast cannot precede attacking, happen more than once per turn, consume its own body as "another", or sacrifice a Treasure already spent for mana. An activated boast does not retroactively count a creature as combat damage.
- Malcolm/Plunderer free-cast requires both to connect, Malcolm to have enough **existing** chorus counters, correct trigger order, and an actually discarded castable card. No free spell is assumed from a blank hand.
- A/B research alternatives sacrifice **both Merchant Raiders**' persistent blocker lock and **The Indomitable**'s repeated combat draw; C gives up **Open into Wonder**'s scalable unblockability/draw. D retains all three. This is a real whole-100 synergy distinction, not a cherry-picked standalone card ranking.

## Explicit outcome and pending gate

The strongest reasoned **research** portfolio is D for a commander-first theft objective: five justified conditional improvements, 24 candidate dispositions, 12 challenged plausible incumbents and four complete hundred-card alternatives. None of those facts establishes multiplayer win rate or global optimum. Enemy flyers/Spirits, a removal-rich four-player table, and repeated Daring Piracy triggers could reverse some choices. There is no precommitted information gain for a large generic simulator, and running one that omits Forerunner or Poisoner would bias the result. The finite tests answer their rules questions; the unmeasured strategy is labeled honestly.
