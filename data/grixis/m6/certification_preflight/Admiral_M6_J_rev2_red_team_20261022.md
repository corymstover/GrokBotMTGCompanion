## Challenging the exact commander's game plan

1. **Theft requires three distinct Pirates damaging one player.** With two Pirates and a Kari Zev-created Ragavan **Monkey** token connecting, Admiral does not steal. The separate printed `Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer` has Monkey Pirate subtype and may qualify if it actually deals combat damage. We retained Kari for menace/first strike/token plus possible Bident/Indomitable draws, **not** because her attacking Monkey is a Pirate. Verified in source-bound Stage7 checks.
2. **A Wave attack is not uncounterable.** `Cosmotronic Wave` is a `{3}{R}` sorcery. It can be countered before resolution; after its resolution, creatures opponents control cannot block that turn, but opposing player may still remove one of exactly three attacking Pirates or prevent all combat damage. The verified finite blocker fixture only claims a result if spell resolves and combat damage occurs. No fictitious guarantee of theft.
3. **Admiral timing is crucial.** An opponent removing Admiral **before the beginning of our end step** prevents her triggered ability from triggering; a later removal after the ability has actually triggered does not automatically counter it. The target must still be a legal **nonland permanent controlled by the player who received three Pirate hits** at resolution. A player who took one separate Pirate hit in a different combat does not qualify by aggregating damage dealt to multiple players.
4. **A Pirate-heavy opponent resists old Palisade but not Wave's blocker ban.** Palisade choosing Pirate leaves enemy Pirate blockers, whereas Wave prevents all opposing creatures from blocking this turn even in a Pirate mirror. This *card-level* advantage belongs to **both** six-Wave alternatives, so it does NOT discriminate between Wave-for-Cannonade and Wave-for-Palisade.
5. **The hardest Wave-for-Palisade regret:** Opposing large-toughness, costly non-Pirate creatures are numerous and can be bounced for tempo in a five-mana turn. Palisade could return them all to hand and tax subsequent recasts, while Wave merely postpones their blocking for the current turn. If the environment is rich in large, slow replayable blockers and low in early opposing attackers, the alternative Wave-for-Cannonade can outperform the chosen all-purpose list. This remains a *qualified sensitivity*, not an unresolved missing rules fact.
6. **The opposite Wave-for-Cannonade regret:** An opponent swings with a crowd of 2-toughness non-Pirates while we have three untapped mana. Cannonade at `{2}{R}` instant can deal two to each non-Pirate during that combat, protecting our Pirate creatures subtype-wise. Neither Wave nor Palisade may be cast at ordinary opponent combat timing. Retaining Cannonade prevents collapsing all anti-swarm interaction into more sorceries.
7. **Kari vs Aerialist is a genuine adversarial reversal.** If two opposing ground creatures can team-block Kari under menace while skies are clear, Aerialist's flying Pirate is a better third source. If only one flying/reach defender and no relevant ground blocker exist, Kari with menace connects while Aerialist can be blocked. Kari's Monkey token may make extra generic-creature-hit draw triggers without counting toward Admiral. Neither extra seventh swap nor EDHREC's lift value proves a universal upgrade.
8. **Dreamcaller vs Open has a four-blocker fail case.** Flash Dreamcaller can tap two nonland permanents if another Pirate is present and be an evasive body in *later* combats; but that does not grant haste. Against four blockers its two taps do not ensure three Pirates connect, whereas Open at X3 `{3}{U}{U}`, if paid and resolved, makes all three selected Pirates unblockable and may draw three. Losing Open to reach eight/nine changes is a serious commander-first weakness.
9. **Regalia/Spotlight for Bident reduce engine endurance.** Brotherhood Regalia can ward/unblock one equipped legendary source; Glaring Spotlight can temporarily hexproof and unblock a team, then must be sacrificed. Cutting Bident for either sacrifices repeated draw from any creature that hits, even alongside The Indomitable. The chosen list keeps both draws and a dedicated Open burst, choosing smoother repeat theft attempts instead of one more isolated protection device.
10. **The historical Forerunner cut remains material.** Forerunner of the Coalition puts a Pirate card on TOP of library and drains each opponent whenever another Pirate enters. With Daring Piracy creating one temporary Pirate each turn, Forerunner can inflict repeat noncombat loss when all combat is blocked. The new candidate replaces it with Broadside's haste/menace and conditional boast; Broadside's boast damage is `2 + sacrificed permanent's mana value`, and sacrificing a Treasure (MV0) does just 2 damage. The cost of losing tutor/drain is explicit and preserved as potential metagame reason to reject the entire new M7 candidate, not silently treated as nonexistent.
11. **Departed Deckhand has a Spirit and targeted-spell Achilles' heel.** It is unblockable except by Spirits, but sacrifices when it becomes a *spell's target*. Wave's targetless can't-block effect does not target it; separately targeted interaction can still kill it. Cloud Pirates can only block flying creatures, and Skyship Plunderer needs a successful hit to add counters of an already existing kind. No universal third hit guaranteed by three printed evasive names alone.
12. **Mana and resource constraints can close an alpha line.** A four-land turn can pay Admiral `{1}{U}{B}{R}` OR Wave `{3}{R}` separately, not both with the same tapped sources. City of Brass takes one life, Path enters tapped, Open at X3 needs UU, Cannonade kept up for opponent turn means not freely spending those sources. The new exact J mana report ties these actual payable costs to source lands and an unchanged 35-land structure.
