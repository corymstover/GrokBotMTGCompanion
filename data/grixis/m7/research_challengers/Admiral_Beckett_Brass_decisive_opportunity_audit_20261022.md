## Eight-plus *real* incumbent challenges

These are not auto-cuts. Each could be defended and each challenger must win a meaningful role contest.

1. **Crumbling Necropolis** — always tapped, slow commander deployment; **City of Brass** enables immediate colored mana but accumulates nonpreventable-by-life-payment triggered *damage* when tapped.
2. **Daring Saboteur** — 2-mana Pirate with recurring 3-mana unblockability and optional loot; **Departed Deckhand** supplies passive near-unblockability against non-Spirits (a Spirit *Pirate*), but dies whenever any player targets it with a spell; **Slippery Scoundrel** is slower and conditional on ten permanents.
3. **Dire Fleet Captain** — two-mana Pirate that scales its own attacking body with other Pirates, but it does not evade; **Skyship Plunderer** is two-mana flying Pirate with opportunistic existing-counter addition, **Cloud Pirates** are a one-mana flying Pirate, **Neckbreaker/Vargus** buff other bodies but are unhelpful when they cannot connect.
4. **Merchant Raiders** — four-mana Pirate that continually taps and freezes opposing blockers on Pirate entry; **Broadside Bombardiers** is one mana cheaper, a Pirate with immediate menace/haste and a boast removal/burn line *after it attacks* at the actual sacrifice cost. Replacing Raiders loses repeatable board control, not merely a generic four-drop.
5. **The Indomitable** — four-mana non-Pirate Vehicle, recurring conditional self-recast and powerful repeatable combat draw; **Blustersquall** represents immediate all-blocker tap on one turn for four mana, with no repeat value. **Bedlam** represents persistent but symmetrical no-blocking; **Herald's Horn** is slower tribal smoothing/discount. This is the largest unproven tradeoff.
6. **Open into Wonder** — XUU gives multiple Pirates unblockability *and* draws on hits, but costs more mana with multiple targets and is sorcery speed. **War Cadence** costs an initial 2R plus XR to tax blocks each turn; **Blustersquall** is instant and hits all opponents' creatures, but offers no cards. Existing Open is likely defensible; do not cut lightly.
7. **March of the Drowned** — cheap two-Pirate graveyard recursion or any creature; **Patriarch's Bidding** is five-mana symmetrical mass tribal recursion. Bidding is stronger only in a board-wipe-heavy context without more rewarding opposing tribal graveyards.
8. **Zara, Renegade Recruiter** — five-mana flying Pirate adds a temporary opposing-hand creature on attack, which usually isn't Pirate unless another effect grants it the type; **Kindred Charge** can create a fleet for six mana but loses legendary copies to the legend rule. Preserve Zara's distinct theft/Coercive-Recruiter value unless a true earlier-connection gain is demonstrated.
9. **Admiral Brass, Unsinkable** — slower high-MV Pirate recursion/board effect; **Kindred Charge** is another expensive board payoff, not a demonstrated better version. High curve is a challenge, but the current card has intrinsic Pirate-recovery value that Charge does not.
10. **Warkite Marauder / Kitesail Freebooter** — low-cost *flying Pirates* respectively blank a blocker on attack and remove a noncreature spell temporarily; cutting either for Cloud Pirates or a generic finisher may reduce protection or effective evasion despite saving a mana.
11. **Smoldering Marsh / Path of Ancestry** — occasional tapland and guaranteed tapland; **Access Tunnel** gives a narrow, three-mana unblockable activation but sacrifices colored-pip reliability to a 4-MV UBR commander.
12. **Bident of Thassa / Kindred Discovery** — premier tribal combat draw; **Reconnaissance Mission** has a cycling fallback but does not obviously outperform either. Deck must not overinvest in non-Pirate draw at the cost of three connected sources.

### Discriminating scenario logic before any larger simulation

1. On the turn commander is cast for four, an untapped fourth-land City may produce the needed pip while Necropolis cannot; City triggers one self-damage per tap. A fixed four-mana hand lacking other exact UBR pips does *not* magically fix itself.
2. With Beckett, Departed Deckhand, Skyship Plunderer and Broadside Bombardiers attacking a player with one grounded non-Spirit blocker and no flying/menace blocker, at least the first two Pirates can deal damage; Broadside is *not guaranteed* a third hit if two suitable untapped blockers exist or removal lands. A paid pre-block Blustersquall overload can tap both to give three sources, provided all attackers are legal and no combat prevention/removal intervenes.
3. Broadside's boast can be activated after it is declared attacking, once that turn. It sacrifices *another* creature or artifact as a cost. A sacrificed Treasure's mana value is 0; only two damage is dealt. Don't combine spending that Treasure for mana with sacrificing it to the boast.
4. In B, four lands plus Admiral, two ready Pirates and a newly cast Slippery Scoundrel neither grant the city's blessing automatically nor allow the summoning-sick Scoundrel to attack. Herald's Horn cost reduction does not pay colored pips and cannot cure that tempo gap.
5. A table-wide Fog, instant kill of the third Pirate before damage, commander kill before beginning of end step, or removal of the stolen permanent before its targeted trigger resolves can each thwart the predicted theft. Removal of commander *after* its trigger is already on stack does not undo it.
