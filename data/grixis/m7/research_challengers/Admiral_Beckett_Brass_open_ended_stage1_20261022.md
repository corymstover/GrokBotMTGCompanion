## Why post-floor work is necessary

The former owner-ready analysis included 24 materially judged names and 12 real incumbent challenges, but selected exactly five swaps and all three compared alternative packages also contained exactly five. That is a plausible selection-budget anchor, not evidence that sixth or seventh changes cannot help. M6.6–M6.9 prior tests remain evidence within their exact physical/source scope; they do not certify this new option pool.

The actual commander win requirement is *combat damage by three distinct Pirates to the SAME opponent*, followed by Admiral's beginning-endstep permanent-theft trigger. A card that makes each attacker larger but does not help three attackers connect can be substantially worse than a less glamorous low-drop/evasion/protection source. The additional investigations below deliberately challenge existing nonselected slots without assuming larger count is better.

## Wider opportunity map — newly reopened leads

The following are research leads; **no new card is treated as fully source/candidate certified until the complete Scryfall Oracle/legality/identity record and relevant physical interactions have been bound.**

1. **Captain Storm, Cosmium Raider** (outside the former 24 examined candidates). Printed 2-mana Human Pirate, U/R color identity; each friendly artifact entry can put a +1/+1 counter on a Pirate. Numerous existing Treasure creators warrant a deckwide artifact-entry and color-cost count. The biggest unanswered decision is whether this proactive engine outperforms March of the Drowned's low-cost recovery, or whether a different weaker support/top-end slot is the better cut.
2. **Shizo, Death's Storehouse** (off-ranking mana/evasion opportunity). Real black source and activated fear on **legendary** attackers, not a universal Pirate-unblockable land. Compare every three- and four-land color source fixture: Shizo cannot itself fund its paid fear activation and be tapped for mana in the same turn. It may beat a tapped tribal land in early deployment, but is not a cosmetic untapped-land upgrade.
3. **Strionic Resonator**. Its activated copy of a controlled triggered ability is a possible **second** Admiral permanent-theft trigger after three Pirates connect; payment is \{2\}, tap, and the target trigger must already be on the stack. This strengthens the actual commander payoff, not generic damage, but cannot create hits itself and can be stranded before the condition is met. Compare actual alternative cuts to preserve draw/protection; note Roaming Throne already contributes some trigger redundancy. Requires a new precise stack/target/source proof before any proposed inclusion.
4. **Molten Echoes**. Selecting Pirate can copy newly entering nontoken Pirate creatures, with haste, to create additional distinct eligible attackers; may improve the *hit-count* prerequisite more directly than a high-mana anthem. It costs \{2\}\{R\}\{R\}, needs subsequent creatures, token exile at the next end step, and legendary copies remain subject to the legend rule unless another effect changes that. Compare against expensive recursion and current Double Down copy engine, not a cheap evasive Pirate by default.
5. **Amphin Mutineer**. Four-mana Salamander Pirate supplies exile-based creature interaction and has an encore option. The encore tokens attack **different opponents** if possible; they do not magically give three Pirate hits on one selected victim. Evaluate whether its actual creature removal plus Pirate type beats existing interaction without eroding the cheap-connection core.
6. **Brotherhood Regalia / Shizo / other evasion-protection slots.** Record current Oracle and paid equip/activation limitations before assuming protection or unblocked hits. Do not insert an artifact merely because it appears in a tiny EDHREC optimized slice.
7. **Captain Storm with Skyship Plunderer** is a named cross-card package rather than an isolated engine: an artifact entry first creates the relevant +1/+1 counter on a legal Pirate target; Skyship can increase an existing counter only after it connects and resolves its damage trigger. Examine whether this feedback actually changes three-source hit reliability or only postconnection damage.
