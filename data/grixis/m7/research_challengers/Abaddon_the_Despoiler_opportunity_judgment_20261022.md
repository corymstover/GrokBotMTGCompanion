## Commander-first objective and honest boundary

Abaddon costs `{2}{U}{B}{R}`, and grants cascade to spells cast **from hand, on your turn**, only when their **mana value** is no greater than the **aggregate life lost by opponents that turn**. A cast *from exile* feeds different parts of the deck but does **not** gain Abaddon's granted cascade. Three opponents each losing 3 creates X=9; losing 3 total creates only X=3. The preferred plan is to deal loss cheaply, cast several hand spells at a now-enabled cascade threshold, and convert nonhand casts into damage, Treasures and other sources of leverage. The compact Breach/LED/Brain Freeze win is retained, never made the sole reason to play these colors.

The approved deck has **100 cards, 94 distinct names, 34 lands**, basics Island/Mountain/Swamp ×3; Bracket **4 — Optimized** is the supported provisional ceiling. Role tags report 19 ramp, 11 draw, 6 tutors, 9 removal, 4 counters and 1 protection, with substantial overlap: a ritual is not a sustainable rock, a cascade spell is not necessarily removal that answers a resolved stax permanent, and a free counterspell is not always useful when hit by cascade. The mana base has seven fetches and six typed shocks/original duals, fast fixing, three battle lands, Ancient Tomb and one always-tapped tri-land. Retain 34 lands during this comparison; trading colored access for a speculative spell would not be a free cut.

There is no honest whole-deck win-rate delta. Prior large physical/sequenced tests omitted significant incumbent functions and relevant opposing moves, and source-paid Ignus/Assault hits were rare **in those restricted policies**. That neither proves a failed strategy nor implies a global optimum.

## Exact complete challenger configurations (all unapproved)

**A — ENGINE + RESOLVED-HATE ANSWER (provisional lead, medium confidence)**  
+1 `Grinning Ignus`, +1 `Chaos Warp` / -1 `Rakdos, Lord of Riots`, -1 `The Lost and the Damned`.  
Complete 100: `Abaddon_the_Despoiler_engine_answer.txt`.

- **Ignus for Rakdos:** Two existing interactions favor actual Abaddon gameplay. With front-face **Birgi** already controlled, cast Ignus normally for `{2}{R}`; Birgi's trigger supplies red after the cast, then at sorcery timing pay `{R}` and return Ignus as an activation cost, getting `{C}{C}{R}`. Recasting Ignus costs those three mana, and Birgi replaces the red activation cost for the next iteration: repeatable *paid creature casts* without net mana production. This creates an arbitrary finite declared storm count, useful only with an outlet such as **Brain Freeze**, or repeatable **Abaddon-granted cascades** on your turn if the same-turn opponent-loss threshold already reaches **three** and Abaddon survives. A cascade of an MV3 Ignus can only find *less than MV3* hits. With Nightscape Familiar the generic recast cost is smaller, but the loop still needs payable **red activation mana**; it is not an unconditional infinite mana engine. Without Birgi, each recast consumes new red. Both an opponent removing Birgi/Abaddon or a Rule of Law effect can strand this line. Rakdos's creature discounts and immediate body are lost. The deciding *strategic* advantage is Ignus's direct access to the commander's hand-cast cascade rule and the already-included Birgi rather than relying on combat; it is not a simulated win-rate claim.
- **Warp for Lost:** Abaddon is vulnerable once **Rule of Law**, **Rest in Peace**, a nonblue protection permanent, or an artifact lock resolves. The baseline stack counters and Otawara channel cannot permanently answer all such permanents; `{2}{R}` instant Warp can target a nonland permanent *after* resolution, including enchantments. It does not guarantee safe removal: the target's owner reveals a replacement permanent from the shuffled library, and rules/timing, spell tax or interaction can prevent success. It is valuable even without Abaddon and can gain cascade when cast *from hand on your turn at X≥3*; holding it for opponents' turns gives no Abaddon cascade. The precise cost is losing Lost's source-native 3/3 tokens from every nonhand spell and land-from-nonhand synergy; source identity is reduced but engine continuity and meaningful counterplay improve.

**B — COMBAT + RESOLVED-HATE ANSWER (strong alternative; lower floor when disrupted)**  
+1 `Aggravated Assault`, +1 `Chaos Warp` / -1 `Rakdos, Lord of Riots`, -1 `The Lost and the Damned`.  
Complete 100: `Abaddon_the_Despoiler_combat_answer.txt`.

- This preserves the same interaction upgrade as A and trades Ignus's directly cascade-driven loop for a payable Neheb/Assault repeat-combat line. With `Neheb, the Eternal` and Assault controlled, aggregate opponent life loss **≥5** before your first postcombat main produces at least 5R, which pays the `{3}{R}{R}` sorcery-speed activation; added combat/postcombat main phases let Neheb trigger again under its **current Oracle's 'each'** wording. At exactly 5R per trigger with no additional damage this is *not* arbitrarily increasing red mana and does not itself win; with `Loyal Subordinate` plus Abaddon each combat beginning can make all opponents lose 3, allowing a finite lethal line even without attackers. With creature removal/Drannith/Rule of Law and no payoff beyond combat, this becomes a three-mana enchantment plus five-mana activation with little immediate tempo. The strongest argument for B over A is that Abaddon can already be a trampling combat threat and Neheb is an existing ramp card; it requires no Birgi. The strongest argument against B is that Neheb must be present and survive each postcombat-main trigger and extra combats may be profitless through blockers or removed Loyal. **Assault remains a meaningful alternative, not rejected for an inconclusive old simulator.**

**C — TRIPLE-COMBO TRIAL (not selected; higher setup and cascade-quality cost)**  
+1 Ignus, +1 Assault, +1 Warp / -1 Rakdos, -1 Lost, -1 Inevitable Betrayal.  
Complete 100: `Abaddon_the_Despoiler_triple_trial.txt`.

- Both renewable engines now share mana/protection/commander slots, and the deck covers both combat and spell routes, but swapping away another zero-MV no-cost suspend cascade hit actively **reduces cascade payoff density**, while Ignus and Assault are low-floor setup draws without their independent enablers. Three upgrades simultaneously should not be mistaken for guaranteed increased performance. Prefer A until actual whole-deck game-state evidence reverses that choice; C documents the genuine package consideration required by the owner, not a forced third swap.

The three candidates are material **substitutions inside the physical 100**, not +2 or +3 additions to 100. All retain one Abaddon commander, 34 lands, the original three each basic Islands/Mountains/Swamps and no other repeated cards; the repo-specific inexpensive tests verify the exact deck-name multiset differences.

## Adversarial rebuttal to baseline inertia

An unchanged-list advocate can point to excellent tutor/fixing/protection density, 34 lands, multiple zero-MV cascade hits and the true 40K value engines; cutting Rakdos/Lost sacrifices pressure, discounts, and Spawn production. The stronger adversarial **case against unchanged baseline** is that an optimized pod can resolve a hard nonblue artifact/enchantment lock: the list's abundant stack counters are irrelevant afterward, and Otawara can only delay. It also leaves existing Birgi's remarkable hand-cast cascade interaction unexploited. This is why Warp + Ignus is preferred for further verification rather than defaulting to zero swaps because previous simulator arms were insensitive.

**What could reverse the decision:** first, an exact stack/rules issue that stops the paid Ignus/Birgi/Abaddon loop; second, fresh Scryfall legality or Oracle discrepancy; third, source-faithful side-by-side access/opponent-response fixtures showing Neheb/Assault reaches reliable closed games substantially more often *after charging the exact Rakdos and Lost opportunity costs*; fourth, evidence Lost's exile/fetch token production protects the winning plan against particular opponents more often than Warp meaningfully repairs hate. No simulator will be run without a predefined choice-reversal endpoint and a finite stopping rule.
