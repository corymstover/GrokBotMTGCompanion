**Commander-first target**
Wick rewards Rat entries by creating or growing one Snail, then converts that Snail's stored power into damage to each opponent plus the same amount of card draw. The optimized list therefore prioritizes: cheap/multiple Rat entries; effects that preserve or amplify Snail counters/power; free sacrifice outlets; type-changing effects that preserve Snail while adding Rat; compact commander-native loops; and protection sufficient to force through Wick's activated ability.

**Important rejected alternatives**
- **Panharmonicon:** doubles Wick's Rat-entry trigger but is four-mana, noncreature, and narrower than Roaming Throne, which is itself a Rat and has ward.
- **Three Tree City:** extremely high ceiling in a wide Rat board but colorless in setup turns; Wick's `{U}{B}{R}` activation makes colored source quality more important than tribal burst mana.
- **Swarmyard Massacre / The Meathook Massacre:** powerful sweepers/payoffs, but the interaction suite is already sufficient and neither beats the selected low-cost commander-specific engine slots.
- **Chthonian Nightmare:** synergistic recursion/sacrifice engine, but energy/mana-value restrictions do not displace the compact native Wick loops or selected tutors/outlets.
- **Rank Rat / Thornbite Staff:** reviewed for Rat-combo infrastructure but lost on setup cost, narrowness, or requiring more surrounding pieces than the selected engines.
- **Conspiracy:** choosing Rat causes affected creatures to lose other creature types. With Wick, this removes Snail from the token, unlike Arcane Adaptation, Leyline of Transformation, and Maskwood Nexus, which preserve Snail while adding Rat/every type. The latter three are therefore the correct combo engines.
- **Generic Grixis cEDH package:** Oracle/Consultation/Breach-style homogenization can raise raw generic combo density but makes Wick less necessary. It was rejected under the commander-first/natural-ceiling standard.

**Final red-team correction**
The first closure candidate still retained **Plague of Vermin**. The adversarial pass identified **Song of Totentanz** as a material improvement: Plague costs `{6}{B}` and lets every player buy Rats with life; Song scales from `{X}{R}`, creates only your Rats, and gives your current creatures haste. Because every Rat entry advances Wick's Snail, Song is materially faster and less symmetrical.

After that correction, the entire list, top-100 pool, near-misses, mana base, interaction, type-changing package, combo package, protection density, and commander-independent alternatives were challenged again. The next serious pass produced **ZERO NEW MATERIAL IMPROVEMENTS**.
